Student question: If I miss a Duì Yào question—or a question from any other topic—how many related questions can the adaptive exam ask, and what happens to the domain percentages?

Two different systems

What AcupunctureTests does and what happens on the real exam

OUR SITE

Simulation for study and practice

Linear simulation: presents the selected practice exam's 100 questions for 150 minutes, permits returning, flagging and reviewing, and displays results after submission.

Current adaptive: preserves the same 100 questions and the six instructional 28/24/18/15/10/5 domains. After each response, it seeks a higher difficulty after a correct answer or a lower difficulty after an incorrect answer.

NCBAHM 2026 blueprint: builds a new 100-item form from already-published questions. It preserves the four official 22/24/30/24 weights and adjusts difficulty from the response history. It may complete the selected exam with questions from other published exams to reach the exact balance.

Both adaptive modes lock the response after moving forward. AcupunctureTests does not create questions during the attempt, calculate an official psychometric ability estimate or produce the NCBAHM scaled score.

REAL EXAM

Official CAT administered by NCBAHM

The English Chinese Herbology exam contains 100 multiple-choice questions and lasts 2.5 hours. Each candidate receives an adaptive path: questions are selected according to the official content-outline percentages and, within each domain, to match estimated candidate ability.

A correct answer leads to a more difficult item and an incorrect answer to an easier item. After moving forward, the candidate cannot return or change the answer. Results use a scaled score; 70 is the passing score and does not mean 70% correct.

The official exam does not use AcupunctureTests' six internal domains, and NCBAHM does not publish the complete algorithm, item parameters or a maximum number of consecutive questions for one microtopic.

Answer to the Duì Yào example: on the real exam, missing an item can cause the next selected item to be easier, but the public documentation does not guarantee that it will also test Duì Yào. Duì Yào belongs to official Domain I, and the system must continue to honor that domain's overall weight. No public topic-specific follow-up count exists for one pair or any other microtopic.

Recommended adaptive sequence

One error is evidence to check, not proof of a weakness

1 · Detect

Record the error

Classify the parent domain, microtopic, objective and difficulty. Do not immediately assume that the whole domain is weak.

2 · Confirm

Schedule one related probe

Use a different question that tests the same objective without repeating the wording or answer pattern.

3 · Adjust

Move down or transfer

After another error, use a foundation item. After a correct answer, use an application or transfer item.

4 · Stop

Respect the cap

Stop after two consecutive correct answers or after the topic limit is reached. Mark the topic for later study instead of asking indefinitely.

Example · Domain 3

If the learner misses a Duì Yào question

This is a recommended design for a future version of our site, not an NCBAHM rule: internal Domain 3 would still contain no more than 18 scored questions. If the missed item concerns one pair, practice could use up to three additional questions related to that pair or objective—four total including the original—but all four would consume positions inside the 18-question Domain 3 quota.

  • First probe: same relationship, different clinical presentation.
  • Second probe if needed: contribution of each herb or a more foundational distinction.
  • Transfer probe: formula-versus-pair boundary, compatibility or a nearby contrast.
  • Stop after two consecutive correct answers or after reaching four total.

If all 18 Domain 3 positions have already been used, the exam cannot insert another scored Duì Yào question. It may offer an unscored remediation item after the exam.

Measurement boundary

Adaptive practice is not automatically a psychometric CAT

A fully calibrated computerized adaptive test also needs item-level psychometric parameters, an ability estimate based on the response history, exposure control and a tested stopping rule. Until those data are calibrated, the accurate description is content-balanced adaptive practice.

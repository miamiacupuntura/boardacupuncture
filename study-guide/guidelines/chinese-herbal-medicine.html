Board guideline

Chinese Herbal Medicine

Turn the current Board outline into a guided study route for herbs, Duì Yào pairs, formulas and clinical safety.

Continuous reading document. Read from top to bottom without changing pages. This guide contains the study order, key changes, priorities and final review.
1

Starting point

If you are starting from zero: four concepts

Materia medica
The study of each individual medicinal substance: what it is, what it does, when it is used and when it is not.
Duì Yào (对药)
A pair of herbs selected because together they reinforce, complement, balance or direct a strategy. It is not a complete formula.
Formula
An organized prescription of multiple herbs with a hierarchy and a strategy for a defined pattern.
Safety
Identity, processing, dosage, interactions, pregnancy, toxicity, contraindications and substitutions.

Appendix A · Individual herbs

First build a foundation that supports decisions

For every herb learn identity and medicinal part, category, nature, flavor, channels, actions, indications, dosage, preparation and safety. Add one contrast sentence: “I choose it instead of a similar herb when this clue appears.”

Why this matters: without that foundation, Duì Yào becomes pair memorization and formulas become lists without mechanism.

5

Appendix B · Duì Yào

What Duì Yào is and why it comes after herbs

Duì Yào literally means “paired medicinals.” It is the intermediate unit between an individual herb and a complete formula. The pair shows how two functions reinforce, complement, balance or redirect one another when combined.

It is not studied first because you need to know each individual contribution before understanding the relationship. Do not confuse a pair with a complete prescription: it is usually a functional core inside a larger formula.

Example: Má Huáng + Guì Zhī

Má HuángGuì ZhīCombined jobCase clue
Strongly opens the Exterior, induces sweating and diffuses Lung Qi.Warms the nutritive and muscle layers and supports outward release.Powerful release of excess Exterior Wind-Cold; a characteristic pair in Má Huáng Tāng.Chills, body aches, absence of sweating and possible wheezing; screen Má Huáng cardiovascular risk.
6

Duì Yào method

How to master each of the 51 pairs

  1. State the combined job in one sentence.
  2. Explain what the first herb contributes.
  3. Explain what the second contributes and how it differs.
  4. Identify a representative formula where the pair is functional.
  5. Add one clue that confirms the pair and one that rules it out.
  6. Finish with relevant dosage, preparation, interaction or contraindication.

Mastery test: if you only remember two names, you do not yet master the pair; you must explain the relationship.

Appendix C · Formulas

Move from the pair to formula architecture

A formula is not a sum of ingredients. It has a strategy and a Jun–Chen–Zuo–Shi hierarchy: chief, deputy, assistant and envoy. Study the pattern it treats, how each ingredient supports the strategy, which modification changes direction and which contraindication rules it out.

Useful question: which ingredient or pair explains the difference between this formula and another in the same family?

Appendix D · Safety

What not to study as an active treatment

Do not memorize the nine obsolete substances as usable therapeutic options: Xi Jiao, Hu Gu, Xiong Dan, Chuan Shan Jia, Guan Mu Tong, Guang Fang Ji, Ma Dou Ling, Zhu Sha and Ying Su Ke.

You should still recognize each name, the safety or protection issue and the appropriate substitution. Integrate processing, dosage, pregnancy, toxicity and interactions while studying herbs, pairs and formulas.

9

Exam application

Method for solving a question

  1. Identify the pattern and treatment principle before looking at names.
  2. Decide whether the question asks for an herb, pair, formula or safety decision.
  3. Compare temperature, moisture/dryness, location and excess/deficiency.
  4. Find the decisive clue separating the two closest options.
  5. Make a final check for dosage, preparation, interaction, pregnancy, toxicity or obsolete status.
10

Practice within AcupunctureTests

How to move through the modules without losing the thread

You do not need to open every module at once. Work with one category or case and move it through the same sequence: individual herb → comparison → Duì Yào → formula → safety. The first-column names are the modules you will find in AcupunctureTests.

ModuleWhen to use itWhat to doWhat you should produce
Single Herbs ComparisonsAfter reviewing the A–D map.Choose one category and read its comparisons in sequence; write the clue that separates each pair.Three contrast rules you can explain without looking.
Secondary Actions · Herb Families & Parts · Name TrapsOnce you recognize the herb’s primary action.Check secondary actions, identity, medicinal part and confusing names.A short list of errors not to repeat.
Duì Yào PairsAfter mastering the herbs that form the pair.Apply the six steps: combined job, each herb’s contribution, formula, positive clue, negative clue and safety.A complete explanation of the pair, not just two names.
Formula CompositionBefore attempting to compare many formulas.Identify pattern, strategy, Jun–Chen–Zuo–Shi hierarchy, relative dosage and preparation.An outline explaining why each ingredient is present.
Formula Comparisons · Formula Families · Most Important FormulasAfter understanding formula architecture.Compare neighboring formulas and locate the ingredient, pattern or contraindication that changes the answer.A decision rule between two similar formulas.
Safety Library · Herbal IncompatibilitiesDuring every block, not only at the end.Add one line to every note about dosage, processing, pregnancy, interaction, toxicity or obsolete status.One safety decision: use, modify, avoid, substitute or refer.
Continuous ReaderWhenever you enter a card group.Read forward within the same group and keep the chapter number so you do not lose your position.One group completed in order without jumping between pages.

Navigation rule: finish the group you are reading with Continuous Reader, note the chapter, and only then move to the next module in the sequence.

Complete example within the site

How I would study Má Huáng, Guì Zhī and Má Huáng Tāng

  1. Single Herbs Comparisons. First I would compare Má Huáng with Guì Zhī. I would write this rule: Má Huáng dominates in excess Exterior Wind-Cold with absence of sweating and possible wheezing; Guì Zhī is especially important for harmonizing the nutritive and defensive layers and in presentations with spontaneous sweating.
  2. Duì Yào Pairs. Next I would explain the pair: Má Huáng strongly opens the Exterior and diffuses Lung Qi; Guì Zhī warms, supports outward movement and strengthens the release of Wind-Cold. Together they form a more powerful core than either herb alone.
  3. Formula Composition. Then I would reconstruct Má Huáng Tāng: Má Huáng as chief; Guì Zhī as deputy; Xìng Rén as assistant that descends Lung Qi; Gān Cǎo as envoy that harmonizes and moderates.
  4. Formula Comparisons. I would compare it with Guì Zhī Tāng. The decisive clue would be excess with absence of sweating for Má Huáng Tāng versus Exterior deficiency, spontaneous sweating and aversion to wind for Guì Zhī Tāng.
  5. Safety Library. Before finishing I would review Má Huáng dosage, processing and suitability, especially cardiovascular history, hypertension, concurrent stimulants and other relevant contraindications.
  6. Final check. I would answer one case without looking at notes and justify aloud the pattern, choice, ingredient hierarchy and safety. If I cannot explain one of those four parts, I return only to the corresponding module.

Output from this session: one contrast rule between two herbs, one Duì Yào explanation, one formula map, one formula comparison and one safety decision.

12

Repeatable routine

A 45–60 minute AcupunctureTests session

  1. 10 minutes: review three herbs from the same category and state each distinguishing clue.
  2. 15 minutes: read one Single Herbs Comparisons group in order with Continuous Reader and write three contrast rules.
  3. 10 minutes: study one pair in Duì Yào Pairs and explain each component’s contribution.
  4. 15 minutes: reconstruct one formula in Formula Composition and compare it with its closest neighbor.
  5. 5 minutes: add one Safety Library or Herbal Incompatibilities check.
  6. 5 minutes: solve one case or explain the full sequence without consulting the cards.

The next day, do not restart everything: begin by retrieving the five outputs from the prior session from memory and use only the specific module needed to correct the weak point.

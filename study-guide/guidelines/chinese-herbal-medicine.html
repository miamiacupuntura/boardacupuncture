Board guideline

Chinese Herbal Medicine

Turn the current Board outline into a guided study route for herbs, Duì Yào pairs, formulas and clinical safety.

Continuous reading document. Read from top to bottom without changing pages. This guide contains the study order, key changes, priorities and final review.

3 · Read

Your completion goal

  • Distinguish similar options using the decisive clue in the case.
  • Explain each herb’s role inside a pair or formula.
  • Select a formula from the pattern and justify modifications.
  • Detect when a choice is unsafe, obsolete or requires substitution.
4

Individual herbs

Where to focus the study of each herb

  • Correct identity and medicinal part.
  • Category, nature, flavor and channels.
  • Actions, indications and the clue that distinguishes it from similar herbs.
  • Dosage, preparation, toxicity, pregnancy, interactions and contraindications.

Do not study names alone: the goal is to choose the correct herb in a case and explain why the alternatives do not fit.

5

Appendix B · Duì Yào

When to study the 51 pairs

Study Duì Yào after mastering individual herbs. Understand each pair by its shared strategy, division of labor, representative formula and safety clue.

  • First identify what each herb does independently.
  • Then explain what effect appears when they are combined.
  • Finally distinguish that pair from another with a similar indication.
6

Appendix C · Formulas

How to study formulas

  • Name, category, primary function and treated pattern.
  • Ingredients and Jun–Chen–Zuo–Shi hierarchy.
  • Modifications that change the strategy according to the patient.
  • Differences from similar formulas, contraindications and safety.

Less emphasis: accumulating translations or isolated lists. More emphasis: selecting, comparing, modifying and justifying.

7

Appendix D · Safety

What not to study as an active treatment

Do not memorize the nine obsolete substances as usable therapeutic options: Xi Jiao, Hu Gu, Xiong Dan, Chuan Shan Jia, Guan Mu Tong, Guang Fang Ji, Ma Dou Ling, Zhu Sha and Ying Su Ke.

You should still recognize each name, the safety or protection issue, and the appropriate substitution. Also integrate processing, dosage, pregnancy, toxicity and interactions while studying herbs and formulas; do not leave safety until the end.

8

Preparation plan

Recommended final sequence

  1. Consolidate individual herbs and their decisive differences.
  2. Study the 51 Duì Yào pairs from the role of each component.
  3. Advance to formula composition, comparison and modification.
  4. Solve cases combining selection, dosage, contraindications and interactions.
  5. Review obsolete substances and substitutions as a safety block.

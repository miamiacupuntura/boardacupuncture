COURSE AT A GLANCE

Classify the warning before memorizing the point

The Board skill is recognizing why a precaution exists, which contextual factor changes it, and where a short source entry must be checked against the complete point reference.

2–3 hours of structured study
10objectives
7risk families
10examinations
125questions

Use the S-A-F-E-R reasoning method

  1. S · ScreenIdentify patient, pregnancy, infection, bleeding, weakness and modality context.
  2. A · AnatomyName the structure at risk: pleura, organ, vessel, nerve, canal, eye or other tissue.
  3. F · Find the sourceCheck the complete point entry and current safety guidance; do not rely on a short quiz phrase.
  4. E · ExplainClassify anatomical risk, contextual caution, method restriction or historical note.
  5. R · Respond safelyChoose the appropriate action within training, supervision and scope.
!
CLINICAL SAFETY BOUNDARY

Recognition and reasoning—not needling technique

This chapter provides no insertion depths, angles or procedural instructions. Safe practice requires formal training, supervised skills, infection-control competence, current references, individual assessment and compliance with scope of practice. A point omitted from a table is not automatically safe.

Frequently tested through pneumothorax risk.

Build, organ enlargement and bladder status matter.

NeuralSpinal canal, brain and nerves

Name the vulnerable structure.

VascularMajor arteries and veins

Keep the warning attached to local anatomy.

PregnancyContextual precaution

Not the same category as anatomical injury.

ModalityNeedling, moxa and other methods

Do not generalize one method’s restriction.

HistoricalSource-specific

Verify before treating it as current universal guidance.

TEN FOCUSED OBJECTIVES

Study by region and risk pattern

Objective 1 General point cautions and contraindications

Board focus: screen the patient, classify the warning and recognize an adverse response before choosing a point-level answer.

Patient context

Weakness, hunger, fatigue, anxiety, bleeding tendency and pregnancy can change decisions.

Local condition

Infection, ulceration, tumor, recent trauma and vulnerable tissue require separate assessment.

Adverse response

Dizziness, nausea, pallor or cold sweating signal a safety event requiring a trained response.

SUGGESTED READING & SOURCE LIMITS

Verify the full point entry

  • Deadman, Al-Khafaji & Baker. A Manual of Acupuncture. Review “Needling,” “Depth of needling,” surface anatomy and individual point entries.
  • Clean Needle Technique Manual. Use the current required edition for infection control, adverse-event response and safe practice.
  • WHO. WHO Standard Acupuncture Point Locations in the Western Pacific Region. Use for standardized location terminology, not as a substitute for complete safety guidance.
  • Course source table. Treat the brief “Caution or Contraindication” chart as an exam-study source, identify historical wording and compare it with current complete references.

Course Overview
COURSE AT A GLANCE

Classify the warning before memorizing the point

The Board skill is recognizing why a precaution exists, which contextual factor changes it, and where a short source entry must be checked against the complete point reference.

3 hours of structured study
12objectives
7risk families
10examinations
125questions

Three-credit planning framework: twelve measurable objectives are allocated approximately 15 minutes each. Formal CE credit remains subject to Board approval, provider requirements and documented completion.

Use the S-A-F-E-R reasoning method

  1. S · ScreenIdentify patient, pregnancy, infection, bleeding, weakness and modality context.
  2. A · AnatomyName the structure at risk: pleura, organ, vessel, nerve, canal, eye or other tissue.
  3. F · Find the sourceCheck the complete point entry and current safety guidance; do not rely on a short quiz phrase.
  4. E · ExplainClassify anatomical risk, contextual caution, method restriction or historical note.
  5. R · Respond safelyChoose the appropriate action within training, supervision and scope.
!
CLINICAL SAFETY BOUNDARY

Recognition and reasoning—not needling technique

This chapter provides no insertion depths, angles or procedural instructions. Safe practice requires formal training, supervised skills, infection-control competence, current references, individual assessment and compliance with scope of practice. A point omitted from a table is not automatically safe.

TWELVE FOCUSED OBJECTIVES · 3-HOUR PLAN

Study by region, risk pattern and integrated decision-making

Objective 1 General point cautions and contraindications

Board focus: screen the patient, classify the warning and recognize an adverse response before choosing a point-level answer.

Patient context

Weakness, hunger, fatigue, anxiety, bleeding tendency and pregnancy can change decisions.

Local condition

Infection, ulceration, tumor, recent trauma and vulnerable tissue require separate assessment.

Adverse response

Dizziness, nausea, pallor or cold sweating signal a safety event requiring a trained response.

Source reading · Chinese Acupuncture and Moxibustion, pp. 350–352

The supplied pages separate pre-treatment screening, anatomical precautions and management of possible accidents. Use the text to recognize the category and warning signs; verify all actions against current training and emergency protocols.

Source topicRecognition cues summarized from the pagesCurrent study interpretation
FaintingNervous tension, hunger, fatigue, improper positioning or excessive stimulation; dizziness, nausea, pallor, cold sweating, weak pulse or loss of consciousness.Stop treatment, protect the patient and follow the current clinic emergency-response protocol. Escalate when recovery is not prompt or severe symptoms occur.
Stuck needleResistance to lifting, thrusting or rotation; associated in the source with tension, muscle spasm, excessive one-direction rotation or a change in patient position.Do not use force. Maintain patient safety and follow the current supervised protocol for assessment and resolution.
Bent needleDifficulty manipulating or withdrawing the needle with patient discomfort; associated with force, hard-tissue contact, sudden movement or mishandling of a stuck needle.Do not force manipulation or withdrawal. Stabilize the situation and apply the current trained protocol.
Broken needleNeedle breakage during manipulation or withdrawal; associated with poor equipment condition, force, muscle spasm, sudden movement or mishandling.Keep the patient still, prevent further migration and activate the current urgent clinical protocol. Removal beyond trained scope requires appropriate medical care.
HematomaLocalized swelling, distension or pain after withdrawal, associated with blood-vessel injury.Assess severity and follow the current protocol; significant or worsening swelling, pain or neurovascular findings require escalation.
After-effectPersistent local soreness or pain following forceful stimulation or unskilled manipulation.Document and reassess. Persistent, severe or neurologically associated symptoms require appropriate evaluation.

Historical-source boundary: these pages contain detailed legacy management maneuvers. This guide intentionally summarizes recognition and safety principles rather than reproducing procedural instructions.

Objective 10 Precautions for modalities

Board focus: distinguish patient precaution, local-area precaution and restriction for a particular modality.

AskReason
Patient condition?Screening may change whether or when treatment proceeds.
Tissue or region?Local pathology and anatomy require separate assessment.
Which modality?Keep a restriction attached to the specified method.
Current or historical?Interpret the statement within its source and date.

Related Moodle bank: 6 true/false questions.

Objective 11 Compare and prioritize risks across anatomical regions

Measurable outcome: given two points or body regions, the learner will classify each risk family, identify the structure or contextual factor involved and state which information must be verified before treatment.

Thorax vs. abdomen

Differentiate pleural/lung risk from peritoneal or visceral relationships.

Neural vs. vascular

Name the actual canal, nerve, artery or vein instead of relying on a general “dangerous point” label.

Anatomy vs. context

Separate direct tissue risk from pregnancy, patient condition, modality and historical-source limitations.

Assessment evidence: complete one comparison grid with at least four regions and justify each classification using the full point source.

Objective 12 Resolve and document an integrated safety case

Measurable outcome: given an unfamiliar safety scenario, the learner will apply all five SAFER steps, document a source-based rationale and identify an appropriate next action within scope.

  1. ScreenIdentify patient and local factors that change the decision.
  2. ClassifyName the risk family and structure or context.
  3. VerifyConsult the complete point entry and current safety reference.
  4. DocumentRecord the rationale, limits and safe next step or referral.

Assessment evidence: complete one written case using the SAFER worksheet and explain why the closest alternative action is less appropriate.

Herb comparison

Mori Folium (sang ye) vs. Mori Ramulus (sang zhi)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueSang YeSang Zhi
Shared clueBoth come from the mulberry tree and cool Wind-Heat, but the leaf and twig travel to different areas.
Fast distinctionLeaf; disperses Exterior Wind and focuses on the head and face.Twig; opens the collaterals, relieves pain and moves into the limbs and joints.

Comparative answer key

CompareSang YeSang Zhi
Botanical nameMori Folium.Mori Ramulus.
Plant partMulberry leaf.Mulberry twig.
CategoryCool, Acrid Herbs that Release the Exterior.Herbs that Expel Wind-Dampness.
Primary actionDispels Wind from the Exterior.Cools Heat, expels Wind, opens the collaterals and relieves pain.
Body-region clueHead and face.Limbs, joints and collaterals.
Decisive indicationExterior Wind-Heat affecting the upper body.Wind-Heat in the joints with pain or impaired movement.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth come from mulberry, so the plant part predicts the destination: the leaf disperses upward at the Exterior, while the twig reaches outward through the limbs and joints.
Memory cueSang Ye leaf reaches head and face; Sang Zhi twig reaches limbs and joints.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 57. Source Exam Keys CMID 9048, question 2, and CMID 9049, question 8.

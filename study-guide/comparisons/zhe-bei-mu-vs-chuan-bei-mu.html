Herb comparison

Fritillariae thunbergii Bulbus (zhe bei mu) vs. Fritillariae cirrhosae Bulbus (chuan bei mu)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueZhe Bei MuChuan Bei Mu
Shared cluesBoth cool and transform Phlegm-Heat, alleviate cough and disperse nodes.
Fast distinctionStronger, bitter-cold dispersal for thick yellow phlegm, toxicity, swellings and clumps.Milder, sweet-moistening action for dry or chronic cough with weak or Yin-deficient Lungs.

Comparative answer key

CompareZhe Bei Mu (Fritillariae thunbergii Bulbus)Chuan Bei Mu (Fritillariae cirrhosae Bulbus)
Shared categoryHerbs that Transform Phlegm and Stop Coughing · Herbs that Cool and Transform Phlegm-Heat.
Flavor/actionBitter, cold, descending and acrid, producing a stronger dispersing action.Bitter and sweet; the sweetness moistens the Lungs and gives a milder profile.
Cough patternMore frequent for externally contracted cough with thick, yellow, difficult-to-expectorate phlegm; appropriate for Lung-Heat cough.Better for dry cough or chronic cough from weak Lungs, including Lung Yin deficiency, Phlegm-Heat or Wind-Heat.

Phlegm-Fire clueUsed more for swellings from Phlegm-Fire and less for Phlegm-Heat cough in the source's formula example.Emphasizes chronic dry cough rather than the stronger clump-dispersing role.

Cost clueCheaper in the source comparison.More expensive in the source comparison.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe herbs share the same headline actions, but differ in strength, moisture, cough chronicity and preference for clumps or toxicity.
Common confusionZhe Bei Mu is the stronger bitter-cold disperser; Chuan Bei Mu is the milder Lung-moistening option.
How it may appearThick yellow difficult phlegm, toxicity or nodules favors Zhe Bei Mu. Dry chronic cough with Lung weakness or Yin deficiency favors Chuan Bei Mu.
Cold-Phlegm limitationThe source frames this comparison as ineffective for Cold Phlegm-Damp cough and explicitly attaches that warning to Chuan Bei Mu in question 3.
Source question formatsQuestions 1–4 are Matching.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 382. Source Exam Key CMID 8925.

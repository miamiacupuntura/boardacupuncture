Heart–Liver Blood Deficiency vs. Heart–Spleen Deficiency

Both disturb the Spirit; eye, tendon and menstrual signs point to Liver while digestion and bleeding point to Spleen.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueHeart–Liver Blood DeficiencyHeart–Spleen Deficiency
SharedBoth may cause palpitations, insomnia, poor memory, dizziness, pale tongue and a thin weak pulse.
Fast distinctionLiver nourishment fails: blurred vision, dry eyes, cramps, numbness and scanty menstruation.Spleen production/containment fails: poor appetite, loose stool, fatigue, bruising or chronic bleeding.

Clinical table

CompareHeart–Liver Blood DeficiencyHeart–Spleen Deficiency
Second-organ clueEyes, sinews and menstrual Blood.Digestion, Qi production and containment of Blood.
Formula associationBu Gan Tang or a Si Wu Tang family strategy by presentation.Gui Pi Tang.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersThe shared Heart signs are not decisive; the second organ identifies the pattern.
Memory cueLiver shows in eyes and tendons; Spleen shows in meals and leaks.
SafetyPersistent palpitations, anemia symptoms, heavy bleeding, black stool or unexplained bruising requires biomedical investigation.

Review prompts

  • Which has blurred vision and cramps?
  • Which has loose stool and bruising?
  • What signs do both share?

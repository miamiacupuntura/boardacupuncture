Formula comparison

Chai Hu Shu Gan San vs. Xiao Yao San

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueChai Hu Shu Gan SanXiao Yao San
Shared clueBoth course the Liver, regulate Qi and treat rib-side or abdominal discomfort, emotional constraint and menstrual irregularity.
Fast distinctionExcess Qi stagnation: distending pain and a wiry pulse dominate.Qi constraint with deficiency: fatigue, poor appetite, pale signs and menstrual Blood deficiency accompany the mood symptoms.

Comparative answer key

CompareChai Hu Shu Gan SanXiao Yao San
Root patternLiver-Qi stagnation, primarily excess.Liver-Qi constraint with Spleen-Qi and Blood deficiency.
IngredientsChen Pi, Zhi Ke, Chuan Xiong and Xiang Fu distinguish it; both share Chai Hu, Bai Shao and Zhi Gan Cao.Dang Gui, Bai Zhu, Fu Ling, Wei Jiang and Bo He distinguish it; both share Chai Hu, Bai Shao and Zhi Gan Cao.
Pain clueMore pronounced distending rib-side, epigastric or abdominal pain.Milder pain with fatigue, poor appetite or dizziness.
Therapeutic emphasisMoves Qi and relieves pain more strongly.Courses Liver while nourishing Blood and strengthening Spleen.
Exam shortcutStagnation needs movement.Constraint needs movement plus support.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe formulas share a Liver-Qi focus, but Xiao Yao San protects and rebuilds deficient Blood and Spleen Qi instead of simply moving harder.
Memory cueShu Gan moves pain; Xiao Yao moves and nourishes.
SafetyPersistent or severe abdominal pain, jaundice, fever or pregnancy-related pain requires medical evaluation.
SourceSource bank: FWB 4.2.1, Comparison Formulas to Harmonize; corroborated with individual formula profiles.

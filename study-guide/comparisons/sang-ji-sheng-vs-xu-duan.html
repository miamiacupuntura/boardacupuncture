Herb comparison

Taxilli Herba (sang ji sheng) vs. Dipsaci Radix (xu duan)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueSang Ji ShengXu Duan
Shared cluesBoth tonify the Liver and Kidneys, strengthen the sinews and bones, and stabilize pregnancy. They may be combined or substituted in related deficiency patterns.
Fast distinctionExpels Wind-Dampness; favors painful obstruction with Liver-Kidney deficiency.Unblocks the channels, moves Blood and excels at reconnecting injured sinews and bones.

Comparative answer key

CompareSang Ji ShengXu Duan
Botanical nameTaxilli Herba.Dipsaci Radix.
CategoryHerbs that Dispel Wind-Dampness.Herbs that Tonify the Yang.
Properties and channelsNeutral and bitter; Liver and Kidney.Slightly warm, bitter, sweet and acrid; Liver and Kidney.
Shared actionsTonify Liver and Kidneys, strengthen sinews and bones, and stabilize pregnancy.
Primary emphasisExpels Wind-Dampness and treats painful obstruction affecting the lower back, sinews and bones when Liver-Kidney deficiency coexists.Disseminates and unblocks the channels, invigorates Blood without disturbing it, and reconnects sinews and bones.
Additional cluesNourishes Blood; dry skin and hypertension appear in the source material.Trauma, sores and pain; different processing methods emphasize pain, Kidney entry or bleeding.
Formula cluesDu Huo Ji Sheng Tang; Shou Tai Wan.Shou Tai Wan.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe herbs overlap in Liver-Kidney, sinew, bone and pregnancy support. Wind-Damp painful obstruction points to Sang Ji Sheng; trauma and reconnection of sinews or bones point to Xu Duan.
Memory cueSang Ji Sheng dispels Wind-Dampness; Xu Duan reconnects what is injured.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), pp. 796–797. Source Exam Keys CMID 9052, questions 1–3 and 7–9, and CMID 9107, questions 1–6.

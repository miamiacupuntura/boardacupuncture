Anticoagulants & Bleeding Risk

Evaluate additive bleeding, INR instability and peri-procedural risk across the whole formula.

Core rule

RuleReconcile every anticoagulant, antiplatelet, NSAID, supplement and herb; one familiar ingredient cannot establish safety.

High-yield screens

Group / stepWhat to knowSafe decision
Blood-invigorating herbsDān Shēn, Hóng Huā, Táo Rén, Chuān Xiōng, Yù Jīn and É Zhú may add bleeding risk.Assess necessity, dose, combinations and monitoring before use.
Dāng Guī / Sān Qī“Tonic” or “stops bleeding” does not guarantee anticoagulant compatibility.Treat as a medication-level decision, not a label shortcut.
Gǒu Qǐ ZǐCase reports describe elevated INR with warfarin.Coordinate INR checks when starting, changing or stopping.
DOACs versus warfarinRoutine INR tracks warfarin but does not measure apixaban, rivaroxaban or dabigatran effect.Use the medication-specific monitoring plan with prescriber/pharmacist.
Hemostatic herbsA hemostatic herb does not safely reverse an anticoagulant.Never use as home treatment for drug-related bleeding.
ProceduresAcupuncture, surgery, dental work and injections have different bleeding consequences.Share the full formula and follow the procedural clinician’s stop/restart plan.

When to act immediately

EmergencyBlack stool, vomiting blood, severe headache, sudden weakness, uncontrolled bleeding, fainting or head injury while anticoagulated requires urgent evaluation.

Educational use: this library does not replace medical evaluation, poison control, local regulation or pharmacist consultation.

Formula name trap

Si Ni Tang vs. Si Ni San

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueSi Ni TangSi Ni San
Shared clueBoth may present with cold extremities, but the mechanism and urgency are fundamentally different.
Fast distinctionSevere internal Cold from Yang collapse: cold extends proximally, vitality is weak and the pulse is faint.Qi constraint: only the fingers and toes may be cold while the body is warm; the pulse is wiry.

Comparative answer key

CompareSi Ni TangSi Ni San
CategoryFormula that warms the Interior and rescues devastated Yang.Harmonizing formula that spreads constrained Yang/Qi.
MechanismYang is too weak to warm the body.Yang exists but is blocked from reaching the extremities.
Decisive signsProfuse clear diarrhea, absence of thirst, fatigue, curled posture and a minute or faint pulse.Cold hands/feet with irritability, abdominal pain or diarrhea and a wiry pulse.
Therapeutic directionWarm the Interior and restore Yang.Spread Liver Qi, harmonize Liver and Spleen, and release constraint.
Name trapTang: collapse and true Cold.San: constraint, not true Yang collapse.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersCold extremities describe an outcome, not a diagnosis. Ask whether warmth is absent because Yang has collapsed or merely cannot circulate because Qi is constrained.
Memory cueSi Ni Tang has no fire; Si Ni San has fire that cannot reach the fingers.
SafetySi Ni Tang contains Fu Zi; contemporary safe practice requires an appropriately processed ingredient, reliable sourcing and qualified professional supervision. Suspected shock or circulatory collapse is a medical emergency.
SourceSource bank: FWB 7.1 series and harmonizing comparisons; corroborated with the Classic Formulas profiles and formula practice tests.

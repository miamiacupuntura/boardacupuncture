Herb comparison

Allii macrostemi Bulbus (xie bai) vs. Allii tuberosi Semen (jiu cai zi)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueXie BaiJiu Cai Zi
Shared featureBoth are warm, acrid Allium medicinals that move Qi, but their targets differ.
Fast distinctionUnblocks Cold stagnation, transforms Cold-Phlegm in the chest and lubricates the orifices.Warms Kidney Yang and secures Essence for lower-burner leakage.

Comparative answer key

CompareXie BaiJiu Cai Zi
Medicinal and common namesAllii macrostemi Bulbus; macrostem onion or Chinese garlic.Allii tuberosi Semen; Chinese leek seed or allium seed.
CategoryHerbs that Regulate the Qi.Herbs that Tonify the Yang.
Nature, taste and channelsWarm, acrid and bitter; Lung, Large Intestine and Stomach.Warm, sweet and acrid; Kidney and Liver.
Primary actionMoves Qi, unblocks Cold stagnation, transforms chest Phlegm and lubricates the orifices.Enters the Blood, moves Qi, warms Kidney Yang and secures Essence.
Decisive indicationCold-Phlegm chest obstruction with pain radiating to the back and dyspnea.Kidney-Yang deficiency with impotence, urinary incontinence or spermatorrhea.
Formula clueGua Lou Xie Bai Bai Jiu Tang.Zan Yu Dan.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth come from Allium plants and move Qi, but chest Cold-Phlegm identifies Xie Bai while Kidney-Yang and Essence leakage identify Jiu Cai Zi.
Memory cueXie Bai opens the chest; Jiu Cai Zi secures the lower burner.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 541. Source Exam Key CMID 9100, questions 4–8.

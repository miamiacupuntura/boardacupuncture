Herb comparison

Jiang Huang vs. Yan Hu Suo

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueJiang HuangYan Hu Suo
Shared clueBoth are warm, acrid and bitter herbs that regulate and invigorate Blood.
Fast distinctionStronger at breaking Blood stasis and treats Wind-Damp painful obstruction, especially neck and shoulder involvement.Broad pain-relieving herb for menstrual, traumatic, abdominal and other pain when properly combined.

Comparative answer key

CompareJiang HuangYan Hu Suo
Medicinal nameCurcumae longae Rhizoma.Corydalis Rhizoma.
Shared categoryHerbs that Regulate the Blood · Herbs that Invigorate the Blood.
Shared propertiesWarm, acrid and bitter.
ChannelsSpleen, Stomach and Liver.Heart, Liver and Stomach.
Primary emphasisStronger at breaking Blood stasis and also expels Wind from painful obstruction.Important for many forms of Blood-stasis pain and may address varied pain patterns when combined appropriately.
Distinctive indicationsChest or rib pain, amenorrhea, abdominal pain, and Wind-Damp Bi with neck or shoulder pain and impaired upper-limb movement.Menstrual, traumatic, abdominal and other pain.
Preparation clueNo special preparation is keyed in this comparison.Vinegar-frying enhances its pain-relieving action.
Formula cluesJuan Bi Tang.Jin Ling Zi San and Shao Fu Zhu Yu Tang.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth move Blood and stop pain. Wind-Damp obstruction in the neck, shoulder or upper limb favors Jiang Huang; a broad “any pain” or vinegar-preparation clue favors Yan Hu Suo.
Memory cueJiang Huang breaks stasis and reaches Wind-Bi; Yan Hu Suo targets pain broadly.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 608. Source Exam Keys CMID 8998, question 1, and CMID 9117, question 5.

Lung Yin Deficiency vs. Lung Dryness

Separate chronic Yin depletion with empty Heat from a drier presentation without a strongly red tongue.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueLung Yin DeficiencyLung Dryness
SharedBoth cause dry cough, dry throat, hoarseness and scant sputum.
Fast distinctionChronic deficiency with empty Heat: night sweat, afternoon heat, blood-streaked sputum and red peeled tongue.Dryness predominates: dry cough and dry tongue without strong redness or empty-Heat signs.

Clinical table

CompareLung Yin DeficiencyLung Dryness
Thermal signEmpty Heat is present.Heat may be absent or mild.
Tongue / pulseRed, little or no coat; thin-rapid.Dry but not necessarily red; thin or floating-thin.
Formula associationYang Yin Qing Fei Tang.Bai He Gu Jin Tang in the supplied table.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersDryness is the shared symptom; night Heat and a peeled red tongue prove Yin deficiency.
Memory cueDryness dries; Yin deficiency dries and heats.
SafetyPersistent cough, fever, weight loss, low oxygen or blood in sputum requires medical assessment.

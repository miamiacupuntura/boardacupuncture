Pathology or symptom comparison

Upper Eyelid Droop Pattern Differentiation

Diagnostic orientation

Core ideaTiming is decisive: congenital, slowly fatigable or abrupt neurologic onset point to very different presentations and medical causes.
Exam focusCongenital versus fatigable versus sudden ptosis with diplopia

Comparative differentiation

Pattern or presentationDecisive cluesRepresentative formula/herbStrategy
Kidney-Yang deficiencyCongenital ptosis with inability to elevate the lid, pale tongue and deep-weak pulseYou Gui YinWarm Kidney Yang and assist Spleen Yang.
Spleen-Qi deficiencySlow ptosis better in morning/after rest and worse later or after exertion, with fatigue, poor appetite and possibly dysphagiaBu Zhong Yi Qi TangBoost Spleen Qi.
Liver-Blood deficiency with Wind affecting channelsAbrupt one-sided ptosis, often outward eye deviation and double vision, following pale/dizzy deficiency signsYang Xue Dang Gui Di Huang TangHistorical strategy: nourish Liver Blood and dispel Wind.

Study rule

How to distinguishPresent from birth is congenital; fluctuating and fatigable suggests Qi weakness; sudden unilateral ptosis with diplopia is neurologic until proven otherwise.
Memory cueBorn with it—congenital; tires through day—fatigable; sudden + double vision—emergency.
SafetySudden ptosis, unequal pupils, severe headache, double vision, weakness, speech change or eye-movement abnormality can indicate aneurysm or stroke and requires emergency evaluation. Fatigable ptosis with swallowing/breathing weakness is also urgent.

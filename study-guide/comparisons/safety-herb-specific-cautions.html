Herb-Specific Cautions

Study the 27 unusual cautions in the supplied handbook while separating historical pattern-fit notes from adverse effects, urgent symptoms and current contraindications.

Core rule

Two-pass methodFirst recognize the handbook wording for exam purposes. Then verify the exact species, part, processing, dose, patient risk, medications and current regulation before any clinical decision.

Cautions 1–14

MedicinalHandbook wordingResponsible study note
Ma HuangRaises blood pressure.Cardiovascular-active stimulant; screen hypertension, arrhythmia, stimulants and decongestants.
Xi XinCaution: renal problem.Confirm species, part and dose; renal risk and aristolochic-acid substitution or contamination matter.
Xiang RuVomiting if taken hot.Traditional administration caution; stop and assess persistent vomiting or dehydration.
Sheng MaBreathing difficulties / upper excess and lower deficiency.The pattern phrase is historical; breathing difficulty itself needs prompt medical assessment.
Jue Ming ZiDiarrhea and hypotension.Its laxative effect may worsen diarrhea, dehydration or low blood pressure.
Sheng DiPregnancy with Blood deficiency.Older pregnancy/pattern caution; pregnancy use requires qualified obstetric review.
Zi CaoMeasles.Do not treat measles from a chart; suspected measles needs medical and public-health guidance.
Jin Yin HuaQi-deficient sores, Yin ulcers.A traditional pattern-mismatch warning rather than a universal toxicology rule.
Tu Fu LingDo not take with tea.Retain as a traditional dietary note; it is not established here as a universal toxic interaction.
Qing HaoBlood deficiency postpartum.Pattern caution; postpartum fever, weakness or bleeding requires medical evaluation.
Mu TongOverdose leads to renal failure.Identity is critical: aristolochic-acid-containing sources are nephrotoxic and must not be used.
Mu GuaOveruse damages teeth/bone.Preserve as historical wording; do not convert it into a proven modern toxicity claim without evidence.
Ban XiaAll bleeding due to Yin deficiency.Pattern caution; only correctly processed material is used, and raw Ban Xia is toxic.
Bai QianCough from Kidney not grasping Qi.Pattern mismatch: it does not address the root deficiency of every chronic cough.

Cautions 15–27

MedicinalHandbook wordingResponsible study note
Bai Jie ZiNo topical use for sensitive skin.Topical mustard preparations may irritate or blister; avoid damaged or sensitive skin.
Jie GengHemoptysis.Coughing blood is a red flag requiring medical evaluation, not routine self-treatment.
Xing RenCaution: infants and diarrhea.Cyanogenic toxicity risk makes identity, processing and dose essential; infants are especially vulnerable.
Shan ZhaAcid regurgitation.Its acidity may worsen reflux or upper-GI irritation.
Chen PiDry cough from Qi and Yin deficiency, hot phlegm, dry-heat cough, blood spitting.Its drying, moving action may aggravate dry or Yin-deficient presentations; hemoptysis is a red flag.
Fu ZiFalse cold, true heat.Pattern mismatch plus serious aconite cardiotoxicity; requires correct processing and specialist control.
Ren ShenHypertension (systolic over 180 mm Hg).The cutoff is historical, not a universal dosing rule; systolic pressure at or above 180 may require urgent assessment.
Da ZaoExcess Damp, food stagnation, parasites, Damp-Phlegm disorders.A traditional cloying/sweet pattern-fit caution, not a stand-alone diagnosis.
Lu RongIncrease dose slowly.Historical dosing note; also review endocrine/bleeding risk and lawful, ethical animal sourcing.
Wu Wei ZiMay cause heartburn.Monitor reflux or gastric irritation and stop if symptoms worsen.
Yuan ZhiCaution: ulcers and gastritis.May irritate the gastrointestinal tract; avoid casual use with active ulceration.
Bing LangCaution: Middle-Jiao Qi collapse.Older pattern warning; areca also requires modern safety and legal review.
Da HuangEnters milk.Use during lactation needs professional review because infant exposure and diarrhea are concerns.

Source and modern boundary

SourceHandbook of Oriental Medicine, “Caution & Contraindication,” p. 373. Wording is preserved for study but does not replace current toxicology, regulation, obstetric care, poison control or pharmacist consultation.

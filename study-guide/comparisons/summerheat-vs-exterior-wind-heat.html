Pattern comparison

Summerheat vs. Exterior Wind-Heat

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueSummerheatExterior Wind-Heat
Shared clueBoth occur with fever, thirst, sweating, irritability and a rapid pulse during warm weather.
Fast distinctionSeasonal Heat is intense: high fever, profuse sweat, strong thirst, scant dark urine and fatigue; Dampness may add nausea or diarrhea.Exterior Wind-Heat is superficial: fever with mild chills, sore throat, cough, headache and a floating-rapid pulse.

Comparative answer key

CompareSummerheatExterior Wind-Heat
DepthStrong Heat directly injures Qi and fluids; may combine with Dampness.Exterior Wind-Heat at Lung/Wei level.
Decisive clueSummer exposure plus marked sweat/thirst and urinary fluid loss.Sore throat/cough with remaining chills and floating pulse.
Tongue / pulseRed with dry or greasy yellow coat; rapid, possibly soggy.Red tip/edges with thin coat; floating-rapid.
Formula examplesQing Luo Yin or Bai Hu Jia Ren Shen Tang by severity.Yin Qiao San or Sang Ju Yin.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersWarm season alone is not enough. Intense fluid injury and possible Dampness indicate Summerheat; respiratory Exterior signs indicate Wind-Heat.
Memory cueSummerheat scorches and drains; Wind-Heat stays near the surface and throat.
SafetyHeat stroke is an emergency: confusion, collapse, seizure, very high temperature or hot altered skin requires immediate cooling and emergency services.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

  • Which strongly injures fluids?
  • Which retains mild chills?
  • What signs show Summerheat with Dampness?

Herb comparison

Atractylodis Rhizoma (cang zhu) vs. Atractylodis macrocephalae Rhizoma (bai zhu)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueCang ZhuBai Zhu
Shared cluesBoth address Dampness and support the middle, but their relative strengths differ.
Decisive presentationSignificant or severe Dampness with less debility; stronger need to dry, ease the middle or induce sweating.Marked Spleen Qi debility or a chronic Damp-phlegm presentation requiring tonification while eliminating Dampness.

Comparative answer key

CompareAtractylodis Rhizoma (cang zhu)Atractylodis macrocephalae Rhizoma (bai zhu)
CategoryAromatic Herbs that Transform Dampness.Herbs that Tonify the Qi.
Relative strengthSuperior at easing the middle, drying Dampness and inducing sweating.Superior at tonifying the middle and Spleen Qi while eliminating Dampness.
Nature of presentationWarmer and more pungent; better for severe Damp-phlegm and significant Dampness with less debility.Better for chronic Damp-phlegm with Spleen Qi deficiency or marked debility.
Substitution clueMay replace Bai Zhu when Dampness is significant but debility is limited.May replace Cang Zhu when a formula calls for Cang Zhu but the patient has marked debility.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe exam distinction is based on the balance between excess Dampness and underlying deficiency, not merely on the presence of Dampness.
Common confusionBoth can strengthen the middle and eliminate Dampness. Cang Zhu emphasizes drying and dispersing; Bai Zhu emphasizes tonifying.
How it may appearSevere Dampness, a more pungent-warm action or sweating favors Cang Zhu. Chronicity, Spleen Qi deficiency and marked weakness favor Bai Zhu.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 469.

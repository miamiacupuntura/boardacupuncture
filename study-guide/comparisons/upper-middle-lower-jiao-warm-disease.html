Upper vs. Middle vs. Lower Jiao in Warm Disease

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueUpper JiaoMiddle JiaoLower Jiao
Shared clueAll describe the location and evolution of warm disease; progression downward generally reflects increasing depth and fluid/Yin injury.
Fast distinctionLung/defense: fever, mild chills, cough, thirst and sore throat.Spleen-Stomach: blazing Qi Heat with fever/sweat/thirst or Damp-Warm heaviness, fullness and greasy coat.Liver-Kidney: lingering night fever, dry mouth, weak low back, tremor or spasm from depleted Yin.

Comparative answer key

CompareUpper JiaoMiddle JiaoLower Jiao
Primary organsLung and Pericardium.Spleen and Stomach.Liver and Kidney.
Decisive clueExterior respiratory onset.Strong digestive Qi Heat or Damp obstruction.Late-stage Yin depletion and internal Wind.
Tongue / pulseRed tip; floating-rapid.Red/yellow or greasy coat; rapid/slippery.Deep red, little coat; thin-rapid or weak.
Formula examplesYin Qiao San or Sang Ju Yin.Bai Hu Tang or San Ren Tang by dry/Damp branch.Qing Hao Bie Jia Tang or Da Ding Feng Zhu by presentation.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersFever is shared. Respiratory exterior signs, digestive Heat/Dampness and late Yin-Wind signs identify the Jiao.
Memory cueUpper coughs; Middle burns or bogs; Lower dries and stirs Wind.
SafetySuspected serious infection, confusion, stiff neck, breathing difficulty, dehydration, non-blanching rash or seizure requires urgent medical evaluation.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

  • Which Jiao begins in the Lung?
  • Which includes Damp-Warm digestion?
  • Which reflects late Liver-Kidney Yin injury?

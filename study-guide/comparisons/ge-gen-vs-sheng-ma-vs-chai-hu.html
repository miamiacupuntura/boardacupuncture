Herb comparison

Puerariae Radix (ge gen) vs. Cimicifugae Rhizoma (sheng ma) vs. Bupleuri Radix (chai hu)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueGe GenSheng MaChai Hu
Shared cluesAll three are cool, acrid exterior-releasing herbs with Yang-raising actions.
Fast distinctionYang Brightness, stiff neck, fluids, thirst and diarrhea.Sinking Spleen/Stomach Yang, prolapse, rash plus toxicity.Lesser Yang, alternating chills and fever, and Liver Qi constraint.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe shared exterior-releasing and lifting actions require recognition of muscle/fluids, prolapse/toxicity, or Lesser Yang/Liver Qi clues.
Common confusionBoth Ge Gen and Sheng Ma vent rashes, but Sheng Ma adds toxicity resolution; Chai Hu instead supplies the Liver Qi and alternating-chills clue.
How it may appearStiff neck and thirst favors Ge Gen; prolapse or sinking Yang favors Sheng Ma; alternating chills and fever or Liver Qi constraint favors Chai Hu.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 81. Source Exam Key CMID 8917, questions 1–2, and CMID 8955, questions 7–9.

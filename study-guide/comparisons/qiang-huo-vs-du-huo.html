Herb comparison

Notopterygii Rhizoma seu Radix (qiang huo) vs. Angelicae pubescentis Radix (du huo)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueQiang HuoDu Huo
Shared cluesBoth dispel Wind-Dampness, disperse Cold, relieve painful obstruction and alleviate pain. Together they cover upper and lower levels.
Fast distinctionWarmer, drier, faster and more superficial; rises through the Qi level and Greater Yang to treat the upper body.More moderate, slower, deeper and downward-moving; enters the Blood level and Lesser Yin to treat the lower back and legs.

Comparative answer key

CompareQiang HuoDu Huo
Botanical nameNotopterygii Rhizoma seu Radix.Angelicae pubescentis Radix.
CategoryWarm, Acrid Herbs that Release the Exterior.Herbs that Dispel Wind-Dampness.
Direction and levelRises; acts more superficially at the Qi level.Moves downward; penetrates more deeply and readily enters the Blood level.
Channel tendencyGreater Yang (Taiyang).Lesser Yin (Shaoyin).
Body regionUpper back, neck, occiput and head; also shoulders and upper-body joints.Lumbar region, legs, calves and feet.
Wind patternRecently contracted or wandering Wind at the Exterior.Chronic lurking Wind that has penetrated more deeply.
Headache clueGreater-Yang headache, especially involving the occiput.Lesser-Yin headache involving the teeth and cheeks.
Classical appearance clueMore purple, forceful and harsh.More yellow and moderate.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe pair treats Wind-Cold-Damp painful obstruction throughout the body, but direction and depth decide the match: upper, superficial and recent favors Qiang Huo; lower, deeper and chronic favors Du Huo.
Memory cueQiang Huo rises to the upper Taiyang; Du Huo descends to the lower Shaoyin.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), pp. 21 and 325. Source Exam Key CMID 9032, questions 1–2 and 4–10.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

  • Which Wind-Dampness and pain-relieving actions do both herbs share?
  • Which herb rises to the superficial upper body and Greater Yang?
  • Which herb descends to the deeper lower body and Lesser Yin?
  • Which regions and headache patterns identify each herb?
  • How do wandering and lurking Wind distinguish the pair?

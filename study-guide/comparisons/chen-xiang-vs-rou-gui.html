Herb comparison

Aquilariae Lignum resinatum (chen xiang) vs. Cinnamomi Cortex (rou gui)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueChen XiangRou Gui
Shared cluesBoth are acrid, enter the Kidney and Spleen, and treat lower-abdominal pain from Qi/Blood obstruction caused by deficiency Cold.
Decisive mechanismRelieves the presentation primarily by regulating Qi.Relieves the presentation primarily by warming Yang.

Comparative answer key

CompareChen Xiang (Aquilariae Lignum resinatum)Rou Gui (Cinnamomi Cortex)
CategoryHerbs that Regulate the Qi.Herbs that Warm the Interior and Expel Cold.
Properties/channelsWarm, acrid, bitter and aromatic; Stomach, Kidney and Spleen.Hot, acrid and sweet; Heart, Liver, Kidney and Spleen.
Primary emphasisRegulates Qi and directs rebellious Qi downward.Warms Yang and addresses deficiency Cold.
Shared indicationLower-abdominal pain with obstruction of Qi and Blood due to Cold from deficiency.
Formula cluesSi Mo Tang and Su He Xiang Wan for Cold/Qi stagnation with abdominal symptoms; Si Mo Tang for wheezing from excess or deficiency.Hei Xi Dan for Yang collapse or floating deficiency Yang; You Gui Wan for Kidney Yang deficiency; Zhen Ren Yang Zang Tang for Spleen/Kidney Yang deficiency; Nuan Gan Jian for Blood Cold; Shi Quan Da Bu Tang to enhance tonic formulas.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe same lower-abdominal Cold-deficiency presentation may point to either herb, but the tested mechanism differs.
Common confusionChoose Chen Xiang when Qi regulation is foregrounded; choose Rou Gui when Yang warming is foregrounded.
How it may appearRebellious Qi, abdominal distention or wheezing favors Chen Xiang. Yang collapse, Kidney fire or Spleen/Kidney Yang deficiency favors Rou Gui.

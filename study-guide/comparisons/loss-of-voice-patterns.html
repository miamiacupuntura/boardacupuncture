Pathology or symptom comparison

Loss of Voice Pattern Differentiation

Diagnostic orientation

Core ideaAcute hoarseness is separated by Heat, Cold or emotional constraint; chronic hoarseness by dryness, weak voice/fatigue or structural obstruction.
Exam focusAcute exterior temperature versus chronic dryness, fatigue or structural nodules

Comparative differentiation

Pattern or presentationDecisive cluesRepresentative formula/herbStrategy
Acute Wind-HeatSudden hoarseness with sore throat, dry nose, fever, thirst, cough and yellow phlegmShu Feng Qing Re TangDispel Wind-Heat and open voice.
Acute Wind-ColdSudden hoarseness with chills, clear runny nose, itchy throat, thin phlegm and no thirstLiu Wei TangDispel Wind-Cold and diffuse Lung.
Acute Liver-Qi stagnationSudden hoarseness/aphonia with irritability, sighing and chest-hypochondriac distentionBan Xia Hou Po TangSoothe Liver, regulate Qi and open voice.
Chronic Lung-Kidney Yin deficiencyProgressive hoarseness worse after prolonged speaking, dry throat/cough, weak back, tidal fever and night sweatBai He Gu Jin TangNourish Lung-Kidney Yin.
Chronic Spleen-Lung Qi deficiencyProlonged faint voice worse with work/mornings, effortful speech, dyspnea, fatigue, poor appetite and loose stoolBu Zhong Yi Qi TangSupport Spleen-Lung Qi.
Qi-Blood stagnation with congealed PhlegmProlonged effortful hoarseness, foreign-body sensation, chest oppression/phlegm; vocal-cord nodules or polypsHui Yan Zhu Yu TangMove Qi-Blood and transform Phlegm.

Study rule

How to distinguishYellow/thirst is Heat; clear/no thirst Cold; emotional sudden loss Qi; chronic dry/empty Heat Yin; weak tired voice Qi; foreign-body/nodule obstruction stasis-Phlegm.
Memory cueYellow—Heat; clear—Cold; emotion—Qi; dry—Yin; faint-tired—Qi deficiency; nodule—stasis/Phlegm.
SafetyStridor, breathing/swallowing difficulty or neck swelling is urgent. Hoarseness lasting over 3 weeks, coughing blood, neck mass, weight loss, smoking history or recurrent voice loss needs ENT/laryngoscopic evaluation.

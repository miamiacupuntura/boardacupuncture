Herb–Drug Interactions

Screen by pharmacologic effect, medication class and monitoring plan.

Core rule

RuleDo not memorize isolated pairs only—look for additive effects across the entire formula and medication list.

High-yield interaction screens

Group / stepWhat to knowSafe decision
Anticoagulants / antiplateletsBlood-moving or hemostatic herbs such as Dān Shēn, Dāng Guī, Sān Qī, Hóng Huā and Táo Rén may alter bleeding risk; Gǒu Qǐ Zǐ has warfarin/INR reports.Document indication, monitor bruising/bleeding and coordinate INR or medication management.
Digoxin / antiarrhythmicsXiāng Jiā Pí has cardiac glycosides; Gān Cǎo-related hypokalemia can increase arrhythmia/digoxin risk.Avoid unsupervised combinations; monitor pulse, potassium and drug levels when clinically indicated.
Blood-pressure drugsMá Huáng and bitter-orange synephrine may raise heart rate/BP; Gān Cǎo can raise BP and retain sodium; several calming/draining herbs may lower BP.Check baseline BP, stimulant intake and orthostatic symptoms.
Diabetes therapyRén Shēn/Panax and other tonics may influence glucose; formulas also may contain sugars or honey-prepared products.Increase glucose monitoring when starting, stopping or changing a formula.
Diuretics / electrolytesWater-draining herbs plus pharmaceutical diuretics can increase dehydration/electrolyte effects; prolonged high Gān Cǎo can lower potassium.Monitor weight, renal function and electrolytes in higher-risk patients.
Sedatives / alcoholSpirit-calming herbs may add to drowsiness from hypnotics, benzodiazepines, opioids, antihistamines or alcohol.Avoid driving and assess falls/respiratory risk.
Immunosuppressive therapyImmune-modulating tonics may conflict with transplant, biologic or oncology plans.Coordinate with the prescribing specialist; do not infer “immune boosting” is always desirable.

A usable workflow

Group / stepWhat to knowSafe decision
1. ReconcileRecord every prescription, OTC drug, supplement, tea and concentrated extract.Ask specifically about anticoagulants, cardiac, glucose, seizure and transplant drugs.
2. IdentifyUse pinyin + characters + Latin binomial + medicinal part + processing.Common names alone are insufficient.
3. Compare effectsBleeding, BP, glucose, sedation, rhythm, renal/hepatic burden and electrolytes.Evaluate the whole formula, not one headline herb.
4. Plan monitoringDefine what will be checked, by whom and when.No monitoring plan means the risk decision is incomplete.
5. Communicate changesStarting, stopping and dose changes can all alter drug effect.Coordinate with the medication prescriber; never advise abrupt drug discontinuation.

When to act immediately

EmergencyUnexpected bleeding, black stool, fainting, severe weakness, hypoglycemia, chest pain, palpitations or trouble breathing requires urgent medical evaluation.

Educational use: this library does not replace medical evaluation, poison control, local regulation or pharmacist consultation.

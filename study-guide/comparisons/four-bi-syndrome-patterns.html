Four Bi-Syndrome Patterns

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueWind Bi (Xing Bi)Cold Bi (Tong Bi)Damp Bi (Zhuo Bi)Heat Bi (Re Bi)
Shared clueAll obstruct channels and joints, causing pain, stiffness or limited movement.
Fast distinctionPain wanders from joint to joint.Severe fixed pain worsened by cold and improved by warmth.Fixed heavy pain with swelling and numbness.Red, hot, swollen and acutely painful joints.

Comparative answer key

CompareWind Bi (Xing Bi)Cold Bi (Tong Bi)Damp Bi (Zhuo Bi)Heat Bi (Re Bi)
Dominant qualityMovement and variability.Contraction and severe pain.Heaviness and lingering swelling.Inflammation and burning Heat.
Weather clueOften changes rapidly with exposure.Clearly worse in cold weather.Worse in damp weather.Worse with Heat; warmth may aggravate.
Tongue / pulseThin coat; floating/wiry.White coat; tight/slow.Greasy coat; soggy/slippery.Red with yellow coat; rapid/slippery.
Formula examplesFang Feng Tang.Wu Tou Tang—expert use due to aconite.Yi Yi Ren Tang.Bai Hu Jia Gui Zhi Tang or Xuan Bi Tang.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersPain location alone is weak evidence. Wandering, severity/cold response, heaviness/numbness or red Heat identifies the dominant pathogen.
Memory cueWind moves; Cold hurts; Damp weighs; Heat reddens.
SafetyA single acutely red-hot joint with fever, inability to bear weight, major trauma, calf swelling or neurologic weakness requires urgent evaluation. Aconite formulas are not for self-treatment.

Batch 7 · Small Intestine · Tip 5 of 10

Small Intestine Worm Infestation vs. Food Retention

Use exposure, recurrent pain and systemic clues versus overeating and sour-foul digestive signs.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueSmall Intestine Worm InfestationFood Retention
SharedBoth may cause abdominal pain/distention, nausea, appetite change and an unpleasant taste.
Fast distinctionPossible parasites: recurrent or migrating pain, sallow complexion, weight/nutritional change, exposure risk or worms/eggs in stool.Food retention: recent overeating, sour regurgitation, foul breath and thick greasy coating.

Clinical table

CompareSmall Intestine Worm InfestationFood Retention
Diagnostic priorityConfirm with exposure history and appropriate stool/medical testing.Dietary history and acute digestive accumulation.
Formula associationLi Zhong An Hui Tang, Lian Mei An Hui Tang, Hua Chong Wan or Qu Tiao Tang in the supplied table.Bao He Wan or Zhi Shi Dao Zhi Wan.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersSymptoms alone cannot confirm parasites; exposure and testing prevent inappropriate treatment.
Memory cueParasites persist or migrate; retained food follows the meal.
SafetyDo not self-treat suspected parasites with toxic herbs. Blood, fever, dehydration, severe pain or weight loss needs medical testing.

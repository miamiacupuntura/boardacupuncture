Formula comparison

Er Chen Tang vs. Wen Dan Tang

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueEr Chen TangWen Dan Tang
Shared clueBoth transform Phlegm, harmonize the Stomach and can address nausea, vomiting, epigastric fullness, dizziness and palpitations.
Fast distinctionDamp-Phlegm: cough with copious thin white sputum and a greasy white coat.Phlegm-Heat: insomnia, irritability or fright, bitter taste and a greasy yellow coat.

Comparative answer key

CompareEr Chen TangWen Dan Tang
Primary emphasisDries Dampness and regulates Qi to transform Phlegm.Clears Phlegm-Heat, regulates Qi and harmonizes the Gallbladder and Stomach.
IngredientsBan Xia, Chen Pi, Fu Ling, Sheng Jiang and Gan Cao are shared; Wu Mei distinguishes Er Chen Tang.Ban Xia, Chen Pi, Fu Ling, Sheng Jiang and Gan Cao are shared; Zhi Shi, Zhu Ru and Da Zao distinguish Wen Dan Tang.
Target patternDamp-Phlegm centered in Spleen and Lung.Phlegm-Heat disturbing Gallbladder, Stomach and Spirit.
Decisive symptomsCough with profuse white sputum.Insomnia, easy fright, restlessness or bitter taste.
TonguePale or normal with greasy white coating.Red with greasy yellow coating.
PulseSlippery.Slippery and rapid or wiry.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersWen Dan Tang is built on an Er Chen Tang-like Phlegm-transforming core but redirects it toward Heat and Gallbladder/Stomach disharmony.
Memory cueEr Chen dries white Phlegm; Wen Dan clears yellow Phlegm that disturbs sleep.
SafetyDespite the English name “Warm the Gallbladder,” the commonly tested modern pattern is Phlegm-Heat; do not select it merely because a case contains Cold.
SourceSource bank: FWB 6.2 and FWB 6.2.1, Comparison Formulas to Transform Phlegm; corroborated with the individual formula profiles.

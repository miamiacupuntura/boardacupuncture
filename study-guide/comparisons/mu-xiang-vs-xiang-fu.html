Herb comparison

Aucklandiae Radix (mu xiang) vs. Cyperi Rhizoma (xiang fu)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueMu XiangXiang Fu
Shared cluesBoth are bitter and acrid Qi-regulating herbs that enter the Triple Burner, move Qi and stop pain.
Fast distinctionMoves clumped Qi in the Stomach and Intestines and reduces Food Stagnation.Spreads Liver and Gallbladder Qi, releases emotional constraint and regulates menstruation.

Comparative answer key

CompareMu Xiang (Aucklandiae Radix)Xiang Fu (Cyperi Rhizoma)
Shared categoryHerbs that Regulate the Qi.
Shared properties and actionBitter and acrid; enter the Triple Burner, move Qi and stop pain.
Nature and channelsWarm; Gallbladder, Large Intestine, Spleen and Stomach.Neutral and slightly sweet; Liver.
Primary focusClumped Qi in the Stomach and Intestines; also reduces Food Stagnation.Liver, Gallbladder and Triple-Burner Qi stagnation; releases constraint and regulates menstruation.
Clinical cluesEpigastric or abdominal distention and fullness, nausea, diarrhea, dysentery, tenesmus and pain.Emotional constraint, irritability, epigastric or flank distention and pain, and painful or irregular menstruation.
Pain clue in the sourceStops pain associated with Stagnation, Cold, Heat, Excess or Deficiency.Described as a commonly used analgesic for abdominal pain, especially upper abdominal pain.
Dose and preparation1.5–4.5 g; add near the end of the decoction.4.5–9 g.
Formula cluesJuan Bi Tang; Xiang Sha Liu Jun Zi Tang; the source also cites Hou Po Wen Zhong Tang, Mu Xiang Bing Lang Wan and Gui Pi Tang.Yue Ju Wan; Liang Fu Wan; Chai Hu Shu Gan San.
Additional source clueMoves Qi in all three Jiaos; its slight astringency is associated with treating tenesmus.Especially suited to emotional stagnation and menstrual problems, particularly with scanty bleeding.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth move Qi and stop pain, so the decisive distinction is gastrointestinal Food Stagnation for Mu Xiang versus Liver constraint, emotions and menstruation for Xiang Fu.
Memory cueMu Xiang moves intestinal Qi; Xiang Fu frees constrained Liver Qi.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 527. Source Exam Keys CMID 9020 and CMID 9096, questions 1–10.

Spleen Qi Deficiency vs. Dampness Obstructing the Spleen

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueSpleen Qi DeficiencyDampness Obstructing the Spleen
Shared clueBoth cause poor appetite, loose stool, abdominal fullness, fatigue and impaired fluid transformation, and they often coexist.
Fast distinctionDeficiency dominates: weakness, pale complexion, soft abdomen and teeth-marked tongue.Pathogen dominates: heaviness, marked fullness, nausea and thick greasy coating.

Comparative answer key

CompareSpleen Qi DeficiencyDampness Obstructing the Spleen
NatureDeficiency of function.Excess obstruction by Dampness.
Body sensationWeak and tired.Heavy, foggy and encumbered.
Tongue / pulsePale, teeth-marked; weak.Swollen with thick greasy coat; slippery.
Formula examplesSi Jun Zi Tang; Shen Ling Bai Zhu San.Ping Wei San; Huo Xiang Zheng Qi San by context.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersSpleen deficiency can generate Dampness, but the exam asks which side currently dominates: weak root or obstructing branch.
Memory cueDeficiency is weak; Dampness is heavy and greasy.
SafetyPersistent diarrhea, vomiting, weight loss or severe edema requires medical assessment.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

  • Which has a thick greasy coat?
  • Which has a weak pulse?
  • How can the two patterns coexist?

Herb comparison

Psoraleae Fructus (bu gu zhi) vs. Alpiniae oxyphyllae Fructus (yi zhi ren)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueBu Gu ZhiYi Zhi Ren
Shared cluesBoth warm and tonify the Spleen and Kidneys, strengthen Yang, stop diarrhea, and secure Essence and urine.
Fast distinctionPrimarily warms Kidney Yang and secondarily warms the Spleen; favors chronic or cock-crow diarrhea and low-back pain.More strongly warms Spleen Yang and secondarily warms the Kidneys; favors diarrhea after Cold affects the Spleen and Stomach.

Comparative answer key

CompareBu Gu ZhiYi Zhi Ren
Medicinal namePsoraleae Fructus.Alpiniae oxyphyllae Fructus.
Shared categoryHerbs that Tonify the Yang.
Part, properties and channelsDried ripe seed; very warm, bitter and acrid; Kidney and Spleen.Dried ripe fruit; warm and acrid; Kidney and Spleen.
Primary emphasisTonifies Kidney Yang first and warms Spleen Yang indirectly.Warms Spleen Yang more strongly and warms the Kidneys secondarily.
Diarrhea clueChronic or cock-crow diarrhea from Spleen-Kidney Yang deficiency; Si Shen Wan.Diarrhea associated with Cold exposure affecting Spleen and Stomach; also stops diarrhea through Spleen warming.
Kidney and lower-burner cluesImpotence, premature ejaculation, low-back pain, incontinence and enuresis; also Lung-Kidney failure to grasp Qi.Incontinence, urinary dribbling and spermatorrhea; Suo Quan Wan. The source also identifies uterine bleeding.
Additional clueTopical use for alopecia.Warms the Spleen and retains Essence and urine.
Precautions in sourceYin-deficiency Fire or constipation.Seminal emission, frequent urination or uterine bleeding when caused by Heat.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth warm Spleen-Kidney Yang and control diarrhea and leakage. Kidney-first, chronic dawn diarrhea or low-back pain favors Bu Gu Zhi; Spleen-first diarrhea after Cold exposure favors Yi Zhi Ren.
Memory cueBu Gu Zhi begins with the Kidney; Yi Zhi Ren begins with the Spleen.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 788. Source Exam Key CMID 9111, questions 1–3.

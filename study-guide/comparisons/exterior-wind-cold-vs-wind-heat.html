Pattern comparison

Exterior Wind-Cold vs. Exterior Wind-Heat

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueExterior Wind-ColdExterior Wind-Heat
Shared clueBoth begin acutely with fever, aversion to wind, headache, body aches and a floating pulse.
Fast distinctionMild fever with severe chills, no thirst, headache and body/neck pain; clear discharge, white coat and floating-tight pulse.More severe fever with mild chills, thirst and sore throat; may vent rashes or affect the eyes.

Comparative answer key

CompareExterior Wind-ColdExterior Wind-Heat
Typical herb natureMostly acrid and warm.Mostly acrid and cool.
Temperature balanceAversion to cold stronger than fever.Fever stronger than chills.
Layer / organSuperficial Exterior and Lung.Superficial Exterior and Lung with Heat signs.
Additional clueClear nasal discharge and body/neck pain.Rash needing venting or red, painful Wind-Heat eyes.
Tongue / pulseWhite coat; floating-tight.Red tip/edges; floating-rapid.
Formula examplesMa Huang Tang or Gui Zhi Tang by sweat pattern.Yin Qiao San or Sang Ju Yin by throat/cough emphasis.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth are Exterior patterns. Relative fever/chills, thirst, throat and secretion color determine Cold versus Heat.
Memory cueCold is clear with chills; Heat is yellow with throat pain.
SafetyDo not overuse sweat-promoting medicinals: excessive dispersion can consume Qi and Yin. Use extra caution with bleeding or pregnancy. Breathing difficulty, high persistent fever, confusion, stiff neck or dehydration requires medical evaluation.

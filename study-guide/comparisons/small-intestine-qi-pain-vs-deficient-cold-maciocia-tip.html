Small Intestine Qi Pain vs. Deficient Cold

Use pain behavior, chronicity and thermal response to distinguish stagnation from deficiency.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueSmall Intestine Qi PainSmall Intestine Deficient and Cold
SharedBoth may produce lower abdominal pain and borborygmi.
Fast distinctionQi stagnation: twisting, distending or moving pain with a wiry pulse.Deficient Cold: chronic dull pain relieved by warmth and pressure, diarrhea, fatigue and cold limbs.

Clinical table

CompareSmall Intestine Qi PainSmall Intestine Deficient and Cold
NatureStagnant Qi; relatively excess.Yang/Qi weakness; deficient and cold.
PulseDeep-wiry.Deep-slow-weak.
Formula associationChai Hu Shu Gan Tang.Xiao Jian Zhong Tang or Shen Ling Bai Zhu San.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersRelief with warmth/pressure plus chronic diarrhea establishes deficient Cold.
Memory cueQi pain moves; deficient Cold seeks warmth and support.
SafetyPersistent or worsening pain, fever, blood, weight loss or dehydration requires medical assessment.

Review prompts

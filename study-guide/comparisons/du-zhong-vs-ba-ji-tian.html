Herb comparison

Eucommiae Cortex (du zhong) vs. Morindae officinalis Radix (ba ji tian)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueDu ZhongBa Ji Tian
Shared cluesBoth tonify Liver and Kidneys, strengthen sinews and bones, expel Wind-Dampness and belong to Herbs that Tonify the Yang.
Fast distinctionDeficiency-centered lower-back and knee pain plus fetal calming.Wind-Damp invasion exploiting Kidney Yang deficiency plus reproductive and Cold-Womb indications.

Comparative answer key

CompareDu Zhong (Eucommiae Cortex)Ba Ji Tian (Morindae officinalis Radix)
Shared categoryHerbs that Tonify the Yang.
Shared actionsTonify Liver and Kidneys, strengthen sinews and bones, and expel Wind-Dampness.
Properties and channelsWarm, sweet and slightly acrid; Liver and Kidney.Slightly warm, acrid and sweet; Kidney.
Texture or moisture clueWarms Liver-Kidney Qi without being overly drying.Soft, moist and warm without being overly drying.
Primary pain patternLower-back and knee pain due directly to Liver-Kidney deficiency.Lower-back and knee pain from Wind-Damp invasion taking advantage of Kidney Yang deficiency.
Distinctive actionsQuiets the fetus during pregnancy.Treats impotence, infertility, irregular menstruation and lower-abdominal pain from Cold in the Womb.
Formula cluesDu Huo Ji Sheng Tang and You Gui Wan.Er Xian Tang.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth tonify Yang and treat lower-back or knee pain, so fetal calming versus Wind-Damp and reproductive clues distinguish them.
Common confusionPure Liver-Kidney deficiency or pregnancy favors Du Zhong; Wind-Damp invasion, impotence, infertility or Cold-Womb pain favors Ba Ji Tian.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 794. Source Exam Key CMID 8938, questions 4–6.

Wind-Cold Invading the Lung vs. Wind-Water Affecting the Lung

Separate a respiratory Exterior pattern from an Exterior failure of water regulation with sudden facial edema.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueWind-Cold Invading LungWind-Water Affecting Lung
SharedBoth may follow an Exterior attack with aversion to wind/cold, cough and a floating pulse.
Fast distinctionRespiratory signs dominate: sneezing, nasal discharge, body aches and cough.Water regulation fails: sudden swelling of face/eyelids, scant urination and heaviness accompany Exterior signs.

Clinical table

CompareWind-Cold Invading LungWind-Water Affecting Lung
Primary systemLung diffusion with an Exterior pathogen.Lung diffusion fails to move water through the upper body.
PulseFloating-tight.Floating-slippery or floating-rapid depending presentation.
Formula associationMa Huang Tang or Xiao Qing Long Tang by signs.The supplied table associates Xiao Qing Long Tang; edema mechanism must guide selection.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersSudden upper-body edema—not cough alone—identifies Wind-Water.
Memory cueWind-Cold blocks breath; Wind-Water blocks breath and swells the face.
SafetyNew facial swelling can be allergy, kidney or heart disease. Tongue swelling, wheeze or breathing difficulty is an emergency.

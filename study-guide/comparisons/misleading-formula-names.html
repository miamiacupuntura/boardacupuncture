6 misleading names

Name 1

Wēn Dǎn Tāng 温胆汤

“Warm the Gallbladder” is functionally a cooling Phlegm-Heat formula.

Reality:
Timidity, insomnia, palpitations, dizziness or nausea with Phlegm-Heat signs.

Name 2

Bǔ Yáng Huán Wǔ Tāng 补阳还五汤

Despite “supplement Yang,” the formula primarily tonifies Qi and moves Blood after stroke.

Reality:
Chronic post-stroke weakness or hemiplegia from Qi deficiency with Blood stasis—not acute stroke treatment.

Name 3

Dāng Guī Bǔ Xuè Tāng 当归补血汤

The Blood-tonifying formula named for Dāng Guī is mostly Huáng Qí.

Reality:
Blood deficiency after loss with floating deficiency Heat, fatigue or weak recovery.
Read chapter 3 of 6 →
Name 4

Yù Nǚ Jiān 玉女煎

“Jade Woman” is not a gynecology formula—it treats Stomach Heat with Kidney Yin deficiency.

Reality:
Toothache, bleeding/swollen gums, thirst and hunger with underlying Yin deficiency.
Read chapter 4 of 6 →
Name 5

Xī Jiǎo Dì Huáng Tāng 犀角地黄汤

The historical title says rhinoceros horn, but modern lawful practice does not use rhino horn.

Reality:
Heat entering nutritive/Blood levels with bleeding, rash, high fever or delirium.
Read chapter 5 of 6 →
Name 6

Two Formulas Called Juān Bì Tāng 蠲痹汤

Two historical prescriptions share exactly the same name but target different Bi presentations.

Reality:
If the ingredient list features Huáng Qí and Jiāng Huáng, choose the upper-limb deficiency version.

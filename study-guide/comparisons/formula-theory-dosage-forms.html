Theory 11 of 12

Dosage Forms & Clinical Purpose

Compare decoctions, powders, pills, granules and topical preparations.

Core rule

RuleDosage form changes speed, strength, convenience and exposure; it is not merely packaging.

Form and purpose

ConceptWhat it meansApplication
Decoction · TangFlexible and traditionally associated with faster, stronger adjustment in acute or complex cases.Requires correct preparation and has storage/adherence limits.
Powder · SanSmaller, portable and efficient; may be infused, swallowed or used topically depending on formula.Particle size and route materially change exposure.
Pill · WanConvenient, stable and traditionally slower/gentler for chronic or maintenance use.Commercial pills vary in concentration and excipients.
Granule/extractModern pre-extracted products improve convenience and consistency.Conversion from crude herb is manufacturer-specific, not universally “six times.”
Gelatin/syrup/honey preparationUsed to bind, nourish, moisten or deliver ingredients.Account for sugar, allergens, animal origin and storage.
Topical/wash/soakLocal route can concentrate exposure at skin or mucosa.Topical does not mean nontoxic; broken skin changes absorption.

Selection questions

ConceptWhat it meansApplication
Clinical tempoHow quickly must the strategy act?Match form to urgency without sacrificing safety.
AdherenceCan the patient prepare, tolerate and store it correctly?The best theoretical form fails if it cannot be used reliably.
Product equivalenceAre the ingredients, extraction ratio and daily exposure truly comparable?Do not substitute forms by name alone.

Responsible use

Educational summary for formula analysis and exam preparation. Doses, toxic preparations, interactions and clinical decisions require current references and professional supervision.

Theory 4 of 12

Chief, Deputy, Assistant & Envoy

Map every ingredient to the Jun–Chen–Zuo–Shi hierarchy.

Core rule

RuleRole is defined by the job inside this formula—not by a permanent rank assigned to the herb.

The four roles

ConceptWhat it meansApplication
Chief · JunDirectly treats the principal pattern and its defining manifestations.Often carries the largest strategic weight, but may be one herb or a functional unit.
Deputy · ChenReinforces the chief or directly treats an important coexisting pattern.A formula can have multiple deputies serving different branches.
Helping assistant · ZuoStrengthens chief/deputy actions or treats secondary symptoms.Ask what important complaint remains after the core strategy.
Corrective assistant · ZuoReduces toxicity, harshness or an unwanted tendency.This role explains many apparently contradictory ingredients.
Strategic assistant · ZuoUses an opposing action that helps a difficult formula reach its goal.Opposition can be deliberate rather than an error.
Envoy · ShiGuides to a region/channel or harmonizes temperature, direction and speed.An envoy may perform both guidance and integration.

Common traps

ConceptWhat it meansApplication
Largest doseChief often—but not always—has the numerically largest dose.Determine role from strategy before grams.
Famous herbA well-known herb is not automatically chief.The same herb can change role across formulas.
One role onlyOne ingredient can perform more than one role.Explain the dominant role and any secondary function.

Responsible use

Educational summary for formula analysis and exam preparation. Doses, toxic preparations, interactions and clinical decisions require current references and professional supervision.

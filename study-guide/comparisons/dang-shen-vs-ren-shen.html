Herb comparison

Codonopsis Radix (dang shen) vs. Ginseng Radix (ren shen)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueDang ShenRen Shen
Shared cluesBoth are dried roots that tonify Spleen and Lung Qi and belong to Herbs that Tonify the Qi.
Fast distinctionMilder Qi deficiency, especially Spleen-Lung deficiency with weak digestion.Stronger Qi deficiency and urgent patterns of collapse, abandonment or profuse bleeding.

Comparative answer key

CompareDang Shen (Codonopsis Radix)Ren Shen (Ginseng Radix)
Shared categoryHerbs that Tonify the Qi.
Part usedDried root.
Relative strengthMilder; used when deficiency is not severe and sometimes preferred for ordinary Spleen and Lung Qi deficiency.Much stronger; powerfully tonifies primal Qi and is used for severe deficiency and collapse.
Organ emphasisPrimarily Spleen and Lung; useful when Spleen Qi deficiency causes weak digestion.Tonifies primal Qi, reinforces the Spleen and Lung, generates fluids and supports the Heart and Spirit.
Blood and FluidsSupports the normal production of Blood and Fluids through its Qi-tonifying action.Generates fluids and is often combined with Blood tonics when Qi deficiency impairs Blood production.
Emergency clueNot the first choice for abandonment or collapse.Used for emergencies involving collapse, abandonment or profuse bleeding with severe Qi loss.
Formula clueCommonly substitutes for Ren Shen in milder tonifying formulas.Bai Hu Jia Ren Shen Tang is the clue when intense Heat with thirst and sweating has injured Qi and fluids.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe two herbs share Spleen- and Lung-Qi tonification, so strength and emergency use are the fastest differentiators.
Memory cueDang Shen supports ordinary Spleen-Lung deficiency; Ren Shen strongly restores primal Qi and rescues collapse.
Dose facts in sourceDang Shen: 9–30 g. Ren Shen: 1–9 g, with up to 30 g listed for collapse. These are exam-source facts, not prescribing guidance.
Compatibility facts in sourceBoth are listed as incompatible with Li Lu. Additional Ren Shen cautions name Wu Ling Zhi and substances that may reduce its tonifying effect, including Lai Fu Zi, tea and turnips.
Adverse-effect facts in sourceDang Shen: upset stomach, itching and reduced appetite. Ren Shen overdose: headache, insomnia, palpitations and elevated blood pressure. Suspected overdose requires professional medical evaluation; this page is not emergency guidance.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 715. Source Exam Keys CMID 8928 and CMID 9043, questions 1–12.

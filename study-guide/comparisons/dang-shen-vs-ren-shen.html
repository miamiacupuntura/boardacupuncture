Herb comparison

Codonopsis Radix (dang shen) vs. Ginseng Radix (ren shen)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueDang ShenRen Shen
Shared cluesBoth are dried roots with similar Qi-tonifying properties and functions. Both belong to Herbs that Tonify the Qi.
Fast distinctionMilder deficiency patterns, especially Spleen and Lung Qi deficiency with difficult digestion.Stronger and used for emergencies, abandonment, collapse or profuse bleeding.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe two herbs have similar Qi-tonifying functions, so severity and emergency use are the fastest differentiators.
Common confusionMilder Spleen-Lung deficiency favors Dang Shen; abandonment, collapse or profuse bleeding favors Ren Shen.
Dose facts in sourceDang Shen: 9–30 g. Ren Shen: 1–9 g, with up to 30 g listed for collapse. These are exam-source facts, not prescribing guidance.
Compatibility facts in sourceBoth are listed as incompatible with Li Lu. Additional Ren Shen cautions name Wu Ling Zhi, Gui Ban, Lai Fu Zi, tea and turnips. Dang Shen receives context-specific Mai Men Dong statements in questions 5 and 6.
Adverse-effect facts in sourceDang Shen: upset stomach, itching and reduced appetite. Ren Shen overdose: headache, insomnia, palpitations and elevated blood pressure. The source mentions mung bean soup, but suspected overdose requires urgent professional medical care; this page is not emergency guidance.
Source-data noteQuestions 6, 8, 10 and 11 include dashed placeholders instead of a substantive paired answer. No missing content has been inferred.

Exam trap · Category inconsistency

Equiseti hiemalis Herba (mu zei) vs. Tribuli Fructus (ci ji li)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueMu ZeiCi Ji Li
Shared cluesBoth dredge and disperse Wind-Heat, brighten the eyes and stop itching, and may be combined for these conditions.
Fast distinctionReleases the muscle layer, expels Wind-Dampness and targets superficial visual obstructions.Calms the Liver, extinguishes Internal Wind and moves constrained Liver Qi and Blood.

Corrected comparative answer key

CompareMu Zei (Equiseti hiemalis Herba)Ci Ji Li (Tribuli Fructus)
Correct categoryCool, Acrid Herbs that Release the Exterior.Substances that Extinguish Wind and Stop Tremors.
Shared actionDisperse Wind-Heat, brighten eyes and relieve itching.
Primary emphasisReleases the muscle layer and expels Wind-Dampness.Calms Liver and extinguishes Internal Wind.
Distinctive clueMore strongly grinds down superficial visual obstructions and is described as specific for them.Dredges Liver constraint to promote stagnant Qi and Blood flow.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe shared eye and itching actions can obscure the exterior-versus-Internal-Wind distinction.
Common confusionSuperficial visual obstruction and muscle-layer release favors Mu Zei; Liver constraint and Internal Wind favors Ci Ji Li.
Exam trapThe source materials conflict: CMID 8923 and CMID 9021 each include a question that labels Mu Zei “Warm, Acrid,” while another labels it “Cool, Acrid.” The correct category is Cool, Acrid Herbs that Release the Exterior.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 974. Source Exam Keys CMID 8923 and CMID 9021, questions 3–4; category corrected here.

Yin Stages: Taiyin vs. Shaoyin vs. Jueyin

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueTaiyinShaoyin ColdShaoyin HeatJueyin
Shared clueAll are deeper Yin-stage patterns; the task is to identify digestive deficiency, devastated Yang, depleted Yin or mixed upper Heat/lower Cold.
Fast distinctionSpleen Cold: abdominal fullness, poor appetite, vomiting and diarrhea without thirst.Yang collapse: aversion to cold, cold limbs, somnolence, clear diarrhea and faint pulse.Yin depletion: irritability, insomnia, dry mouth/throat and dark scant urine.Mixed reversal: thirst and chest Heat above with hunger without desire to eat, vomiting or diarrhea and cold below.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersDiarrhea can occur in several stages. Thermal direction, consciousness, thirst and pulse strength locate the deeper mechanism.
Memory cueTaiyin digests poorly; Shaoyin freezes or dries; Jueyin reverses Heat above and Cold below.
SafetyAltered consciousness, shock signs, severe dehydration, GI bleeding, rigid abdominal pain or persistent vomiting requires emergency care. Fu Zi formulas require expert use.

Jin Gui Shen Qi Wan vs. Zhen Wu Tang vs. Si Ni Tang

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueJin Gui Shen Qi WanZhen Wu TangSi Ni Tang
Shared clueAll warm Yang and may use Fu Zi for Cold, weakness, edema or impaired fluid metabolism; severity and treatment direction differ.
Fast distinctionChronic Kidney-Yang deficiency with weak back/knees, urinary dysfunction and cold signs.Spleen-Kidney Yang deficiency produces water accumulation, edema, heaviness and dizziness.Devastated Yang collapses acutely with profuse cold sweat, severe cold limbs and a faint pulse.

Comparative answer key

CompareJin Gui Shen Qi WanZhen Wu TangSi Ni Tang
SeverityChronic deficiency.Deficiency complicated by water retention.Acute life-threatening collapse.
Primary actionWarms Kidney Yang and restores Qi transformation.Warms Yang and promotes movement of accumulated water.Rescues devastated Yang and restores the pulse.
Decisive clueLong-term lumbar/urinary/reproductive weakness.Edema and fluid signs dominate.Cold sweat, severe cold limbs and minute pulse.
Exam shortcutRebuild fire.Move water.Rescue now.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersAll are “Yang formulas,” but they answer different clinical questions: chronic restoration, water management or emergency rescue.
Memory cueJin Gui rekindles; Zhen Wu drains; Si Ni rescues.
SafetyFu Zi must be professionally processed and prescribed. Collapse signs require emergency services—not herbal self-treatment.

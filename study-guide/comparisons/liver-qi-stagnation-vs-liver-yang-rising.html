Pattern comparison

Liver Qi Stagnation vs. Liver Yang Rising

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueLiver Qi StagnationLiver Yang Rising
Shared clueBoth can produce irritability, a wiry pulse, headache and symptoms worsened by stress.
Fast distinctionConstraint and distention: sighing, rib-side fullness, mood swings and irregular menses.Upward pressure: dizziness, tinnitus, vertex/temporal headache and red face with deficiency below.

Comparative answer key

CompareLiver Qi StagnationLiver Yang Rising
DirectionQi is constrained and fails to spread.Yang rises excessively to the head.
RootUsually excess or functional constraint.Usually Liver-Kidney Yin deficiency below.
Tongue / pulseOften normal or dusky; wiry.Red sides, little coat; wiry, sometimes thin.
Formula examplesChai Hu Shu Gan San; Xiao Yao San if deficient.Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth involve Liver dysregulation, but stagnation feels tight and distended while rising Yang concentrates symptoms in the head.
Memory cueQi stagnation presses sideways; Yang rising pushes upward.
SafetySudden severe headache, neurologic deficit, very high blood pressure or altered consciousness is an emergency.

Stomach Qi Stagnation vs. Food Retention

Use food history, regurgitation and tongue coating to distinguish functional constraint from retained food.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueStomach Qi StagnationFood Retention
SharedBoth cause epigastric distention/fullness, belching, irritability and discomfort after eating.
Fast distinctionQi is stuck: distention varies with emotion and belching may relieve it.Food is retained: clear overeating history, sour/foul regurgitation, bad breath and a thick greasy coat.

Clinical table

CompareStomach Qi StagnationFood Retention
Primary clueConstraint and impaired Qi movement.Undigested food accumulation.
Tongue / pulseOften normal or mildly coated; wiry.Thick greasy coat; slippery or forceful.
Formula associationChen Xiang Jiang San or Ban Xia Hou Po Tang by context.Bao He Wan or Zhi Shi Dao Zhi Wan.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersA dietary trigger plus foul-sour signs and thick coating establishes Food Retention.
Memory cueQi stagnation feels blocked; Food Retention smells sour and looks coated.
SafetyPersistent vomiting, severe distention, inability to pass stool/gas, fever or intense pain may indicate obstruction and needs urgent care.

Review prompts

  • What proves retained food?
  • Which varies with emotion?
  • How does the coating differ?

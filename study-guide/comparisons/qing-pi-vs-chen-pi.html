Herb comparison

Citri reticulatae viride Pericarpium (qing pi) vs. Citri reticulatae Pericarpium (chen pi)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueQing PiChen Pi
Shared cluesBoth come from the same fruit at different stages, regulate Qi, are warm and acrid, and enter the Stomach.
Fast distinctionImmature green peel; fiercer, sinking and Liver/Gallbladder focused.Mature peel; more moderate, floating and Spleen/Lung focused.

Comparative answer key

CompareQing Pi (immature)Chen Pi (mature)
Botanical labelCitri reticulatae viride Pericarpium.Citri reticulatae Pericarpium.
Common categoryHerbs that Regulate the Qi.
Properties/channelsWarm, acrid and bitter; Gallbladder, Liver and Stomach.Warm and acrid; Lung, Spleen and Stomach.
Relative actionFiercer; breaks Qi stagnation, reduces accumulation and transforms obstruction.More moderate; moves Qi, strengthens the Spleen, dries Dampness and transforms phlegm.
DirectionSinks and directs downward, entering Liver/Gallbladder and treating what is low.Raises and floats, entering Spleen/Lung and treating what is higher.
Five-phase imageJust-ripening spring Wood energy; relates to the Liver.Harmonious Earth nature; relates to the Spleen.
Clinical emphasisLiver Qi stagnation with phlegm binding the chest, breast or ribs; mastitis; severe Food Stagnation; Shan Qi.Middle-jiao Qi stagnation or turbid Dampness with nausea, vomiting, fullness or hiccup; Lung phlegm with profuse sputum; added to tonic formulas to prevent stagnation.
Formula cluesMu Xiang Bing Lang Wan; Tian Tai Wu Yao San.Er Chen Tang; Ping Wei San; San Zi Yang Qin Tang; Liu Jun Zi Tang.
Name clueGreen or unripe tangerine peel; “blue citrus” in the source.Ju Pi; mature tangerine peel.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe two medicinals come from the same fruit and share key properties, but maturity changes their force, direction and channel emphasis.
Common confusionQing Pi is the forceful Liver/Gallbladder and downward option; Chen Pi is the moderate Spleen/Lung option that dries Dampness and transforms phlegm.
How it may appearBreast/rib pain, severe stagnation or a downward clue favors Qing Pi. Middle-jiao nausea/fullness, profuse sputum or use in tonic formulas favors Chen Pi.
Dose in sourceThe exam material lists a common range of 3–9 g. This page is an exam-review comparison, not a prescribing guide.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 515. Source Exam Key CMID 8914.

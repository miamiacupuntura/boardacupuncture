Liver Qi Invading the Spleen vs. Invading the Stomach

Use the direction of digestive failure—stool and transport versus nausea and counterflow—to locate the invasion.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueLiver Invading SpleenLiver Invading Stomach
SharedBoth are emotionally triggered Liver–digestive disharmonies with distention, pain and a wiry pulse.
Fast distinctionSpleen transport disrupted: alternating constipation/diarrhea, abdominal pain and post-stress bowel change.Stomach descent disrupted: epigastric fullness, sour regurgitation, nausea, vomiting and belching.

Clinical table

CompareLiver Invading SpleenLiver Invading Stomach
Digestive directionTransformation/transport and stool are affected.Receiving/descending fails and Qi rebels upward.
Formula associationXiao Yao San or Tong Xie Yao Fang by deficiency and diarrhea.Si Mo Tang or Xuan Fu Dai Zhe Tang by counterflow presentation.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersBowel pattern identifies Spleen invasion; upper GI counterflow identifies Stomach invasion.
Memory cueSpleen invasion changes stool; Stomach invasion sends Qi up.
SafetyPersistent vomiting, blood, dehydration, severe pain or weight loss requires medical investigation.

Review prompts

  • Which alternates diarrhea/constipation?
  • Which has acid reflux?
  • What direction fails in each?

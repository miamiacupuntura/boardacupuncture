Herb comparison

Longan Arillus (long yan rou) vs. Jujubae Fructus (da zao)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueLong Yan RouDa Zao
Shared cluesBoth fruits are sweet and warm, enter the Spleen, augment Qi and Blood, tonify the Spleen and Stomach, and calm the Spirit.
Fast distinctionMore moistening and more strongly Blood-tonifying and Spirit-calming.Qi tonic that uniquely moderates harsh herbs and protects the Spleen.

Comparative answer key

CompareLong Yan Rou (Longan Arillus)Da Zao (Jujubae Fructus)
English nameLongans; flesh of longan fruit.Jujube dates; Chinese date.
CategoryHerbs that Tonify the Blood.Herbs that Tonify the Qi.
Properties and channelsSweet and warm; Spleen and Heart.Sweet and warm; Spleen and Stomach.
Primary emphasisTonifies Heart and Spleen Blood, enriches the Heart and more strongly calms the Spirit.Tonifies Qi, nourishes Blood, calms the Mind and harmonizes other herbs.
Distinctive indicationsInsomnia, palpitations and memory lapse associated with excessive thinking or overwork.Fatigue and shortness of breath; also moderates harsh herbs so they do not injure the Spleen.
Contraindications in sourceMiddle Jiao Dampness and Phlegm-Fire.Excess Dampness, food stagnation, Phlegm, worms and abdominal fullness.
Dose in source6–15 g; often eaten alone.3–12 pieces or 10–30 g.
Formula cluesGui Pi Tang and Da Fang Feng Tang.Gan Mai Da Zao Tang and many other formulas; also Gui Pi Tang.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth are sweet, warm fruits that support Qi, Blood and the Spirit, so category and secondary action are the clearest separators.
Common confusionHeart Blood deficiency and stronger Spirit calming favor Long Yan Rou; Qi deficiency and formula harmonization favor Da Zao.
Common formulaBoth appear in Restore the Spleen Decoction (Gui Pi Tang).
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 763. Source Exam Keys CMID 8941 and CMID 9001, questions 1–7.

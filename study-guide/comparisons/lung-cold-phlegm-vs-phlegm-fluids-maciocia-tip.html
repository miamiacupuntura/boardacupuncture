Cold-Phlegm vs. Phlegm-Fluids Obstructing the Lung

Distinguish a broad Cold-Phlegm cough from retained thin fluids with characteristically watery-frothy sputum.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueCold-PhlegmPhlegm-Fluids Obstructing Lung
SharedBoth cause cough with copious white watery sputum, chest oppression and a slippery-slow pulse.
Fast distinctionCold-Phlegm is broader: cold feeling, phlegm in throat and symptoms worse after cold exposure.Phlegm-Fluids emphasize retained thin fluid: very watery/frothy sputum, dizziness and fluid sounds or fullness.

Clinical table

CompareCold-PhlegmPhlegm-Fluids Obstructing Lung
EmphasisCold congeals Phlegm.Thin fluids remain mobile but fail to transform.
Formula associationShe Gan Ma Huang Tang or San Zi Yang Qin Tang by context.Ling Gan Wu Wei Jiang Xin Tang in the supplied table.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersPhlegm-Fluids are a thin-fluid subtype; unusually watery/frothy sputum and dizziness sharpen the diagnosis.
Memory cueCold makes Phlegm; failed transformation leaves fluid sloshing.
SafetyPink/frothy sputum, severe breathlessness or sudden low oxygen can signal pulmonary edema—call emergency services.

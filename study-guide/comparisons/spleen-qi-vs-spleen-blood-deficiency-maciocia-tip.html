Spleen Qi Deficiency vs. Spleen Blood Deficiency

Separate weak digestive function from the supplied table’s combined digestive and Blood-nourishment picture.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueSpleen Qi DeficiencySpleen Blood Deficiency
SharedBoth cause fatigue, poor appetite, mild abdominal distention, pale tongue and a weak pulse.
Fast distinctionQi failure: loose stools, post-meal bloating and weak limbs dominate.Blood-nourishment picture: pallor, dizziness and scanty periods join the Spleen symptoms.

Clinical table

CompareSpleen Qi DeficiencySpleen Blood Deficiency
EmphasisDigestive transformation and transport.Qi weakness plus inadequate Blood production/nourishment.
Formula associationSi Jun Zi Tang.Gui Pi Tang in the supplied table when Heart/Spleen Blood signs coexist.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersThe label is justified by Blood-deficiency signs layered onto Spleen weakness, not by fatigue alone.
Memory cueQi deficiency digests poorly; Blood deficiency adds pallor and scanty flow.
SafetyPossible anemia, heavy or absent menstruation, weight loss or GI bleeding requires investigation.

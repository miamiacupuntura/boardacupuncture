Herb comparison

Maltosum (yi tang) vs. Glycyrrhizae Radix (gan cao)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueYi TangGan Cao
Shared clueBoth are sweet Qi tonics that enter the Lung, Spleen and Stomach, tonify Middle-Burner Qi, moderate urgency and alleviate abdominal pain.
Fast distinctionGenerates fluids more strongly, alleviates thirst and directly nourishes Blood.Resolves toxicity, stops spasms and harmonizes other herbs, but does not directly nourish Blood.

Comparative answer key

CompareYi TangGan Cao
Medicinal or scientific nameMaltosum · Saccharum Granorum; maltose or malt sugar.Glycyrrhizae Radix · Glycyrrhiza uralensis; licorice.
CategoryHerbs that Tonify the Qi.Herbs that Tonify the Qi.
Properties and channelsSlightly warm and sweet; Lung, Spleen and Stomach.Neutral or warm and sweet; all 12 channels, especially Heart, Lung, Spleen and Stomach.
Primary emphasisFortifies Spleen Qi, soothes the Middle, generates Lung fluids, relieves thirst and nourishes Blood.Tonifies basal and Spleen Qi, clears Heat and toxicity, moistens Lung, transforms Phlegm, stops cough, relieves spasms and harmonizes formulas.
Decisive clinical cluesLow appetite, weak voice, dry cough and abdominal pain from deficiency Cold.Fatigue or palpitations from Qi-Blood deficiency; cough or wheeze; Fire-toxin throat or sores; abdominal or leg spasms.
Preparation clueMalt sugar dissolved into the decoction; source dose 30–60 g.Honey-fried to tonify Qi and stop spasms; raw to relieve toxicity; source dose 3–6 g.
Formula cluesXiao Jian Zhong Tang; Huang Qi Jian Zhong Tang; Da Jian Zhong Tang.Zhi Gan Cao Tang; Gui Pi Tang; Shen Ling Bai Zhu San; Gan Mai Da Zao Tang; Shao Yao Gan Cao Tang; many harmonizing roles.
Shared formulasXiao Jian Zhong Tang and Huang Qi Jian Zhong Tang.
Precautions in sourceExcess Dampness, nausea, vomiting, Phlegm-Heat or food stagnation.Excess Dampness, nausea or vomiting; the source also lists long-term hypertension, edema and hypothyroid concerns, plus incompatibilities with Gan Sui, Da Ji, Yuan Hua and Hai Zao and antagonism with Yuan Zhi.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth are sweet Middle-Burner Qi tonics for abdominal pain, but fluid generation and Blood nourishment point to Yi Tang, while detoxification, spasm relief and formula harmonization point to Gan Cao.
Memory cueYi Tang makes fluids; Gan Cao guards and harmonizes.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 741. Source Exam Keys CMID 8964 and CMID 9109, questions 1–11.

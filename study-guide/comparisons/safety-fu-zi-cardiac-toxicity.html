Fù Zǐ & Cardiac Toxicity

Understand aconite processing, rhythm toxicity, early symptoms and emergency response.

Core rule

RuleProcessing reduces aconite risk but cannot make identity, dose or decoction errors harmless.

Safe-use framework

Group / stepWhat to knowSafe decision
MechanismAconitine-type alkaloids disrupt sodium channels and cardiac electrical stability.Expect mixed neurologic, gastrointestinal and rhythm findings.
IdentityFù Zǐ, Chuān Wū and Cǎo Wū differ in material, potency and use.Never substitute by pinyin familiarity or appearance.
Processing/decoctionCorrect Páo Zhì and specified prolonged pre-decoction reduce toxic alkaloids.Use authenticated prepared product; never process raw aconite at home.
Risk factorsExcess dose, concentrated products, alcohol, arrhythmia, electrolyte disturbance and rhythm-active drugs reduce safety.Review antiarrhythmics, digoxin, diuretics and cardiac history.
Early symptomsNumb lips/tongue, tingling, weakness, dizziness, nausea, vomiting or diarrhea may begin quickly.Stop immediately; do not call it a therapeutic reaction.
Severe toxicityPalpitations, slow or rapid irregular pulse, hypotension, chest symptoms or syncope.Emergency cardiac monitoring and treatment may be required.

When to act immediately

EmergencySuspected aconite exposure with numbness, vomiting, weakness, palpitations or irregular pulse is an emergency. Do not induce vomiting; bring the product.

Educational use: this library does not replace medical evaluation, poison control, local regulation or pharmacist consultation.

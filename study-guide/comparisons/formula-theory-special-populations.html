Theory 8 of 12

Special Populations & Clinical Context

Adjust strategy for age, pregnancy, organ function, surgery and concurrent medication.

Core rule

RuleA historical fraction or general rule is never a substitute for patient-specific pediatric, obstetric or medical review.

Higher-risk contexts

ConceptWhat it meansApplication
ChildrenDevelopment, weight, formulation, swallowing ability and rapid disease change alter risk.Do not apply age fractions mechanically; require pediatric-qualified dosing.
Older adults/frailtyReduced reserve, polypharmacy, falls and renal/hepatic change narrow tolerance.Start conservatively and review medications and function.
Pregnancy/lactationBlood breakers, drastic purgatives, toxic agents and milk transfer require special review.Use dedicated pregnancy/lactation screening before any formula.
Liver/kidney diseaseClearance, fluid balance and susceptibility to contaminants may be altered.Coordinate laboratory and medical monitoring.
Perioperative/bleedingBlood-moving, anticoagulant, sedative and glucose/BP effects can affect procedures.Follow the procedural team’s individualized stop/restart plan.
Concurrent drugsSimilar or opposing actions may create bleeding, rhythm, BP, glucose, sedation or electrolyte risk.Reconcile the entire formula and medication list.

Exam principle

ConceptWhat it meansApplication
Same pattern, different patientThe treatment principle may remain, but intensity and ingredient choice change.Choose the safest strategy that still addresses the mechanism.
Referral thresholdSevere, unexplained or rapidly changing symptoms may not be appropriate for herbal management alone.Recognize when biomedical evaluation takes priority.

Responsible use

Educational summary for formula analysis and exam preparation. Doses, toxic preparations, interactions and clinical decisions require current references and professional supervision.

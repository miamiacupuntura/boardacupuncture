Pathology or symptom comparison

Infantile Convulsions Pattern Differentiation

Diagnostic orientation

Core ideaA pediatric seizure is first a medical emergency question; pattern differentiation is secondary to airway, timing and urgent evaluation.
Exam focusFever, consciousness, digestive signs and acute versus chronic onset

Comparative differentiation

Pattern or presentationDecisive cluesRepresentative formula/herbStrategy
Acute fright Wind—external WindAbrupt high fever, thirst, headache, inflamed throat, agitation, jaw and limb spasms, rigid neck, upturned eyes or opisthotonosQing Wen Bai Du YinClear Heat, open the orifices and extinguish Wind.
Acute fright Wind—internal Phlegm-HeatPoor appetite, vomiting, fever, abdominal pain or constipation, then impaired consciousness, convulsions, wheezing and abdominal distentionXiao Er Hui Chun DanClear Phlegm-Heat and extinguish Wind.
Acute fright Wind—sudden frightNo fever; cold limbs, restless sleep or crying, marked jumpiness, greenish complexion and intermittent convulsionsHu Po Bao Long Wan; Zhu Sha An Shen Wan is historical/obsoleteSettle fright and calm the spirit; never use cinnabar-containing products.
Chronic fright Wind—Spleen deficiency with Liver exuberanceEmaciation, sallow complexion, fatigue, sleep with eyes partly open, intermittent spasms, cold limbs, loose green stool or edemaHuan Gan Li Pi TangSupport the Spleen and moderate Liver Wind.
Chronic fright Wind—Liver-Kidney Yin deficiencyEmaciation, red cheeks, hot palms and soles, restlessness, dry stool and intermittent convulsionsDa Ding Feng Zhu or San Jia Fu Mai TangEnrich Yin and extinguish deficiency Wind.

Study rule

How to distinguishHigh fever and abrupt rigidity point to acute Heat-Wind; digestive obstruction and noisy breathing point to Phlegm-Heat; cold fright without fever differs from chronic wasting and deficiency signs.
Memory cueAcute: fever, Phlegm or fright. Chronic: wasting with Spleen or Yin deficiency.
SafetyCall emergency services for a first seizure, a seizure lasting 5 minutes or more, repeated seizures, breathing difficulty, injury, stiff neck or persistent altered consciousness. Place the child on the side and put nothing in the mouth. Zhu Sha/cinnabar contains mercury and is obsolete.

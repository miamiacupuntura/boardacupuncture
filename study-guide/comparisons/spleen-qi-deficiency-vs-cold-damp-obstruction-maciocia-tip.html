Spleen Qi Deficiency vs. Cold-Damp Obstructing the Spleen

Separate a deficient root from an excess pathogenic obstruction of the same digestive functions.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueSpleen Qi DeficiencyCold-Damp Obstruction
SharedBoth cause poor appetite, abdominal distention, fatigue and loose stools.
Fast distinctionDeficiency: chronic weakness, pale tongue with teeth marks and weak pulse.Obstruction: marked heaviness/fullness, nausea, thick greasy white coat and slippery/soggy pulse.

Clinical table

CompareSpleen Qi DeficiencyCold-Damp Obstruction
NatureDeficient transformation.Excess Damp pathogen blocks transformation.
Formula associationSi Jun Zi Tang.Ping Wei San.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersTongue coating and heaviness reveal the excess branch; weak pulse and chronicity reveal the deficient root.
Memory cueDeficiency lacks power; Dampness adds weight and coating.
SafetyUnexplained weight loss, severe pain, blood, dehydration or persistent symptoms needs evaluation.

Review prompts

  • Which has teeth marks?
  • Which has thick greasy coat?
  • Can both coexist?

Pattern comparison

Dampness vs. Phlegm

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueDampnessPhlegm
Shared clueBoth result from impaired fluid transformation and may cause heaviness, chest/epigastric fullness, nausea, a greasy coat and slippery qualities.
Fast distinctionDiffuse and heavy: body heaviness, edema, loose stool, cloudy discharge, poor appetite and a soggy/slippery pulse.Condensed and obstructive: sputum, nodules, globus, dizziness, altered consciousness or a distinctly slippery pulse.

Comparative answer key

CompareDampnessPhlegm
Physical ideaWateriness and diffuse accumulation.Condensed pathological fluid.
Typical locationMiddle Jiao, flesh, joints and lower burner.Lung, throat, channels or sensory orifices.
Decisive clueHeaviness, swelling and loose stool.Sputum, mass, vertigo or orifice obstruction.
Formula examplesPing Wei San or Shen Ling Bai Zhu San by excess/deficiency.Er Chen Tang or Di Tan Tang by location.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersPhlegm is often described as a more condensed development of Dampness. Visible sputum is not required when nodules, vertigo or orifice signs show hidden Phlegm.
Memory cueDampness spreads and weighs; Phlegm condenses and blocks.
SafetySudden confusion, focal neurologic deficit, severe vertigo, breathing difficulty, coughing blood or a new neck mass requires biomedical evaluation.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

  • Which produces diffuse heaviness?
  • Which produces nodules or altered consciousness?
  • How can Phlegm exist without sputum?

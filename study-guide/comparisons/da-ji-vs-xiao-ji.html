Herb comparison

Cirsii japonici Herba sive Radix (da ji) vs. Cirsii Herba (xiao ji)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueDa JiXiao Ji
Shared cluesBoth cool Blood, stop bleeding and drive downward to guide out Blood stasis. Their flowers are described as sweet and warm and their roots as slightly cooling.
Fast distinctionStronger sore- and swelling-reducing herb for abscesses.Weaker for sores; primarily associated with bloody urine.

Comparative answer key

CompareDa JiXiao Ji
Shared categoryHerbs that Regulate the Blood · Herbs that Stop Bleeding.
Habitat clueGrows in mountain valleys.Grows on flats and marshes.
Primary distinctionIts root treats swollen abscesses; also reduces sores and swellings.Breaks up Blood stasis but has very little sore-reducing effect.
Bleeding emphasisCools Blood and stops coughing or vomiting of blood, epistaxis, uterine bleeding and hematuria.Weaker overall and used primarily for bloody urine.
Formula clueShi Hui San.Xiao Ji Yin Zi.
Additional source detailsCool and sweet; enters Liver, Spleen and Heart. Contraindicated in Spleen and Stomach deficiency Cold.Breaks up stasis and disperses toxicity.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe similar names and shared hemostatic actions make strength and symptom location especially important.
Common confusionAbscesses, boils and stronger sore reduction favor Da Ji; bloody urine and little sore reduction favor Xiao Ji.
Source-data noteQuestion 3 contains the incomplete phrase “Is slight, it can stop bleeding and reduce heat” for Da Ji. This note preserves the supported actions without supplying the missing wording.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 568. Source Exam Key CMID 8940.

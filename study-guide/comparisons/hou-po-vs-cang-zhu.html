Herb comparison

Magnoliae officinalis Cortex (hou po) vs. Atractylodis Rhizoma (cang zhu)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueHou PoCang Zhu
Shared cluesBoth are acrid, bitter and warm, enter the Spleen and Stomach, and dry Dampness in middle-burner Damp obstruction.
Decisive clueQi stagnation with epigastric or abdominal fullness, constipation, or wheezing.Pronounced Dampness requiring strong drying, Spleen support, sweating or Wind-Damp dispersal.

Comparative answer key

CompareHou Po (Magnoliae officinalis Cortex)Cang Zhu (Atractylodis Rhizoma)
Shared categoryAromatic Herbs that Transform Dampness.
Primary emphasisPromotes Qi movement while drying Dampness and directs Qi downward.Is especially drying and strengthens the Spleen internally.
Fullness and accumulationTreats epigastric and abdominal distention and fullness from accumulation with form, such as constipation, or from formless constrained Dampness.Better recognized by its strong aromatic drying action rather than by downward Qi movement.
Exterior and respiratory clueDirects rebellious Qi downward to treat wheezing.Induces sweating, releases the exterior and dispels Wind-Dampness.
Additional clueFullness is its central distinctive indication in this comparison.With Huang Bai, treats Damp-Heat in the lower burner; it is also used for night blindness.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersTheir shared properties, channels, category and middle-burner Dampness action make the secondary action decisive.
Common confusionHou Po primarily moves Qi while drying Dampness; Cang Zhu primarily dries Dampness while strengthening the Spleen.
How it may appearFullness, constipation or wheezing favors Hou Po. Sweating, Wind-Dampness, lower-burner Damp-Heat with Huang Bai or night blindness favors Cang Zhu.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 472. Source Exam Keys CMID 8904 and CMID 8986, questions 3–5.

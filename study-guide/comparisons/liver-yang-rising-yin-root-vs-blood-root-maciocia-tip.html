Liver Yang Rising: Yin-Deficiency Root vs. Blood-Deficiency Root

Use Heat and Kidney signs versus pallor and undernourishment to identify the deficient root beneath rising Yang.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueYang Rising from Yin DeficiencyYang Rising from Blood Deficiency
SharedBoth cause dizziness, tinnitus, headache, blurred vision, tremor and a wiry-thin pulse.
Fast distinctionYin root: dry throat/eyes, night sweating, low-back weakness, scant dark urine and red or uncoated tongue.Blood root: pallor, insomnia, numbness, scanty menses and a pale thin tongue without strong Heat.

Clinical table

CompareYang Rising from Yin DeficiencyYang Rising from Blood Deficiency
Root clueDryness, empty Heat and Kidney involvement.Pale undernourishment of eyes, sinews and Spirit.
Formula associationSan Jia Fu Mai Tang appears in the supplied table for severe Yin-fluid depletion.E Jiao Ji Zi Huang Tang appears for Blood-deficient Wind/Yang signs.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersThe upward symptoms do not reveal the root; tongue color, Heat and nourishment signs do.
Memory cueYin root is dry-hot; Blood root is pale-empty.
SafetySevere dizziness with neurologic deficit, chest pain or very high blood pressure needs urgent evaluation.

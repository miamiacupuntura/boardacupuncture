Three Qi-stagnation locations · FWB Regulate Qi

Yue Ju Wan vs. Ban Xia Hou Po Tang vs. Gua Lou Xie Bai Bai Jiu Tang

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueYue Ju WanBan Xia Hou Po TangGua Lou Xie Bai Bai Jiu Tang
Shared clueAll move constrained Qi and address secondary accumulations, but the location and dominant manifestation differ.
Fast distinctionSix stagnations—Qi, Blood, Phlegm, Fire, Dampness and Food—with broad digestive/emotional constraint.Qi and Phlegm bind in the throat: a plum-pit or globus sensation that cannot be swallowed or coughed out.Turbid Phlegm blocks chest Yang: chest pain or oppression, wheezing and pain radiating to the back.

Comparative answer key

CompareYue Ju WanBan Xia Hou Po TangGua Lou Xie Bai Bai Jiu Tang
Primary locationSystemic/middle-burner stagnation.Throat and chest.Chest and upper back.
Treatment directionMove Qi to release six linked stagnations.Move Qi, transform Phlegm and direct rebellious Qi downward.Open chest Yang, move Qi and expel turbid Phlegm.
Key ingredientsXiang Fu, Chuan Xiong, Cang Zhu, Zhi Zi and Shen Qu.Ban Xia, Hou Po, Fu Ling, Zi Su Ye and Sheng Jiang.Gua Lou, Xie Bai and Bai Jiu.
Decisive clueSeveral stagnation types coexist.Persistent lump-in-throat sensation.Chest Bi pain/oppression with Phlegm.
Exam shortcutEverything is stuck.One knot in the throat.Chest Yang cannot open.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it matters“Qi stagnation” is only the starting point. The distribution—multiple systems, throat, or chest Yang—selects the formula.
Memory cueYue Ju unblocks six; Ban Xia Hou Po opens the throat; Gua Lou–Xie Bai opens the chest.
SafetyNew chest pain, pressure, sweating, shortness of breath or pain radiating to the arm, jaw or back requires emergency evaluation.
SourceSource bank: FWB 9.1.2, Comparison Formulas to Regulate the Qi, activity 5; corroborated with individual formula profiles.

Formula comparison · FWB Warm the Interior

Si Ni Tang vs. Wu Zhu Yu Tang

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueSi Ni TangWu Zhu Yu Tang
Shared clueBoth warm internal Cold and may present with vomiting, diarrhea and cold extremities, but the mechanism and dominant symptom differ.
Fast distinctionSpleen-Kidney Yang devastation: profound cold limbs, somnolence, clear diarrhea and a minute or faint pulse.Liver-Stomach deficiency-Cold with rebellious turbid Yin: vomiting or dry heaves, vertex headache and irritability.

Comparative answer key

CompareSi Ni TangWu Zhu Yu Tang
Organ focusSpleen and Kidney Yang, with systemic collapse.Liver, Stomach and Kidney, with upward rebellion.
Dominant directionYang fails to warm and support the whole body.Cold and turbid Yin drive Stomach/Liver Qi upward.
Decisive clueSomnolence, profound weakness and extensive cold limbs.Vomiting or dry heaves with vertex headache and agitation.
Key ingredientsProcessed Fu Zi, Gan Jiang and Zhi Gan Cao.Wu Zhu Yu, Sheng Jiang, Ren Shen and Da Zao.
Therapeutic emphasisRestore Yang and stop Cold diarrhea.Warm Liver-Stomach and descend rebellious Qi to stop vomiting.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersCold extremities and digestive symptoms overlap. Collapse and diarrhea point to Si Ni Tang; a strongly upward, vomiting-centered pattern points to Wu Zhu Yu Tang.
Memory cueSi Ni rescues sinking Yang; Wu Zhu Yu sends rebellious Cold downward.
SafetySi Ni Tang contains processed Fu Zi and requires qualified supervision. Wu Zhu Yu is a potent medicinal with dose-related adverse effects. Suspected shock or persistent vomiting requires urgent care.

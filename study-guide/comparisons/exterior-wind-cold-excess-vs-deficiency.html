Exterior Wind-Cold Excess vs. Exterior Wind-Cold Deficiency

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueExterior Wind-Cold ExcessExterior Wind-Cold Deficiency
Shared clueBoth present acutely with fever, aversion to cold, headache, body aches and a floating pulse.
Fast distinctionClosed pores: no sweating, stronger chills, wheezing and a floating-tight pulse.Disharmonized pores: spontaneous sweating, aversion to wind and a floating-moderate or frail pulse.

Comparative answer key

CompareExterior Wind-Cold ExcessExterior Wind-Cold Deficiency
Exterior dynamicStrong pathogen tightly constrains the surface.Defensive Qi cannot regulate the surface and Ying-Wei are disharmonized.
SweatAbsent.Present spontaneously.
PulseFloating and tight.Floating and moderate/frail.
Formula examplesMa Huang Tang.Gui Zhi Tang.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe word “deficiency” describes the state of the Exterior, not necessarily a chronic constitutional deficiency.
Memory cueExcess closes; deficiency leaks.
SafetyDifficulty breathing, chest pain, high fever, confusion or severe dehydration requires medical care. Ma Huang formulas require cardiovascular screening.

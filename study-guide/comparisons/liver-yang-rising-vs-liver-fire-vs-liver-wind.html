Pattern comparison

Liver Yang Rising vs. Liver Fire vs. Liver Wind

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueLiver Yang RisingLiver Fire BlazingLiver Wind
Shared clueAll can rise to the head and cause headache, dizziness, tinnitus, red face or irritability.
Fast distinctionDeficiency-rooted ascendance: dizziness and headache with weak low back or Yin-deficiency signs.Full excess Heat: intense anger, bitter taste, red eyes, constipation and forceful rapid pulse.Movement and neurologic signs: tremor, spasm, convulsion, numbness or sudden loss of control.

Comparative answer key

CompareLiver Yang RisingLiver Fire BlazingLiver Wind
MechanismLiver/Kidney Yin fails to anchor Yang.Excess Liver Heat flares upward.Extreme Yang/Fire or deficiency generates internal movement.
Defining clueUpward symptoms plus deficiency below.Strong excess Heat signs.Involuntary movement or acute neurologic change.
Tongue / pulseRed sides; wiry, often thin.Red with yellow coat; wiry-forceful-rapid.Varies by cause; wiry, rapid or deficient.
Formula examplesTian Ma Gou Teng Yin.Long Dan Xie Gan Tang by full pattern.Zhen Gan Xi Feng Tang for severe ascendance; emergency assessment first.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThese are related but not interchangeable. Wind is identified by movement; Fire by full Heat; Yang rising by a deficient root.
Memory cueYang rises; Fire burns; Wind moves.
SafetySudden severe headache, facial droop, weakness, slurred speech, seizure or altered consciousness is an emergency.

Herb comparison

Bupleuri Radix (chai hu) vs. Stellariae Radix (yin chai hu)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueChai HuYin Chai Hu
Shared clueBoth can clear Heat, but the source emphasizes that they are otherwise unrelated botanically and functionally.
Decisive clueExterior Heat, alternating chills and fever, and a raising action.Deficiency Heat with no raising quality.

Comparative answer key

CompareChai Hu (Bupleuri Radix)Yin Chai Hu (Stellariae Radix)
CategoryCool, Acrid Herb that Releases the Exterior.Herb that Clears Heat from Deficiency.
Heat typeVents and cools exterior Heat.Cools and reduces Heat specifically caused by deficiency.
DirectionHas a raising quality.Has no raising quality.
Distinctive indicationExcels for alternating chills and fever.Deficiency Heat and the childhood nutritional-impairment indication referenced by the source.
Negative clueCannot clear Deficiency Heat or treat childhood nutritional impairment.Not the exterior-releasing, alternating-chills or Yang-raising herb.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe shared words “Chai Hu” can imply a relationship that the source explicitly rejects.
Common confusionChai Hu is exterior-releasing and raising; Yin Chai Hu clears Deficiency Heat and does not raise.
How it may appearAlternating chills and fever favors Chai Hu. A pure Deficiency-Heat presentation favors Yin Chai Hu.
Source question formatsQuestions 5 and 6 are Matching.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 227. Source Exam Key CMID 8917.

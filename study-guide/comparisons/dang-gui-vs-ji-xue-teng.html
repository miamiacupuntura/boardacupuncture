Herb comparison

Angelicae sinensis Radix (dang gui) vs. Spatholobi Caulis (ji xue teng)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueDang GuiJi Xue Teng
Shared cluesBoth tonify and invigorate Blood, stop pain and regulate menstruation. Both are warm and sweet and enter the Liver and Heart.
Fast distinctionPrimarily tonifies Blood; moistens the Intestines and treats Blood-deficiency constipation.Primarily invigorates Blood; unblocks collaterals and soothes sinews for numbness, paralysis and Wind-Damp painful obstruction.

Comparative answer key

CompareDang Gui (Angelicae sinensis Radix)Ji Xue Teng (Spatholobi Caulis)
CategoryHerbs that Tonify the Blood.Herbs that Regulate and Invigorate the Blood.
PropertiesWarm, acrid and sweet.Warm, bitter and slightly sweet.
ChannelsLiver, Heart and Spleen.Heart, Liver and Spleen.
Primary emphasisPrimarily tonifies Blood and secondarily invigorates it.Primarily invigorates Blood while also tonifying it.
Distinctive actionMoistens the Intestines; reduces swelling and generates flesh.Invigorates the collaterals and soothes the sinews.
Distinctive indicationsBlood deficiency, gynecological patterns, deficiency-Cold stasis pain, dry stools, sores and abscesses.Blood stasis with deficiency, joint pain, weak limbs, numbness, paralysis and Wind-Damp painful obstruction.
Formula or combination cluesSi Wu Tang, Wen Jing Tang, Run Chang Wan, Xiao Yao San and Dang Gui Si Ni Tang, among many others.For chronic Wind-Damp painful obstruction with Blood deficiency, the source combines it with Sang Ji Sheng, Niu Xi and Du Huo.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth tonify and move Blood and regulate menstruation; the relative emphasis on tonification versus collateral movement identifies the herb.
Common confusionConstipation, gynecology or flesh generation favors Dang Gui; numbness, paralysis, weak limbs or Wind-Damp Bi favors Ji Xue Teng.
Additional examples in sourceThe source lists aplastic anemia and leukopenia under Ji Xue Teng. These are exam-source facts, not treatment recommendations.
Source-data noteQuestions 1–3 spell the botanical name “Spatholobi Cadis”; question 4 uses “Spatholobi Caulis.” This note uses Caulis consistently.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 605. Source Exam Keys CMID 8927 and CMID 8997, questions 1–4.

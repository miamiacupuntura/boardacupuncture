Toxicity & Emergency Red Flags

Recognize high-risk identities, overdose patterns and when referral cannot wait.

Core rule

RuleIdentity first, then dose/process, then patient risk. A familiar pinyin sound is never enough.

High-risk identity groups

Group / stepWhat to knowSafe decision
Aristolochia namesGuān Mù Tōng, Guǎng Fáng Jǐ and Qīng Mù Xiāng are obsolete aristolochic-acid sources.Nephrotoxic and carcinogenic; do not use or substitute.
Aconite familyFù Zǐ, Chuān Wū and Cǎo Wū contain cardiotoxic alkaloids.Prepared product and expert protocol only; pregnancy contraindication.
Zhū Shā / Xióng HuángCinnabar contains mercury; realgar contains arsenic.Restricted/prohibited in many jurisdictions; avoid casual or prolonged internal use.
Xiāng Jiā PíPeriploca bark contains cardiac glycosides.Digoxin-like toxicity and cardiac-drug interactions.
Kǔ Xìng Rén and other cyanogenic kernelsAmygdalin can release cyanide, especially from raw/crushed excess.Use authenticated processed material and professional dosing; keep away from children.
Harsh water expellantsGān Suì, Dà Jǐ and Yuán Huā can violently purge fluids.Narrow indications, short-term expert use and major pregnancy/weak-patient cautions.
Abrus / Xiāng Sī ZǐRosary pea seed contains abrin.Potentially fatal when chewed or ingested; not an internal substitute for Chì Xiǎo Dòu.

Urgent red flags

Group / stepWhat to knowSafe decision
Cardiac/neurologicNumb lips/tongue, spreading weakness, palpitations, irregular pulse, fainting, seizures.Emergency services and poison control now.
Liver injuryDark urine, jaundice, severe fatigue, right-upper abdominal pain, persistent nausea.Stop suspected product and obtain urgent medical evaluation.
Kidney injuryMarkedly reduced urine, swelling, blood in urine, flank pain after an unknown product.Urgent medical evaluation; preserve packaging for identification.
AnaphylaxisTrouble breathing, throat/tongue swelling, widespread hives, collapse.Emergency epinephrine protocol and emergency services.
Pregnancy emergencyHeavy bleeding, severe abdominal pain, faintness or contractions after herbal exposure.Emergency obstetric evaluation.

When to act immediately

EmergencyDo not induce vomiting unless poison control directs it. Bring the package, prescription, botanical label, dose and timing to emergency care.

Educational use: this library does not replace medical evaluation, poison control, local regulation or pharmacist consultation.

Liver Yang Rising vs. Liver Fire Blazing

Separate deficiency-rooted ascendance from a stronger Full-Fire excess.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueLiver Yang RisingLiver Fire Blazing
SharedBoth rise to the head and cause headache, dizziness, tinnitus, irritability and a wiry pulse.
Fast distinctionDeficiency root: chronic dizziness/headache with dry eyes, night sweat or weak low back; pulse wiry-thin.Full excess: explosive anger, red face/eyes, bitter taste, dark urine, dry stool and a forceful wiry-rapid pulse.

Clinical table

CompareLiver Yang RisingLiver Fire Blazing
RootUsually Liver/Kidney Yin deficiency.Full excess Heat, often from prolonged constraint.
TongueRed, often peeled or little coat.Redder sides with dry yellow coat.
Formula associationTian Ma Gou Teng Yin.Long Dan Xie Gan Tang by presentation.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersIntensity alone is insufficient; deficiency signs and coating distinguish rising Yang from blazing Fire.
Memory cueYang rises from an empty root; Fire blazes from a full excess.
SafetySudden worst headache, weakness, facial droop, speech change or vision loss is a stroke emergency.

Review prompts

  • Which has low-back weakness?
  • Which has a yellow coat?
  • What pulse shows Full Fire?

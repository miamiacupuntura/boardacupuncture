Pattern comparison

Dryness Attacking the Lung vs. Lung Yin Deficiency

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueDryness Attacking the LungLung Yin Deficiency
Shared clueBoth cause dry cough, dry nose/throat, scant sputum, thirst and possible hoarseness.
Fast distinctionAcute exterior onset, often autumn: slight fever/chills, headache and dry cough with a floating pulse and thin dry coat.Chronic internal deficiency: weak persistent cough, afternoon Heat, night sweats, five-center Heat and red peeled tongue with thin rapid pulse.

Comparative answer key

CompareDryness Attacking the LungLung Yin Deficiency
OnsetAcute and seasonal/exposure related.Chronic or post-febrile/consumptive.
Exterior signsMild fever, chills or headache present.Absent; Empty Heat replaces them.
Tongue / pulseDry tongue with thin coat; floating-rapid.Red, dry, little coat; thin-rapid.
Formula examplesSang Xing Tang or Qing Zao Jiu Fei Tang by severity.Bai He Gu Jin Tang or Sha Shen Mai Dong Tang.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersDry cough is shared. Acute exterior timing and a remaining coat indicate invading Dryness; chronic Empty Heat and peeled tongue indicate Yin deficiency.
Memory cueExternal Dryness arrives; Lung Yin deficiency lingers.
SafetyBreathing difficulty, chest pain, coughing blood, low oxygen, high fever or cough lasting over three weeks requires medical assessment.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

  • Which begins acutely in dry weather?
  • Which has night sweats?
  • How do pulse and tongue coat differ?

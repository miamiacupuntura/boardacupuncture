Herb comparison

Pheretima (di long) vs. Arisaema cum Bile (dan nan xing)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueDi LongDan Nan Xing
Shared cluesBoth clear Heat and settle jitteriness and convulsions; both address febrile convulsions, Lung Heat with cough or wheezing, and impaired limb movement after Wind-Stroke.
Fast distinctionMore strongly clears Heat, calms the Liver and unblocks the collaterals.More strongly expels Wind, transforms Phlegm and settles convulsions.

Comparative answer key

CompareDi Long (Pheretima)Dan Nan Xing (Arisaema cum Bile)
CategorySubstances that Extinguish Wind and Stop Tremors.Herbs that Transform Phlegm and Stop Coughing · Herbs that Cool and Transform Phlegm-Heat.
Shared actionsClear Heat, settle convulsions and treat Lung Heat or post-stroke limb impairment.
Primary emphasisClears Heat, calms the Liver and unblocks the collaterals.Expels Wind, transforms Phlegm and settles convulsions.
Lung clueAsthma and dyspnea associated with bronchiolitis.Spasmodic cough and wheezing from Hot Phlegm obstructing the Lungs.
Neurological cluePrimarily treats paralysis by unblocking the collaterals.Phlegm-Heat causing loss of consciousness in Wind-Stroke.
Examples in sourceRestlessness, convulsions, asthma and post-stroke paralysis.Childhood pneumonia, acute bronchitis, hemiplegia and Phlegm-Heat collapse.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth treat Heat, convulsions, wheezing and post-stroke impairment, so collateral obstruction versus Wind-Phlegm is the key distinction.
Common confusionParalysis and collateral blockage favor Di Long; Hot Phlegm, spasmodic cough or impaired consciousness favor Dan Nan Xing.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 981. Source Exam Keys CMID 8943 and CMID 8932.

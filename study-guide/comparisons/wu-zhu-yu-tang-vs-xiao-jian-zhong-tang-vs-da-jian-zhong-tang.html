Wu Zhu Yu Tang vs. Xiao Jian Zhong Tang vs. Da Jian Zhong Tang

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueWu Zhu Yu TangXiao Jian Zhong TangDa Jian Zhong Tang
Shared clueAll warm the middle and treat abdominal pain or vomiting from Interior Cold with deficiency.
Fast distinctionLiver-Stomach Cold rebellion: vomiting, acid regurgitation and vertex headache.Moderate deficiency-Cold with spasmodic abdominal pain relieved by pressure and warmth.Severe deep Cold with intense pain, vomiting and visible/palpable abdominal movement.

Comparative answer key

CompareWu Zhu Yu TangXiao Jian Zhong TangDa Jian Zhong Tang
Primary dynamicCold Qi rebels upward.Middle deficiency causes tension and spasm.Severe Cold obstructs and forces Qi rebellion.
Pain intensityPain may occur, but vomiting/headache defines it.Intermittent, cramping and relieved by pressure.Intense, persistent or dramatic.
Distinct strategyWarms Liver/Stomach and descends rebellion.Warms and nourishes while harmonizing Yin-Yang.Strongly warms, disperses Cold and relieves severe pain.
Exam shortcutCold rises.Cold cramps.Cold attacks intensely.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersTemperature is shared; direction and severity distinguish upward rebellion, nutritive spasm and major Cold obstruction.
Memory cueWu Zhu Yu rises to the vertex; Xiao Jian eases cramps; Da Jian treats major pain.
SafetySevere rigid abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, distention with no stool/gas, fever or bleeding requires emergency evaluation.

Interior Excess Cold vs. Yang-Deficiency Cold

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueInterior Excess ColdYang-Deficiency Cold
Shared clueBoth cause aversion to cold, cold limbs, abdominal discomfort, pale tongue signs and a slow pulse.
Fast distinctionAcute obstruction: severe fixed pain, worse with pressure, robust constitution, thick white coat and a deep tight forceful pulse.Chronic weakness: dull pain improved by pressure, fatigue, loose stool, edema or clear urine, pale swollen tongue and deep weak slow pulse.

Comparative answer key

CompareInterior Excess ColdYang-Deficiency Cold
MechanismCold pathogen contracts and blocks Qi.Yang Qi fails to warm, move and transform.
Pain / pressureSevere, fixed and resistant to pressure; warmth helps.Dull, lingering and relieved by warmth and pressure.
Tongue / pulsePale with thick moist white coat; deep-tight-slow.Pale, swollen and wet; deep-weak-slow.
Formula examplesLiang Fu Wan or Tian Tai Wu Yao San by location.Li Zhong Wan, Zhen Wu Tang or Si Ni Tang by depth.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersTemperature alone does not distinguish them. Force, onset, pressure response and pulse strength separate excess obstruction from deficient warming.
Memory cueExcess Cold grips; Yang deficiency fades.
SafetySevere abdominal pain, rigid abdomen, persistent vomiting, fainting, GI bleeding or hypothermia requires urgent care. Fu Zi formulas require processed material and expert supervision.

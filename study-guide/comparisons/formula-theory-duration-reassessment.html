Theory 7 of 12

Duration, Stop Rules & Reassessment

Match treatment intensity to phase and define when the formula must change.

Core rule

RuleEvery strong strategy needs an endpoint, a reassessment interval and a stop rule.

Treatment phase

ConceptWhat it meansApplication
Acute/excessDispersing, sweating, purging, breaking stasis and strong Heat-clearing strategies may act quickly and consume resources.Use only while the matching excess pattern remains.
After the endpointOnce sweating, bowel movement, pathogen release or stabilization occurs, the same intensity may become harmful.Stop or transition to regulation and recovery.
Chronic diseaseGentler harmonizing or tonifying strategies may be used longer with periodic review.“Gentle” does not eliminate interaction, organ or quality risk.
Relapse or no responseFailure may mean wrong pattern, wrong product, inadequate dose, poor adherence or a biomedical red flag.Reassess rather than automatically escalating.

Monitoring questions

ConceptWhat it meansApplication
TargetWhat measurable symptom/sign should improve first?Define expected direction and timeframe.
Adverse effectWhich new symptom means the formula is too cold, hot, drying, cloying, stimulating or toxic?Give explicit stop instructions.
TransitionWhat pattern is expected after the acute branch resolves?Prepare the next principle instead of repeating by habit.

Responsible use

Educational summary for formula analysis and exam preparation. Doses, toxic preparations, interactions and clinical decisions require current references and professional supervision.

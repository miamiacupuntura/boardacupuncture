Pathology or symptom comparison

Urticaria Pattern Differentiation

Diagnostic orientation

Core ideaWheal color and trigger separate acute Wind branches; digestive symptoms, chronic fatigue or menstrual timing identify the remaining presentations.
Exam focusWheal color, trigger, digestive signs, chronicity and menstrual timing

Comparative differentiation

Pattern or presentationDecisive cluesRepresentative formula/herbStrategy
Wind-Cold invasionWhite wheals worse with Cold/Wind, better with warmth, especially in winterGui Zhi Tang or Jing Fang Bai Du SanDispel Wind-Cold and harmonize Nutritive-Defensive Qi.
Wind-Heat invasionAbrupt red intensely itchy wheals that appear/disappear quickly, with yellow coat and rapid pulseXiao Feng SanDispel Wind and clear Heat.
Stomach-Intestine Damp-HeatRed itchy wheals with epigastric/abdominal pain, constipation or diarrhea and yellow coatFang Feng Tong Sheng San + Yin Chen Hao TangClear Damp-Heat and regulate stool.
Qi and Blood deficiencyRecurrent hives for months/years, worse with exertion or fatigue, pale tongue and weak pulseBa Zhen TangSupplement Qi and Blood.
Chong-Ren dysfunctionHives recur before each menstrual period and resolve as menstruation ends, with dysmenorrhea/irregular cyclesSi Wu Tang + Er Xian TangRegulate Chong-Ren.

Study rule

How to distinguishWhite/cold is Wind-Cold; red/rapid is Wind-Heat; abdominal/bowel signs indicate Damp-Heat; chronic fatigue indicates deficiency; cycle timing indicates Chong-Ren.
Memory cueWhite-cold; red-hot; gut-damp; tired-deficient; monthly-Chong/Ren.
SafetyHives with lip/tongue swelling, throat tightness, wheezing, faintness or vomiting can be anaphylaxis: use epinephrine if prescribed and call emergency services. Persistent hives need allergy/medical assessment.
SourceInternal Medicine 1, Handout 12: Wind Rash (Feng Zhen, Urticaria).

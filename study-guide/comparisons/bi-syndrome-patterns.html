Pathology or symptom comparison

Bi Syndrome Pattern Differentiation

Diagnostic orientation

Core ideaPain behavior identifies the dominant pathogen; chronic deformity or constitutional weakness signals deeper obstruction or deficiency.
Exam focusMoving versus fixed pain, temperature, swelling and chronic deformity

Comparative differentiation

Pattern or presentationDecisive cluesRepresentative formula/herbStrategy
Wind-dominant BiPain moves between joints, flexion/extension difficulty and aversion to WindFang Feng TangDispel Wind and open channels.
Cold-dominant BiSevere fixed cold pain, worse with Cold and better with Heat; no rednessWu Tou TangWarm channels and dissipate Cold.
Damp-dominant BiHeavy aching, swelling and numbness, worse in rainy weatherYi Yi Ren TangDrain Dampness and open channels.
Heat-dominant BiHot red swollen painful joints with fever, thirst and dark scant urineBai Hu Jia Gui Zhi TangClear Heat and open channels.
Blood stasis and Phlegm turbidityChronic swelling, stiffness, deformity, immobility and purple/dark tongueTao Hong YinDispel stasis, transform Phlegm and relieve pain.
Qi-Blood and Liver-Kidney deficiencyProlonged pain with weak back/knees, numbness, dyspnea and pale weak signsDu Huo Ji Sheng Tang or San Bi TangSupplement deficiency while dispelling Wind-Cold-Damp.

Study rule

How to distinguishMoving is Wind; severe cold fixed pain is Cold; heavy swollen pain is Damp; red-hot pain is Heat; deformity/purple is stasis-Phlegm; weakness is deficiency.
Memory cueMoves—Wind; freezes—Cold; weighs—Damp; burns—Heat; deforms—stasis; weakens—deficiency.
SafetyA single hot red swollen joint with fever, sudden inability to bear weight, major trauma or rapidly progressive weakness requires urgent evaluation.

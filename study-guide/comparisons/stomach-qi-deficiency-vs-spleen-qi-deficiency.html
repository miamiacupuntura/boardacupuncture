Pattern comparison

Stomach Qi Deficiency vs. Spleen Qi Deficiency

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueStomach Qi DeficiencySpleen Qi Deficiency
Shared clueBoth cause poor appetite, fatigue, pale tongue and a weak right-guan pulse; they commonly coexist.
Fast distinctionReceiving/descending fails: epigastric emptiness, poor appetite, mild nausea, belching or tendency to vomiting.Transformation/transport fails: post-meal bloating, loose stools, heaviness, weak limbs and possible Dampness or edema.

Comparative answer key

CompareStomach Qi DeficiencySpleen Qi Deficiency
Direction/functionReceives food and descends Qi.Transforms food/fluids and raises clear Qi.
Decisive clueNausea, belching and epigastric emptiness.Loose stool, bloating, heaviness and muscle weakness.
Tongue / pulsePale, possibly center depression; weak.Pale, possibly swollen/toothmarks; weak.
Formula examplesSi Jun Zi Tang; Xiang Sha Liu Jun Zi Tang if nausea/stagnation.Si Jun Zi Tang or Shen Ling Bai Zhu San when Dampness/diarrhea dominates.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersPoor appetite is shared. Upward rebellion identifies Stomach; stool, Dampness and weak limbs identify Spleen transport failure.
Memory cueStomach cannot receive and descend; Spleen cannot transform and lift.
SafetyPersistent vomiting, progressive swallowing difficulty, GI bleeding, severe pain or unexplained weight loss requires medical evaluation.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

  • Which produces belching and nausea?
  • Which produces loose stool and heaviness?
  • Why do they often coexist?

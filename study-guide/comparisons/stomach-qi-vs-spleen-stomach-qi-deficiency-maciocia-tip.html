Batch 6 · Stomach · Tip 2 of 10

Stomach Qi vs. Spleen–Stomach Qi Deficiency

Separate a Stomach-centered empty presentation from combined failure of receiving and transforming food.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueStomach Qi DeficiencySpleen–Stomach Qi Deficiency
SharedBoth cause poor appetite, fatigue, epigastric discomfort, pale tongue and a weak pulse.
Fast distinctionStomach-centered: morning tiredness and epigastric emptiness predominate.Combined pattern: loose stool, post-meal bloating, weak limbs and possible Dampness join the Stomach signs.

Clinical table

CompareStomach Qi DeficiencySpleen–Stomach Qi Deficiency
Failed functionReceive and descend food Qi.Receive/descend plus transform/transport.
Formula associationSi Jun Zi Tang.Si Jun Zi Tang or Shen Ling Bai Zhu San when loose stool and Dampness fit.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersLoose stool and transport failure identify Spleen involvement beyond the Stomach-centered deficiency.
Memory cueStomach receives weakly; Spleen–Stomach also transforms weakly.
SafetyPersistent diarrhea, dehydration, GI bleeding, severe pain or unexplained weight loss requires assessment.

Herb comparison

Pinelliae Rhizoma preparatum (zhi ban xia) vs. Inulae Flos (xuan fu hua)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueZhi Ban XiaXuan Fu Hua
Shared cluesBoth disperse clumped Phlegm, settle the Stomach and stop vomiting.
Fast distinctionDries Dampness for thick, viscous Phlegm that is difficult to expectorate.Directs rebellious Qi downward for profuse, thin, clear Phlegm that is easy to expectorate.

Comparative answer key

CompareZhi Ban XiaXuan Fu Hua
Botanical namePinelliae Rhizoma preparatum.Inulae Flos.
Shared categoryHerbs that Transform Phlegm and Stop Coughing · Warm Herbs that Transform Cold-Phlegm.
Primary mechanismDries Dampness to transform Phlegm.Directs rebellious Qi downward, breaks clumped accumulation and promotes the movement of pathogenic water.
Phlegm clueThick, viscous Phlegm that is difficult to expectorate.Profuse, thin, clear Phlegm that is easy to expectorate.
Water and middle-burner clueFullness in the flanks, chest or abdomen from pathogenic water or thin mucus; nausea when Spleen-Stomach Qi deficiency permits Water-Dampness to collect.Pathogenic water with rebellious Qi; emphasizes downward movement rather than drying.
Stomach clueSettles nausea by drying Dampness and transforming Phlegm in the middle burner.Stops nausea and vomiting by directing rebellious Stomach Qi downward.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth can treat Phlegm with nausea or vomiting. The consistency of the Phlegm identifies the better answer: thick and sticky favors Zhi Ban Xia; abundant, thin and clear favors Xuan Fu Hua.
Memory cueBan Xia dries thick Damp-Phlegm; Xuan Fu Hua descends thin, watery Phlegm.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 433. Source Exam Key CMID 9105, questions 1–2.

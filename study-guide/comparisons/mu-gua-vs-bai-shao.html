Herb comparison

Chaenomelis Fructus (mu gua) vs. Paeoniae Radix alba (bai shao)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueMu GuaBai Shao
Shared cluesBoth treat disorders of the sinews and are commonly combined for muscular and sinew cramping or spasms.
Decisive patternDampness predominates, including damp painful obstruction, restricted joint movement or sudden turmoil disorder.Yin and Blood insufficiency leave the muscles and sinews undernourished, producing cramping, spasms and pain.

Comparative answer key

CompareChaenomelis Fructus (mu gua)Paeoniae Radix alba (bai shao)
CategoryHerbs that Dispel Wind-Dampness.Herbs that Tonify the Blood.
Primary mechanismTransforms dampness to relax the sinews.Nourishes Blood, preserves Yin and softens the Liver to relax the sinews.
Clinical emphasisDamp painful obstruction with restricted movement; sudden turmoil disorder.Spasms, cramping and pain from malnourishment of the muscles and sinews due to Yin and Blood insufficiency.
Additional actionsHarmonizes the Spleen and treats vomiting, diarrhea and leg qi with edema.Softens the Liver and treats Yin deficiency with floating Yang and gynecological disorders.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe shared sinew indication can hide two different mechanisms: Mu Gua resolves dampness, while Bai Shao nourishes Yin and Blood and softens the Liver.
Common confusionCramping and spasms alone do not distinguish the herbs because both may be used together. Look for either damp obstruction and restricted movement or an undernourished Yin-Blood presentation.
How it may appearJoint restriction, damp painful obstruction, vomiting or diarrhea favors Mu Gua. Blood or Yin insufficiency, gynecological involvement and cramping from poor nourishment favor Bai Shao.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 333. Source Exam Key CMID 9023, questions 1–4.

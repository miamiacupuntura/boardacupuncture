Si Ni San vs. Xiao Yao San vs. Yue Ju Wan

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueSi Ni SanXiao Yao SanYue Ju Wan
Shared clueAll regulate constrained Qi and may treat emotional depression, chest/rib-side fullness or digestive discomfort.
Fast distinctionQi constraint blocks Yang from reaching the extremities: cold hands and feet with wiry pulse.Liver-Qi constraint with Spleen-Qi and Blood deficiency: fatigue, poor appetite and menstrual signs.Qi stagnation generates the six stagnations—Qi, Blood, Phlegm, Fire, Dampness and Food.

Comparative answer key

CompareSi Ni SanXiao Yao SanYue Ju Wan
Root patternConstrained Yang dynamics.Liver-Spleen disharmony with deficiency.Multiple stagnations, beginning with Qi.
Decisive clueCold extremities without true Yang collapse.Constraint plus fatigue, pallor or poor appetite.Mixed signs of several stagnations.
Primary emphasisSpreads Qi and restores Yang movement.Courses Liver, nourishes Blood and strengthens Spleen.Moves Qi broadly to release accumulated constraint.
Exam shortcutConstraint makes the hands cold.Constraint in a deficient patient.Everything is stuck.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersAll move constraint, but the complication differs: blocked Yang movement, deficient Blood/Spleen Qi, or multiple secondary stagnations.
Memory cueSi Ni reaches the limbs; Xiao Yao supports; Yue Ju unblocks six.
SafetyCold extremities with fainting, weak pulse, chest pain or altered consciousness may indicate shock and requires emergency care.
SourceSource bank: FWB 9.1, Comparison Formulas to Regulate the Qi; corroborated with harmonizing materials and individual profiles.

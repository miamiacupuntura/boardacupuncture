The Seven Relationships Map

Qī Qíng (七情) describes what happens when medicinals meet inside a prescription.

One-line map

Memory orderSolo artist → twins → helper → toxic herb fears → protector suppresses → effect is weakened → dangerous combination.
Fast distinctionMore effect: Xū/Shǐ. Less toxicity: Wèi/Shā. Less effect: Wù. More risk: Fǎn.

The seven and their exam aliases

RelationshipCommon English labelsAnchor example
Dān Xíng 单行Single effect; one medicinal acts alone.Rén Shēn alone in Dú Shēn Tāng.
Xiāng Xū 相须Mutual necessity / mutual accentuation; similar actions reinforce.Shí Gāo + Zhī Mǔ.
Xiāng Shǐ 相使Mutual employment / mutual enhancement; a different-action helper assists.Huáng Qí + Fú Líng for deficiency edema.
Xiāng Wèi 相畏Mutual fear / mutual counteraction; the toxic herb is tamed.Bàn Xià “fears” Shēng Jiāng.
Xiāng Shā 相杀Mutual killing / mutual suppression; the protector suppresses toxicity.Shēng Jiāng suppresses Bàn Xià toxicity.
Xiāng Wù 相恶Mutual aversion / mutual antagonism; one herb weakens another’s effect.Lái Fú Zǐ weakens the Qi-tonifying emphasis of Rén Shēn.
Xiāng Fǎn 相反Mutual opposition / mutual incompatibility; the combination raises harm.The Shí Bā Fǎn groups.

Safety frame

These are historical classification terms and required exam knowledge. They do not replace exact species identification, processing, dose, medication reconciliation or current toxicology guidance.

The Seven Relationships Map

Qī Qíng (七情) describes what happens when medicinals meet inside a prescription.

One-line map

Memory orderSolo artist → twins → helper → toxic herb fears → protector suppresses → effect is weakened → dangerous combination.
Fast distinctionMore effect: Xū/Shǐ. Less toxicity: Wèi/Shā. Less effect: Wù. More risk: Fǎn.

The seven

RelationshipMeaningAnchor example
Dān Xíng 单行Single medicinal acts alone.Rén Shēn alone in Dú Shēn Tāng.
Xiāng Xū 相须Similar actions reinforce one another.Shí Gāo + Zhī Mǔ.
Xiāng Shǐ 相使A different-action helper strengthens the principal action.Huáng Qí + Fú Líng for deficiency edema.
Xiāng Wèi 相畏The toxic/harsh herb has its toxicity reduced.Bàn Xià “fears” Shēng Jiāng.
Xiāng Shā 相杀The protector suppresses the other herb’s toxicity.Shēng Jiāng suppresses Bàn Xià toxicity.
Xiāng Wù 相恶One herb weakens another’s therapeutic effect.Lái Fú Zǐ weakens the Qi-tonifying emphasis of Rén Shēn.
Xiāng Fǎn 相反The combination is traditionally regarded as toxic or seriously harmful.The Shí Bā Fǎn groups.

Safety frame

These are historical classification terms and required exam knowledge. They do not replace exact species identification, processing, dose, medication reconciliation or current toxicology guidance.

Herb comparison

Chuanxiong Rhizoma (chuan xiong) vs. Salviae miltiorrhizae Radix (dan shen)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueChuan XiongDan Shen
Shared cluesBoth invigorate Blood, remove stagnation, stop pain, and enter the Liver and Pericardium.
Fast distinctionWarm, acrid, light and Qi-moving; reaches exterior for Wind and headache.Cooling and bitter; cools Blood, clears Heat and calms Heat-related irritability.

Comparative answer key

CompareChuan XiongDan Shen
Shared categoryHerbs that Regulate and Invigorate the Blood.
Properties/channelsWarm and acrid; Liver, Gallbladder and Pericardium.Slightly cold and bitter; Heart, Pericardium and Liver.
MovementLight, dispersing and unblocking; has more effect on Qi in the Blood and reaches the exterior to expel Wind.Invigorates and cools Blood, removes stagnation and soothes irritability caused by Heat.
Clinical emphasisMenstrual/postpartum stasis, Wind-Cold headache and Wind-Damp painful obstruction.Menstrual, postpartum, cardiac or general stasis pain; palpitations, irritability and insomnia; Ying-level Heat with macules or boils.
Formula cluesTao Hong Si Wu Tang; Sheng Hua Tang; Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang; Chuan Xiong Cha Tiao San.Dan Shen Yin; Tian Wang Bu Xin Dan; Qing Ying Tang.
Compatibility notes in sourceAntagonizes Shan Zhu Yu and Huang Qi; counteracts Hua Shi and Huang Lian; incompatible with Li Lu.Do not use with Li Lu.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth move Blood and stop pain, but temperature, Qi movement, exterior action and Heat-calming effects separate them.
Common confusionWarm, light, Qi-moving and Wind/headache clues favor Chuan Xiong; cooling Blood, Heart symptoms or Ying Heat favors Dan Shen.
Dose/preparation in sourceChuan Xiong: 3–10 g, powder 1–1.5 g, tincture 1–3 ml. Dan Shen: 3–15 g, with larger ranges and wine-frying notes also listed by the source. These are exam-source facts, not prescribing guidance.
Source question formatsQuestions 8–13 are Matching.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 603. Source Exam Key CMID 8921.

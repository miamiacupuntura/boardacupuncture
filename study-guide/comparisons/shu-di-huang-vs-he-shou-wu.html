Herb comparison

Rehmanniae Radix preparata (shu di huang) vs. Polygoni multiflori Radix preparata (he shou wu)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueShu Di HuangHe Shou Wu
Shared clueBoth nourish Blood, enrich Yin, tonify Liver and augment Kidneys. Both are slightly warm and sweet and enter Liver and Kidney.
Fast distinctionBlack, stronger and more cloying; primarily Kidney, Essence, marrow and true Yin.Reddish brown, gentler and easier to digest; primarily Liver, nutritive Blood and hair.

Comparative answer key

CompareShu Di HuangHe Shou Wu
Medicinal nameRehmanniae Radix preparata.Polygoni multiflori Radix preparata.
Scientific nameRehmannia glutinosa.Polygonum multiflorum.
Part usedPrepared dried root tuber.Dried prepared root.
CategoryHerbs that Tonify the Blood.Herbs that Tonify the Blood.
Properties and channelsSlightly warm and sweet; Liver, Kidney and Heart.Slightly warm, bitter, sweet and astringent; Liver and Kidney.
Color and primary emphasisBlack; primarily enters Kidney to augment Essence and marrow and strongly tonify true Yin; secondarily tonifies Blood and nourishes Liver.Reddish brown; primarily enters Liver to regulate and tonify nutritive Blood; secondarily enriches Kidney Yin and secures Essence.
Relative strength and digestionStronger, but greasy, cloying and difficult to digest.Not as strongly tonifying, but less greasy and easier to digest.
Blood and Yin indicationsPale complexion, dizziness, insomnia, palpitations, irregular menstruation or bleeding; Five-Heart Heat, steaming bones and night sweats.Gray hair, dizziness and weak vision; skin problems from Fire toxin or Blood deficiency; constipation from Blood deficiency.
Essence clueWeak lower back and legs, tinnitus and gray hair.Secures Essence for vaginal discharge or spermatorrhea.
Cautions in sourceSpleen-Stomach deficiency, stagnant Qi and Phlegm. The source lists diarrhea, abdominal pain, dizziness, low energy and palpitations as possible mild effects.Spleen deficiency, diarrhea and Phlegm; the source says not to combine with onion, chives or garlic and not to decoct in a steel container.
Key formulasBa Zhen Tang; Bai He Gu Jin Tang; Bu Fei Tang; Da Bu Yin Wan; Dang Gui Liu Huang Tang; Di Huang Yin Zi; Hu Qian Wan; Liu Wei Di Huang Wan; Shi Quan Da Bu Tang; Si Wu Tang; Yang He Tang; You Gui Wan; Yu Nu Jian; Zan Yu Dan; Zhu Jing Wan; Zuo Gui Yin.He Ren Yin; Qi Bao Mei Ran Dan.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth treat deep Liver-Kidney and Blood-Yin deficiency, but powerful Kidney-Essence and marrow tonification favors Shu Di Huang; Liver-Blood, hair, Essence securing and easier digestion favor He Shou Wu.
Memory cueBlack Shu Di goes deep to Kidney and marrow; red-brown He Shou Wu feeds Liver Blood and hair.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 746. Source Exam Key CMID 8975, questions 1–6, and CMID 9070, questions 1–6.

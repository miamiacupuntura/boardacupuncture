Lung Qi Deficiency vs. Wind-Cold Invasion

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueLung Qi DeficiencyWind-Cold Invasion
Shared clueBoth can cause cough, shortness of breath, aversion to cold and abnormal sweating, and deficiency can predispose to invasion.
Fast distinctionChronic weakness: low voice, fatigue, spontaneous sweating and recurrent colds.Acute onset: chills, headache, body aches, nasal congestion and a floating pulse.

Comparative answer key

CompareLung Qi DeficiencyWind-Cold Invasion
NatureInternal deficiency/root.External excess/branch.
TimingChronic or recurrent.Acute.
PulseWeak, especially right cun.Floating-tight or floating-moderate by subtype.
Formula examplesYu Ping Feng San for Defensive-Qi deficiency.Ma Huang Tang or Gui Zhi Tang by sweat pattern.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersA weak root and an acute branch may coexist, but the exam asks whether the current presentation is chronic deficiency or active invasion.
Memory cueDeficiency keeps recurring; invasion begins suddenly.
SafetyBreathing difficulty, chest pain, high fever, confusion or low oxygen requires urgent medical assessment.

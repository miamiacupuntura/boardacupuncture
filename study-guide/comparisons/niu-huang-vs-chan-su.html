Herb comparison

Bovis Calculus (niu huang) vs. Bufonis Venenum (chan su)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueNiu HuangChan Su
Shared cluesBoth resolve toxicity, reduce swelling, open the orifices and awaken consciousness.
Fast distinctionPowerfully cooling; clears Heat and dislodges Phlegm for febrile disorders or Wind-Stroke with impaired consciousness.Acrid, warm and dispersing; reduces painful toxic swelling and clears turbid filth. It is highly toxic.

Comparative answer key

CompareNiu Huang (Bovis Calculus)Chan Su (Bufonis Venenum)
CategoryAromatic Substances that Open the Orifices.Substances for External Application; highly toxic.
Shared actionsResolve toxicity, reduce swelling, open the orifices and awaken consciousness.
Nature and methodPowerfully cooling and aromatic; clears Heat, resolves toxicity and dislodges Phlegm.Acrid, warm, mobilizing and dispersing; clears turbid filth, reduces swelling and stops pain.
Consciousness clueMuddled consciousness from febrile disease or Wind-Stroke.Fainting from Summerheat or turbid filth with vomiting, diarrhea and abdominal pain.
Swelling clueHeat-toxin with strong Heat signs.Painful sores and toxic swellings.
Critical cautionUse according to professional Materia Medica guidance.Highly toxic; internal use requires expert professional control, is contraindicated in pregnancy, and contact with the eyes must be avoided.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth open the orifices and resolve toxicity, but strong cooling of Heat-Phlegm favors Niu Huang, whereas warm dispersal of turbid filth with painful swelling favors Chan Su.
Memory cueNiu Huang cools Heat and opens; Chan Su disperses filth and swelling—but is highly toxic.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 1037. Source Exam Key CMID 9024, question 2.

Theory 2 of 12

The Seven Herb Relationships

Differentiate reinforcement, assistance, toxicity control, antagonism and single use.

Core rule

RuleThe seven relationships describe what happens between ingredients; they do not replace diagnosis or modern safety review.

Beneficial or usable relationships

ConceptWhat it meansApplication
Mutual accentuation · Xiang XuTwo herbs with similar functions reinforce a shared therapeutic effect.Example: Jing Jie + Fang Feng to release Wind from the Exterior.
Mutual enhancement · Xiang ShiOne herb treats the main problem while another with a different action assists it.Example: Zhi Mu + Huang Bai for deficiency Heat in the Lower Jiao.
Mutual counteraction · Xiang WeiOne ingredient has its toxicity or harshness reduced by another.Example: processed Ban Xia is traditionally paired with Sheng Jiang.
Mutual suppression · Xiang ShaOne ingredient actively reduces toxicity or an adverse effect of another.Classify by which herb performs the suppressing action.
Single effect · Dan XingA balanced herb may occasionally be used alone for a simple or urgent strategy.Single-herb use is the exception, not proof that formulas are unnecessary.

Relationships to avoid

ConceptWhat it meansApplication
Mutual antagonism · Xiang WuOne herb weakens the therapeutic action of another.Classic example: Lai Fu Zi may reduce the Qi-tonifying emphasis of Ren Shen.
Mutual incompatibility · Xiang FanThe traditional combination is regarded as producing toxicity or serious harm.Treat traditional incompatibility lists as mandatory exam knowledge and add modern interaction review.
Decisive distinctionWei/Sha describes mitigation; Wu describes reduced efficacy; Fan describes harmful incompatibility.Do not collapse the three into “they do not work together.”

Responsible use

Educational summary for formula analysis and exam preparation. Doses, toxic preparations, interactions and clinical decisions require current references and professional supervision.

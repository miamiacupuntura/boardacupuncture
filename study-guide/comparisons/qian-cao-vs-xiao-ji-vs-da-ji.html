Herb comparison

Rubiae Radix (qian cao) vs. Cirsii Herba (xiao ji) vs. Cirsii japonici Herba sive Radix (da ji)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueQian CaoXiao JiDa Ji
Shared cluesAll three enter the Blood, cool Heat, stop bleeding and dispel Blood stasis.
Fast distinctionGynecological bleeding and rebellious cough.Urination and bleeding with painful urinary dribbling.Clumps and sores, especially when fresh.

Comparative answer key

CompareQian CaoXiao JiDa Ji
Shared categoryHerbs that Regulate the Blood · Herbs that Stop Bleeding.
Primary emphasisVaginal bleeding due to Heat.Facilitates urination.Disperses clumps and reduces sores.
Distinctive indicationAlso enters the Lungs for rebellious cough from the Liver accosting the Lungs.Bleeding in painful urinary dribbling.Most distinctive when used fresh for clumps and sores.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe shared Blood-cooling and hemostatic actions make the organ system and symptom location the fastest differentiators.
Common confusionGynecology or cough points to Qian Cao; urinary bleeding points to Xiao Ji; clumps and sores point to Da Ji.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 566. Source Exam Keys CMID 8940; CMID 9038, question 1; and CMID 9099, question 1.

Damp-Heat by Location: Liver–Gallbladder vs. Bladder vs. Large Intestine

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueLiver–Gallbladder Damp-HeatBladder Damp-HeatLarge-Intestine Damp-Heat
Shared clueAll show Heat plus Damp obstruction: heaviness/fullness, yellow greasy coat and a slippery rapid or wiry-rapid pulse.
Fast distinctionBile/genital route: jaundice, bitter taste, rib-side pain, genital itching/swelling or yellow discharge.Urinary route: urgency, frequency, burning painful scant dark urine or blood/sand in urine.Stool route: foul urgent diarrhea or dysentery with mucus/blood, abdominal pain, burning anus and tenesmus.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe thermal and tongue signs overlap. Follow what the body is failing to excrete normally: bile/genital fluids, urine or stool.
Memory cueYellow body/genitals—Liver-GB; burning urine—Bladder; burning stool—Large Intestine.
SafetyJaundice, fever with flank pain, inability to urinate, pregnancy urinary symptoms, bloody diarrhea, severe dehydration or rigid abdominal pain requires urgent medical evaluation.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

  • Which location causes jaundice?
  • Which causes burning urination?
  • Which causes tenesmus with mucus-blood stool?

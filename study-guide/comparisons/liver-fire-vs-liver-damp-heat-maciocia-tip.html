Liver Fire Blazing vs. Damp-Heat in the Liver

Separate hot upward flaring from Heat weighted down by Dampness and digestive or genital symptoms.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueLiver Fire BlazingLiver Damp-Heat
SharedBoth may cause irritability, bitter taste, red tongue sides, hypochondriac discomfort and a wiry-rapid pulse.
Fast distinctionFire rises: red face/eyes, temporal headache, tinnitus, anger and dry constipation.Damp-Heat weighs and oozes: heaviness, nausea, jaundice, sticky taste, genital itching/discharge and a greasy yellow coat.

Clinical table

CompareLiver Fire BlazingLiver Damp-Heat
Texture/directionDry, intense and upward.Heavy, sticky and concentrated in Liver–Gallbladder/lower burner.
Tongue / pulseRed sides, dry yellow coat; wiry-rapid-forceful.Red with greasy yellow coat; wiry-slippery-rapid.
Formula associationLong Dan Xie Gan Tang by excess presentation.Long Dan Xie Gan Tang may also appear, but Dampness signs determine the strategy.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersThe same formula can appear in both; greasy coating, heaviness and discharges prove Dampness.
Memory cueFire flares up; Damp-Heat sticks and sinks.
SafetyJaundice, severe right-upper abdominal pain, fever, eye pain or sudden hearing loss needs urgent evaluation.

Review prompts

  • Which has red eyes/headache?
  • Which has greasy coating?
  • Why can formula name alone not decide?

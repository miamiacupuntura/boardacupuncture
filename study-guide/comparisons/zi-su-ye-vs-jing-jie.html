Herb comparison

Zi Su Ye vs. Jing Jie

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueZi Su YeJing Jie
Shared clueBoth are warm, acrid, Exterior-releasing herbs that induce sweating.
Fast distinctionChoose when Wind-Cold occurs with middle-burner Qi stagnation, nausea, vomiting or pregnancy.Choose for stronger Wind dispersal, Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat, and rashes or itching from Wind in the Blood.

Comparative answer key

CompareZi Su YeJing Jie
Botanical namePerillae Folium.Schizonepetae Herba.
CategoryWarm, Acrid Herbs that Release the Exterior.Warm, Acrid Herbs that Release the Exterior.
PropertiesWarm, acrid and aromatic.Acrid and slightly warm.
ChannelsLung and Spleen.Lung and Liver.
Exterior actionDisperses Wind-Cold.Stronger at dispelling Wind and can treat either Wind-Cold or Wind-Heat.
Distinctive usesMoves Qi in the chest and Stomach; treats nausea, vomiting, morning sickness and seafood poisoning.Disperses Wind from the Blood for skin eruptions and itching; the charred herb stops bleeding.
Dose and preparation3–9 g; do not decoct for a long time.3–9 g.
Part usedDried leaf, sometimes with young branches.Aerial portions and flowers; the spikes alone are called Jing Jie Sui.
Formula cluesXing Su San and Xiang Su San.Yin Qiao San and Huai Hua San.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe herbs overlap in releasing the Exterior, but Zi Su Ye adds middle-burner Qi regulation while Jing Jie has broader Wind use and a stronger Blood-level skin emphasis.
Memory cueZi Su Ye settles the Stomach; Jing Jie vents the skin.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 16. Source Exam Key CMID 8994, questions 1–5.

Herb comparison

Ostreae Concha (mu li) vs. Trionycis Carapax (bie jia)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueMu LiBie Jia
Shared cluesBoth are heavy, salty substances that benefit Yin, anchor or subdue Yang and soften areas of hardness.
Fast distinctionMore strongly anchors Yang, secures leakage and quiets the Spirit.More strongly nourishes Yin and cools at a deeper level for steaming bones and night sweats.

Comparative answer key

CompareMu Li (Ostreae Concha)Bie Jia (Trionycis Carapax)
CategorySubstances that Anchor, Settle, and Calm the Spirit.Herbs that Tonify the Yin.
Shared actionsBenefit Yin, anchor or subdue Yang and soften hardness.
Relative strengthMore strongly anchors and subdues Yang.More strongly nourishes Yin and cools at a deeper level.
Astringent actionSecures and prevents leakage of sweat, seminal emissions and vaginal discharge.The source comparison emphasizes deep Yin enrichment rather than Mu Li's astringent binding.
Spirit and sweating clueQuiets the Spirit and treats sweating through astringent inhibition supported by its cool nature.Treats steaming bones and night sweats by strongly enriching Yin.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth shells support Yin and subdue Yang, but leakage and Spirit clues favor Mu Li, whereas deeper Yin deficiency with steaming bones favors Bie Jia.
Memory cueMu Li anchors and binds; Bie Jia deeply enriches Yin.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 846. Source Exam Key CMID 9019, question 1.

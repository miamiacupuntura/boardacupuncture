Preparation & Decoction

Processing and timing can change direction, potency and toxicity.

Core rule

RuleConfirm identity and required processing before deciding how the substance enters the formula.

Processing changes the medicinal

Group / stepWhat to knowSafe decision
Aconite: Fù Zǐ / Chuān Wū / Cǎo WūPrepared product only; requires qualified prescribing and prolonged pre-decoction according to the authenticated product and applicable standard.Raw or improperly prepared aconite can cause fatal arrhythmia and neurologic toxicity.
Bàn Xià / Tiān Nán XīngUse the specified processed form; processing reduces acrid irritant toxicity.Raw material is not an interchangeable substitute.
Shēng vs. Gān vs. Pào JiāngFresh ginger releases Exterior/stops vomiting; dried warms Interior; blast-fried emphasizes warm hemostasis.The processing word is part of the prescription.
Shēng Dì vs. Shú DìRaw-dried Rehmannia cools Blood/nourishes Yin; prepared Rehmannia tonifies Blood/Essence.Do not collapse raw and prepared forms.
Charred herbsCharring often redirects an herb toward stopping bleeding, but the endpoint and product identity matter.Burnt or uncontrolled homemade material is not standardized Páo Zhì.
Honey-fried herbsHoney-frying may increase moistening or tonic emphasis, as with Zhì Gān Cǎo or honey-fried Sāng Yè.Account for sugars and the exact prepared form.

Processing purposes and methods

PurposePrincipleExamples from the chart
Increase or redirect an effectTraditional processing may emphasize, moderate or redirect an action; it does not universally make every medicinal “stronger.”Wine-processed Dang Gui emphasizes moving Blood; vinegar-processed Yan Hu Suo emphasizes Qi/Blood movement and pain relief; honey-prepared Kuan Dong Hua emphasizes moistening the Lung and stopping cough.
Reduce toxicity or irritationProcessing can reduce irritating or toxic constituents, but only when the authenticated method and finished product are correct.Ban Xia is used in a specified processed form; ginger processing may emphasize harmonizing the Stomach and stopping vomiting.
Alter therapeutic propertiesPreparation can materially change temperature, direction and clinical emphasis.Sheng Di Huang is cooling and nourishes Yin; prepared Shu Di Huang is richer, warmer and emphasizes Blood/Essence tonification.
Improve handling and storageDrying, cutting, crushing and standardized heating can improve stability and extraction.Processing instructions remain part of the medicinal identity and cannot be replaced by improvised home preparation.
MethodTypical purposeHigh-yield example or limit
MechanicalCut, crush, grind or remove a nonmedicinal part.Particle size changes extraction and therefore exposure.
Water methodsWash, soak, moisten or levigate when specified.Excess washing can discard active material; follow the authenticated standard.
Dry-frying · ChaoDry, brown or scorch to modify digestion, storage or clinical emphasis.Charred processing is controlled Pao Zhi, not simply burning an herb.
Heat plus waterSteam, boil or otherwise prepare with moist heat.Shu Di Huang is a prepared Rehmannia product, not an interchangeable synonym for Sheng Di Huang.
HoneyTraditionally emphasizes tonifying, moistening or cough-relieving actions.Kuan Dong Hua and Zhi Gan Cao are common study examples; consider sugar exposure.
VinegarTraditionally emphasizes Liver affinity, Qi/Blood movement and pain relief.Yan Hu Suo is classically vinegar-processed, not salt-processed.
WineTraditionally emphasizes Blood movement, channel access or upward movement.Dang Gui is a standard examination example.
Ginger juiceTraditionally moderates cold/irritating qualities and supports the Stomach.Processed Ban Xia is the safety-relevant example; raw Ban Xia is not substituted.
Salt waterTraditionally directs action toward the Kidney and lower body.Salt processing belongs to liquid-excipient processing, not dry-frying.
Corrections to the supplied chartYan Hu Suo is classically vinegar-processed, not salt-processed. Salt belongs under liquid-excipient processing. Fu Ling coated with Zhu Sha is a historical reference only: Zhu Sha contains mercury and is excluded from current safe study recommendations.

Decoction timing

Group / stepWhat to knowSafe decision
Pre-decoctDense minerals/shells and specified toxic roots may need to begin earlier.Timing is substance-specific; “pre-decoct everything hard” is not a complete rule.
Add lateAromatic herbs and volatile medicinals such as Gōu Téng or Qīng Hāo may be added near the end when specified.Long boiling can reduce volatile or heat-sensitive activity.
Dissolve separatelyGelatins such as Ē Jiāo or Lù Jiǎo Jiāo are melted into the strained hot decoction.Direct boiling can scorch or clump the product.
Powder/pill rather than decoctionSome substances such as Qīng Dài or Lù Róng are traditionally administered in powder/pill form.Route and dose must follow a qualified prescription.
Crush before decoctionSome seeds or fruits release constituents better when cracked or crushed.Crushing also increases extraction and can increase toxicity; verify instructions.
Wrap before decoctionFine hairs, powders or floating materials may require a cloth bag.Prevents throat irritation and difficult straining.

When to act immediately

EmergencyNever attempt home detoxification of aconite or other toxic medicinals. Numb mouth, spreading weakness, vomiting, faintness, palpitations or an irregular pulse requires emergency care/poison control.

Educational use: this library does not replace medical evaluation, poison control, local regulation or pharmacist consultation.

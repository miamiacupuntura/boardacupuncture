Preparation & Decoction

Processing and timing can change direction, potency and toxicity.

Core rule

Rule
DetailsConfirm identity and required processing before deciding how the substance enters the formula.

Processing changes the medicinal

Aconite: Fù Zǐ / Chuān Wū / Cǎo Wū
What to knowPrepared product only; requires qualified prescribing and prolonged pre-decoction according to the authenticated product and applicable standard.
Safe decisionRaw or improperly prepared aconite can cause fatal arrhythmia and neurologic toxicity.
Bàn Xià / Tiān Nán Xīng
What to knowUse the specified processed form; processing reduces acrid irritant toxicity.
Safe decisionRaw material is not an interchangeable substitute.
Shēng vs. Gān vs. Pào Jiāng
What to knowFresh ginger releases Exterior/stops vomiting; dried warms Interior; blast-fried emphasizes warm hemostasis.
Safe decisionThe processing word is part of the prescription.
Shēng Dì vs. Shú Dì
What to knowRaw-dried Rehmannia cools Blood/nourishes Yin; prepared Rehmannia tonifies Blood/Essence.
Safe decisionDo not collapse raw and prepared forms.
Charred herbs
What to knowCharring often redirects an herb toward stopping bleeding, but the endpoint and product identity matter.
Safe decisionBurnt or uncontrolled homemade material is not standardized Páo Zhì.
Honey-fried herbs
What to knowHoney-frying may increase moistening or tonic emphasis, as with Zhì Gān Cǎo or honey-fried Sāng Yè.
Safe decisionAccount for sugars and the exact prepared form.

Processing purposes and methods

Purpose

Increase or redirect an effect
PrincipleTraditional processing may emphasize, moderate or redirect an action; it does not universally make every medicinal “stronger.”
Examples from the chartWine-processed Dang Gui emphasizes moving Blood; vinegar-processed Yan Hu Suo emphasizes Qi/Blood movement and pain relief; honey-prepared Kuan Dong Hua emphasizes moistening the Lung and stopping cough.
Reduce toxicity or irritation
PrincipleProcessing can reduce irritating or toxic constituents, but only when the authenticated method and finished product are correct.
Examples from the chartBan Xia is used in a specified processed form; ginger processing may emphasize harmonizing the Stomach and stopping vomiting.
Alter therapeutic properties
PrinciplePreparation can materially change temperature, direction and clinical emphasis.
Examples from the chartSheng Di Huang is cooling and nourishes Yin; prepared Shu Di Huang is richer, warmer and emphasizes Blood/Essence tonification.
Improve handling and storage
PrincipleDrying, cutting, crushing and standardized heating can improve stability and extraction.
Examples from the chartProcessing instructions remain part of the medicinal identity and cannot be replaced by improvised home preparation.

Method

Mechanical
Typical purposeCut, crush, grind or remove a nonmedicinal part.
High-yield example or limitParticle size changes extraction and therefore exposure.
Water methods
Typical purposeWash, soak, moisten or levigate when specified.
High-yield example or limitExcess washing can discard active material; follow the authenticated standard.
Dry-frying · Chao
Typical purposeDry, brown or scorch to modify digestion, storage or clinical emphasis.
High-yield example or limitCharred processing is controlled Pao Zhi, not simply burning an herb.
Heat plus water
Typical purposeSteam, boil or otherwise prepare with moist heat.
High-yield example or limitShu Di Huang is a prepared Rehmannia product, not an interchangeable synonym for Sheng Di Huang.
Honey
Typical purposeTraditionally emphasizes tonifying, moistening or cough-relieving actions.
High-yield example or limitKuan Dong Hua and Zhi Gan Cao are common study examples; consider sugar exposure.
Vinegar
Typical purposeTraditionally emphasizes Liver affinity, Qi/Blood movement and pain relief.
High-yield example or limitYan Hu Suo is classically vinegar-processed, not salt-processed.
Wine
Typical purposeTraditionally emphasizes Blood movement, channel access or upward movement.
High-yield example or limitDang Gui is a standard examination example.
Ginger juice
Typical purposeTraditionally moderates cold/irritating qualities and supports the Stomach.
High-yield example or limitProcessed Ban Xia is the safety-relevant example; raw Ban Xia is not substituted.
Salt water
Typical purposeTraditionally directs action toward the Kidney and lower body.
High-yield example or limitSalt processing belongs to liquid-excipient processing, not dry-frying.
Corrections to the supplied chart
DetailsYan Hu Suo is classically vinegar-processed, not salt-processed. Salt belongs under liquid-excipient processing. Fu Ling coated with Zhu Sha is a historical reference only: Zhu Sha contains mercury and is excluded from current safe study recommendations.

Decoction timing

Pre-decoct
What to knowDense minerals/shells and specified toxic roots may need to begin earlier.
Safe decisionTiming is substance-specific; “pre-decoct everything hard” is not a complete rule.
Add late
What to knowAromatic herbs and volatile medicinals such as Gōu Téng or Qīng Hāo may be added near the end when specified.
Safe decisionLong boiling can reduce volatile or heat-sensitive activity.
Dissolve separately
What to knowGelatins such as Ē Jiāo or Lù Jiǎo Jiāo are melted into the strained hot decoction.
Safe decisionDirect boiling can scorch or clump the product.
Powder/pill rather than decoction
What to knowSome substances such as Qīng Dài or Lù Róng are traditionally administered in powder/pill form.
Safe decisionRoute and dose must follow a qualified prescription.
Crush before decoction
What to knowSome seeds or fruits release constituents better when cracked or crushed.
Safe decisionCrushing also increases extraction and can increase toxicity; verify instructions.
Wrap before decoction
What to knowFine hairs, powders or floating materials may require a cloth bag.
Safe decisionPrevents throat irritation and difficult straining.

When to act immediately

Emergency
DetailsNever attempt home detoxification of aconite or other toxic medicinals. Numb mouth, spreading weakness, vomiting, faintness, palpitations or an irregular pulse requires emergency care/poison control.

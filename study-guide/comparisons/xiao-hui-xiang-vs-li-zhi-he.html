Herb comparison

Foeniculi Fructus (xiao hui xiang) vs. Litchi Semen (li zhi he)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueXiao Hui XiangLi Zhi He
Shared actionsBoth warm the Liver, dispel lower-burner Cold and relieve pain in Cold-Damp testicular swelling or lower-abdominal disorders.
Fast distinctionWarms Liver and Kidneys and harmonizes the Stomach; look for Stomach Cold with indigestion, reduced appetite or vomiting.Regulates Liver Qi and relieves pain from Qi or Qi-Blood stagnation; look for groin, abdominal, menstrual or postpartum pain.

Comparative answer key

CompareXiao Hui XiangLi Zhi He
Medicinal nameFoeniculi Fructus.Litchi Semen.
CategoryHerbs that Warm the Interior and Expel Cold.Herbs that Regulate the Qi.
Nature, taste and channelsWarm and acrid; Liver, Kidney, Spleen and Stomach.Warm, sweet, acrid and astringent; Liver and Stomach.
Main actionWarms Liver and Kidneys, expels Cold, relieves pain, regulates Qi and harmonizes the Stomach.Disperses Cold and stagnation, regulates Qi and stops pain.
Lower-burner clueCold invading the Liver channel with lower-abdominal pain or Cold bulging disorders.Liver-channel Cold with painful groin or testicular swelling; also pain from Liver-Qi or Qi-Blood stagnation.
Distinctive digestive or gynecologic clueStomach Cold with abdominal pain, indigestion, poor appetite or vomiting.Abdominal, epigastric, premenstrual or postpartum pain from stagnation.
Formula cluesTian Tai Wu Yao San and Nuan Gan Jian.Nuan Gan Jian, modified for scrotal pain.
Caution clueAvoid in Yin deficiency with Heat or Stomach Fire; use caution during pregnancy.Use for patterns associated with Damp-Cold.
Interaction clue in the sourceMay affect the absorption rate of ciprofloxacin.May add to the glucose-lowering effect of insulin or other antidiabetic medicines.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe shared lower-burner Cold-pain pattern can look identical. Stomach harmonization favors Xiao Hui Xiang; broader Liver-Qi and Qi-Blood stagnation pain favors Li Zhi He.
Memory cueXiao Hui Xiang warms and harmonizes; Li Zhi He moves Liver Qi and stops stagnation pain.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 547. Source Exam Key CMID 9098, questions 1–7.

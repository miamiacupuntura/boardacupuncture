Herb comparison

Stephaniae tetrandrae Radix (han fang ji) vs. Akebiae Caulis (mu tong)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueHan Fang JiMu Tong
Shared cluesBoth are very bitter and cold, move downward, clear Heat, facilitate fluid pathways and treat Damp-Heat edema, urinary difficulty and Wind-Damp painful obstruction.
Fast distinctionRemoves pathogenic Water and Wind; edema, wheezing and painful obstruction.Clears Heart/Small Intestine Fire and moves Blood; oral ulcers, painful urination and amenorrhea.

Comparative answer key

CompareHan Fang Ji (Stephaniae tetrandrae Radix)Mu Tong (Akebiae Caulis)
Shared categoryHerbs that Drain Dampness.
Shared actionsStrongly clear Heat, facilitate fluid pathways and open the orifices with a downward movement.
Primary strengthFacilitates removal of pathogenic Water and expels pathogenic Wind.Clears Fire from Heart/Small Intestine and promotes Blood flow in the vessels.
Clinical emphasisEdema with abdominal distention, wheezing from phlegm/thin mucus, and Wind-Damp painful obstruction.Mouth/tongue ulcers, painful difficult urination from Heart/Small Intestine Fire, and amenorrhea from Blood stasis.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe herbs overlap strongly in Damp-Heat, fluid and painful-obstruction presentations; the associated Water/Wind or Heart/Blood clue separates them.
Common confusionEdema plus wheezing or Wind-Damp Bi favors Han Fang Ji. Oral ulcers, Heart Fire or amenorrhea favors Mu Tong.
How it may appear“Expels pathogenic Wind” selects Han Fang Ji; “promotes Blood in the vessels” selects Mu Tong.
Source question formatsQuestions 4 and 5 are Matching.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 314. Source Exam Key CMID 8919.

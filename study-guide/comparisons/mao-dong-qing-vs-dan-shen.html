Herb comparison

Ilicis pubescentis Radix (mao dong qing) vs. Salviae miltiorrhizae Radix (dan shen)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueMao Dong QingDan Shen
Shared cluesBoth invigorate and cool the Blood and belong to Herbs that Regulate and Invigorate the Blood.
Fast distinctionHeart-Lung emphasis with stronger toxicity resolution, Phlegm transformation and cough stopping.Heart-Pericardium-Liver Blood-level emphasis with stronger gynecological relevance and irritability reduction.

Comparative answer key

CompareMao Dong Qing (Ilicis pubescentis Radix)Dan Shen (Salviae miltiorrhizae Radix)
Shared categoryHerbs that Regulate the Blood · Herbs that Invigorate the Blood.
Channels or levelsEnters the Heart and Lungs.Enters the Blood levels of the Heart, Pericardium and Liver.
Properties emphasizedAcrid and dispersing, as well as bitter and draining.Bitter and slightly cold.
Shared Blood actionInvigorates and cools Blood.Invigorates Blood, transforms stasis, tonifies and cools Blood.
Distinctive actionsMore strongly resolves toxicity; cools the Lungs, transforms Phlegm and stops cough.Reduces swelling and eliminates irritability; its Liver-channel action makes it more appropriate for gynecological use.
Historical or compatibility clueRecent addition to the pharmacopoeia, first noted in the twentieth century.Do not use with Li Lu according to the source.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth cool and invigorate Blood, so the Lung-Phlegm-toxicity versus Liver-gynecology distinction provides the answer.
Common confusionCough, Phlegm or stronger toxicity resolution favors Mao Dong Qing; gynecological Blood stasis or Heat-related irritability favors Dan Shen.
Safety caution in sourceThe source warns that concurrent use with anticoagulants such as heparin, warfarin or enoxaparin, or antiplatelet drugs such as aspirin, dipyridamole or clopidogrel, may have an additive or synergistic effect. This is an exam-source fact, not prescribing guidance; medication decisions require a qualified clinician.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 665. Source Exam Key CMID 8944.

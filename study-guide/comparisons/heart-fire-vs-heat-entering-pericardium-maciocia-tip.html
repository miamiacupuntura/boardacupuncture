Heart Fire Blazing vs. Heat Entering the Pericardium

Separate a zang-fu Full-Heat pattern from a severe warm-disease stage that clouds consciousness.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueHeart Fire BlazingHeat Entering the Pericardium
SharedBoth involve Heat disturbing the Heart/Spirit and may cause agitation, insomnia, thirst and a red tongue.
Fast distinctionZang-fu Fire: mouth/tongue ulcers, bitter taste, dark urine and a red tip with yellow coat while consciousness is usually intact.Warm-disease emergency: high or night fever, delirium, incoherent speech, reduced consciousness, possible convulsions and a crimson tongue.

Clinical table

CompareHeart Fire BlazingHeat Entering the Pericardium
Clinical frameInternal Full Heat of the Heart system.Severe febrile pathogen reaching the Pericardium/Spirit level.
ConsciousnessRestless but generally oriented.Delirious, obtunded or comatose.
Formula associationXie Xin Tang or Dao Chi San by signs.Qing Gong Tang is a classical association under expert care.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersAltered consciousness and severe febrile illness move the case from ordinary Heart Fire to Pericardium Heat.
Memory cueHeart Fire burns the mouth; Pericardium Heat captures consciousness.
SafetyDelirium, seizure, stiff neck, altered consciousness or very high fever is a medical emergency.

Herb comparison

Menthae haplocalycis Herba (bo he) vs. Cicadae Periostracum (chan tui)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueBo HeChan Tui
Shared cluesBoth disperse Wind-Heat, benefit the throat and help vent rashes.
Decisive clueLiver Qi stagnation or aromatic dispersal.Spasms, convulsions or itching.

Comparative answer key

CompareBo He (Field Mint)Chan Tui (Cicada Slough)
Shared categoryCool, Acrid Herbs that Release the Exterior.
Throat and rashBenchmark Wind-Heat herb for sore throat and rash venting.Similar throat- and rash-focused exterior action.
Distinctive actionSmooths Liver Qi and relieves constraint.Adds treatment of spasms, convulsions and itching.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe shared throat and rash indications make the associated symptom decisive.
Common confusionDo not assign the neurologic or itching clue to Bo He; that distinction belongs to Chan Tui.
How it may appearRash plus itching or convulsions favors Chan Tui. Rash plus Liver Qi constraint favors Bo He.
Source question formatsQuestion 5 is Matching. Question 9 is Multiple Choice: the function unique to Chan Tui is stopping spasms and relieving itching. Questions 13–16 reinforce this in True/False review blocks.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 51. Source Exam Key CMID 9277.

Extreme Heat Generating Wind vs. Liver Fire Generating Wind

Separate an acute systemic febrile convulsion from internal Liver Fire escalating into Wind.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueExtreme Heat Generating WindLiver Fire Generating Wind
SharedBoth can produce tremor, spasm, convulsion, red tongue and a wiry-rapid pulse.
Fast distinctionAcute febrile context: very high fever, delirium, neck rigidity and generalized convulsion.Liver-Fire history: anger, temporal headache, bitter taste, red eyes, epistaxis or sudden tinnitus precedes Wind.

Clinical table

CompareExtreme Heat Generating WindLiver Fire Generating Wind
OriginSevere externally contracted/febrile Heat.Internal Liver Fire rises and stirs Wind.
Formula associationLing Jiao Gou Teng Tang is classically associated.Ling Jiao Gou Teng Tang may also appear; the preceding Liver-Fire signs identify the route.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersThe movement can look identical; the illness history identifies systemic Heat versus organ Fire.
Memory cueExtreme Heat arrives with fever; Liver Fire arrives with the Liver story.
SafetyAny first seizure, persistent seizure, high fever with confusion or stiff neck is an emergency.

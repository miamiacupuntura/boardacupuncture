Stabilize and Astringe · FWB 12.1

Si Shen Wan vs. Li Zhong Wan

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueSi Shen WanLi Zhong Wan
Shared clueBoth warm deficiency-Cold and treat chronic loose stool with abdominal pain relieved by warmth and pressure.
Fast distinctionKidney and Spleen Yang deficiency with characteristic pre-dawn or cock-crow diarrhea.Spleen-Stomach deficiency-Cold with diarrhea, vomiting and poor appetite at any time.

Comparative answer key

CompareSi Shen WanLi Zhong Wan
Primary organKidney with Spleen involvement.Spleen and Stomach.
Treatment emphasisWarms Kidney and Spleen and astringes the Intestines.Warms and strengthens the middle burner and dispels Cold.
Key ingredientsBu Gu Zhi, Wu Zhu Yu, Rou Dou Kou and Wu Wei Zi; administered with Sheng Jiang and Da Zao.Ren Shen, Gan Jiang, Bai Zhu and Zhi Gan Cao.
Decisive clueDiarrhea before dawn with low-back weakness and cold limbs.Middle-Jiao Cold with epigastric/abdominal pain, poor appetite, nausea or vomiting.
Exam shortcutAstringe the gate at dawn.Warm the center.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth can show chronic watery stool and undigested food. The timing and Kidney signs move the answer to Si Shen Wan; a middle-burner presentation favors Li Zhong Wan.
Memory cueSi Shen shuts dawn diarrhea; Li Zhong regulates the cold center.
SafetyPersistent diarrhea can cause dangerous dehydration and may reflect infection, bleeding or other disease. Fever, blood, severe pain, fainting or inability to hydrate requires prompt medical care.
SourceSource bank: FWB 12.1, Comparison Formulas to Stabilize and Astringe, activities 6–10; source wording corrected from “Si Sheng Wan” to the clinically consistent Si Shen Wan.

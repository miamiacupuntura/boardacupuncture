Herb comparison

Curcumae Radix (yu jin) vs. Corydalis Rhizoma (yan hu suo)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueYu JinYan Hu Suo
Shared cluesBoth regulate and invigorate Blood, move Qi and Blood, break stasis and stop pain.
Fast distinctionCold herb for Blood Heat, Heart Blood, Liver constraint transforming into Fire, Damp-Heat jaundice and bleeding.Warm herb that more strongly moves Qi and broadly treats pain, especially Blood-stasis pain.

Comparative answer key

CompareYu JinYan Hu Suo
Medicinal nameCurcumae Radix.Corydalis Rhizoma.
Shared categoryHerbs that Regulate the Blood · Herbs that Invigorate the Blood.
PropertiesAcrid, bitter and cold.Acrid, bitter and warm.
Primary emphasisMoves Qi within the Blood, cools Heart Blood, breaks stasis and stops pain; also relieves Liver constraint.More strongly promotes the flow of Qi and stops pain; may treat many pain patterns when combined appropriately.
Distinctive indicationsChest, rib or abdominal pain and dysmenorrhea from Qi-Blood stasis; Damp-Heat jaundice, mental disturbance and bleeding from Blood Heat.Blood-stasis pain, including menstrual, traumatic and abdominal pain.
Gallbladder and jaundiceFacilitates Gallbladder function and reduces jaundice.Not the distinctive focus in this comparison.
Preparation clueNo special preparation is keyed in this comparison.Vinegar-frying enhances its pain-relieving action.
Formula cluesQi Ge San.Jin Ling Zi San, Shao Fu Zhu Yu Tang and Ge Xia Zhu Yu Tang.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth move Qi and Blood and relieve pain. Blood Heat, constraint transforming into Fire, jaundice or bleeding favors Yu Jin; a broad pain indication or vinegar-preparation clue favors Yan Hu Suo.
Memory cueYu Jin cools, clears and relieves constraint; Yan Hu Suo moves Qi and stops pain.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 608. Source Exam Keys CMID 9117 and CMID 9114, questions 1–4.

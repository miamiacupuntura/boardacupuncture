Pathology or symptom comparison

Purulent Ear Pattern Differentiation

Diagnostic orientation

Core ideaDischarge color, pain and chronicity distinguish the source patterns; perforation and infection require otoscopic medical assessment.
Exam focusAcute pain and pus quality versus chronic clear or scant discharge

Comparative differentiation

Pattern or presentationDecisive cluesRepresentative formula/herbStrategy
External Wind-HeatAcute ear blockage and escalating pain after cold symptoms; perforation may release pus and reduce painMan Jing Zi SanDispel Wind-Heat and resolve toxin.
Liver-GB FireSevere ear symptoms with thick yellow or blood-tinged pus, bitter mouth, dry throat, constipation and dark urineLong Dan Xie Gan TangDrain Liver-GB Fire and resolve toxin.
Dampness encumbers deficient SpleenLarge fluctuating quantity of clear thin pus, chronic poor response, heavy head, fatigue, poor appetite and pale moist tongueTuo Li Xiao Du SanStrengthen Spleen and drain Dampness.
Kidney-Yin deficiency with residual toxinSmall amount of thin chronic pus with marked hearing loss, tinnitus, weak back/knees and fatigueZhi Bai Di Huang WanNourish Kidney Yin and address residual toxin.

Study rule

How to distinguishAcute pain after a cold is Wind-Heat; thick yellow/bloody pus with Fire signs is Liver-GB; copious clear chronic pus is Spleen-Damp; scant chronic pus plus lumbar weakness is Kidney.
Memory cueAcute-cold—Wind; thick-yellow—Fire; copious-clear—Spleen; scant-chronic—Kidney.
SafetyEar pus, severe pain or suspected eardrum perforation needs medical examination; do not place unapproved drops or herbs in the ear. Swelling behind the ear, facial weakness, vertigo, fever or severe headache is urgent.

Herb comparison

Allii fistulosi Bulbus (cong bai) vs. Allii macrostemi Bulbus (xie bai)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueCong BaiXie Bai
Shared propertiesBoth are warm and acrid Allium medicinals that enter the Lung.
Fast distinctionMildly induces sweating to release the Exterior; think Wind-Cold and the Stomach channel.Moves Qi more strongly, drives Cold from the chest, transforms turbid Phlegm and unblocks the Large Intestine.

Comparative answer key

CompareCong BaiXie Bai
Medicinal nameAllii fistulosi Bulbus.Allii macrostemi Bulbus.
CategoryWarm, Acrid Herbs that Release the Exterior.Herbs that Regulate the Qi.
Nature and tasteWarm and acrid; not bitter and less warm than Xie Bai.Warmer, acrid and bitter.
Channel distinctionLung and Stomach; enters the leg Yangming channel.Lung, Stomach and Large Intestine; emphasizes the arm Yangming channel.
Primary actionMildly induces sweating and releases the Exterior.Moves Qi, warms and unbinds the chest, transforms Cold-Phlegm and lubricates the orifices.
Chest clueDoes not have Xie Bai's strong chest-opening and Phlegm-transforming emphasis.Chest painful obstruction radiating to the back with wheezing or shortness of breath from Cold and turbid Phlegm.
Intestinal clueDiarrhea from accumulated Cold and insufficient Yang movement in the lower burner.Cold-stagnation dysenteric disorders; its slippery quality helps unblock the Large Intestine.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe herbs look and taste similar, but Cong Bai acts outward through mild sweating while Xie Bai acts inward on chest Qi, turbid Phlegm and the Large Intestine.
Memory cueCong Bai opens the Exterior; Xie Bai opens the chest and Intestine.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 31. Source Exam Key CMID 9100, questions 1–3.

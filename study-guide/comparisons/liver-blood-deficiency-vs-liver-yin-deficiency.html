Liver Blood Deficiency vs. Liver Yin Deficiency

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueLiver Blood DeficiencyLiver Yin Deficiency
Shared clueBoth fail to nourish the eyes, sinews and menstrual system, causing blurred vision, cramps, dizziness, dryness and scanty menses.
Fast distinctionBlood deficiency is pale: pale face/tongue, numbness, brittle nails, scant menses and a thin or choppy pulse.Yin deficiency is red and hot: dry eyes/throat, tinnitus, night sweating, five-center Heat, red peeled tongue and thin rapid pulse.

Comparative answer key

CompareLiver Blood DeficiencyLiver Yin Deficiency
DepthDeficient Blood nutrition.Deeper depletion of Blood, fluids and Yin anchoring.
Thermal signUsually no pronounced Empty Heat.Afternoon/night Heat and dryness are characteristic.
Tongue / pulsePale, often thin; thin/choppy.Red with little coat; thin-rapid.
Formula examplesSi Wu Tang.Yi Guan Jian or a Liver-Kidney Yin tonic by presentation.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersEye and menstrual symptoms overlap. Pale versus red, and absence versus presence of Empty Heat, reveal the depth of deficiency.
Memory cueBlood deficiency is pale and underfed; Yin deficiency is dry, red and warm.
SafetySudden visual loss, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, heavy bleeding or pregnancy-related bleeding requires urgent evaluation.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

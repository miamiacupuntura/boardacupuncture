Xiao Chai Hu Tang vs. Si Ni San vs. Xiao Yao San

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueXiao Chai Hu TangSi Ni SanXiao Yao San
Shared clueAll three use Chai Hu and regulate a dysfunctional pivot or constrained Qi, but they treat different mechanisms.
Fast distinctionShaoyang pathogen: alternating chills and fever with bitter taste and rib-side fullness.Constrained Yang: cold fingers and toes while the trunk remains warm.Liver-Qi constraint with Spleen-Blood deficiency: fatigue, poor appetite and menstrual or emotional symptoms.

Exam shortcutTurn the Shaoyang pivot.Release trapped Yang.Move Liver Qi and rebuild.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersRemembering only “Chai Hu moves Liver Qi” misses the diagnostic architecture. The associated signs reveal whether the target is Shaoyang, constrained Yang circulation or deficiency-complicated Liver constraint.
Memory cueXiao Chai Hu = alternating; Si Ni San = cold tips; Xiao Yao = constraint plus deficiency.
SafetyThese are pattern-specific prescriptions. Persistent fever, severe abdominal pain, fainting or cold extremities with systemic collapse requires medical evaluation.

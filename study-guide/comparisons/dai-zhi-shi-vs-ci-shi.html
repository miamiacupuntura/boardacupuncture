Herb comparison

Haematitum (dai zhe shi) vs. Magnetitum (ci shi)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueDai Zhe ShiCi Shi
Shared cluesBoth are heavy, Yang-anchoring substances that sedate fright and quiet the Spirit.
Fast distinctionUses its massive weight to direct rebellious Qi downward and clear Liver Fire.Protects true Yin when deficiency below allows Yang to float upward.

Comparative answer key

CompareDai Zhe Shi (Haematitum)Ci Shi (Magnetitum)
Category in sourceSubstances that Extinguish Wind and Stop Tremors.Substances that Anchor, Settle, and Calm the Spirit.
Primary emphasisSuppresses and directs rebellious Qi downward while clearing Liver Fire.Protects true Yin and anchors floating Yang.
Distinctive indicationsLiver Yang ascendancy with dizziness, headache or tinnitus; also nausea, vomiting and wheezing.True Yin deficiency in the lower body that permits Yang to float upward.
Additional actionCools Liver Blood and stops bleeding.The source comparison gives no corresponding blood-cooling or hemostatic action.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth are heavy anchoring substances, so the exam distinguishes them by what each one anchors or redirects.
Common confusionRebellious Qi, vomiting, wheezing or bleeding favors Dai Zhe Shi; deficient true Yin with floating Yang favors Ci Shi.
Memory cueDai Zhe Shi directs down; Ci Shi protects Yin.
Name noteHaematitum is written “dai zhi shi” in Source Exam Key CMID 8924 and “dai zhe shi” in CMID 8942. This note uses Dai Zhe Shi as the visible name while retaining the earlier spelling as a search alias.
Source question formatQuestion 1 is Matching.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 915. Source Exam Key CMID 8924.

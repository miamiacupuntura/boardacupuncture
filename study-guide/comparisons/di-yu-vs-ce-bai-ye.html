Herb comparison

Sanguisorbae Radix (di yu) vs. Platycladi Cacumen (ce bai ye)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueDi YuCe Bai Ye
Shared cluesBoth are bitter, astringent and cold, enter the Liver and Large Intestine, and stop bleeding caused by Heat.
Decisive clueStronger astringency, colder nature, toxicity resolution, lower-jiao Damp-Heat bleeding or skin lesions.Bloody cough or phlegm, cough suppression, phlegm transformation and hair growth.

Comparative answer key

CompareDi Yu (Sanguisorbae Radix)Ce Bai Ye (Platycladi Cacumen)
CategoryHerbs that Regulate the Blood · Herbs that Stop Bleeding.
Properties/channelsSlightly cold, bitter and sour; Liver, Large Intestine and Stomach.Bitter, astringent and slightly cold; Lung, Liver and Large Intestine.
Bleeding emphasisStronger astringency; lower-jiao Damp-Heat bleeding, including bloody dysentery and uterine bleeding.Blood-Heat bleeding, including bloody phlegm, hematemesis, epistaxis, hematuria and uterine bleeding.
Additional actionsColder and resolves toxicity; used externally for burns, eczema and skin ulcers.Transforms phlegm, stops cough, promotes hair growth, and is used topically in early-stage burns; also appears with Wind-Damp Bi.
Formula cluesQing Re Zhi Beng Tang; Di Yu Wan.Shi Hui San; Si Sheng Wan; Huai Hua San; Bai Ye Tang.
Preparation clueThe source associates charred preparations with bleeding and ointment use with burns; both are listed at 6–15 g in the exam material.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe shared bleeding, temperature and external-burn clues make organ emphasis and secondary actions decisive.
Common confusionDi Yu is the stronger, colder astringent with lower-jiao and toxicity clues; Ce Bai Ye adds Lung, cough, phlegm and hair-growth clues.
Caution in sourceDi Yu is contraindicated in Spleen/Stomach Deficiency Cold. The source cautions against long-term Ce Bai Ye use.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 572. Source Exam Keys CMID 8908 and CMID 8933.

Pathology or symptom comparison

Enuresis Pattern Differentiation

Diagnostic orientation

Core ideaAge, continence history, constipation, sleep and urinary symptoms must be assessed before assigning a Chinese medicine pattern.
Exam focusUrine quality, constitutional signs and Heat versus Cold

Comparative differentiation

Pattern or presentationDecisive cluesRepresentative formula/herbStrategy
Kidney Yang deficiencyBedwetting several times nightly, pale complexion, fatigue, copious clear urine, cold limbs and weak low back or legsSuo Quan Wan or Tu Si Zi WanWarm Kidney Yang and secure urine.
Lung-Spleen Qi deficiencyFrequent short urination in a weak or recovering child with dyspnea, fatigue, sallow complexion, poor appetite, loose stool and spontaneous sweatingBu Zhong Yi Qi Tang + Suo Quan WanTonify Lung-Spleen Qi and consolidate.
Liver-channel Damp-HeatSmall-volume, fishy or dark-yellow urine with agitation, sleep talking, teeth grinding, red lips and a yellow greasy coatLong Dan Xie Gan TangClear Liver Damp-Heat.

Study rule

How to distinguishCopious clear urine plus cold is Kidney Yang; weakness with digestive and respiratory Qi signs is Lung-Spleen; scant dark malodorous urine plus agitation is Damp-Heat.
Memory cueClear and cold—Kidney; weak and loose—Lung-Spleen; dark and agitated—Damp-Heat.
SafetyBedwetting can be developmentally normal under about age 5. New secondary enuresis, painful urination, fever, marked thirst, weight loss, constipation, snoring/apnea or neurologic signs needs medical assessment. Avoid shame or punishment. Tu Si Zi Wan versions containing Fu Zi require expert prescribing.
SourceInternal Medicine pediatrics section: Enuresis (Yi Niao).

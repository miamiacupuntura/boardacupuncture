Regulation, Identity & Sourcing

A safe prescription depends on the exact species, part, processing, supplier and jurisdiction.

Core rule

RuleThe label must answer five questions: what species, what part, what processing, what batch and what jurisdiction permits it?

Identity chain

Group / stepWhat to knowSafe decision
NamePinyin plus Chinese characters.Prevents same-sound substitutions such as Dà Jì/Dà Jǐ or Huáng Qín/Huáng Qí.
Botanical identityAccepted Latin binomial and pharmacopoeial name.Separates Stephania from Aristolochia and genuine from adulterant species.
Medicinal partLeaf, twig, bark, root, rhizome, seed, fruit, shell or animal material.Same plant part can change direction and risk.
ProcessingRaw, prepared, honey-fried, charred, wine-steamed or other specified Páo Zhì.Processing is part of identity, not an optional note.
Batch traceabilityLot number, manufacturer, dates and certificate of analysis.Enables recall and investigation of contamination/adulteration.

Quality and legal screen

Group / stepWhat to knowSafe decision
ContaminantsTest for heavy metals, pesticides, microbes, mycotoxins and undeclared pharmaceuticals where relevant.“Natural” does not establish purity.
Obsolete substancesAristolochia drugs and toxic mineral/animal substances may be prohibited or obsolete.Historical formulas require legal, ethical substitutions—not literal copying.
WildlifeMusk, rhinoceros horn, tiger bone, bear bile, pangolin scale and some shells/animals raise CITES and wildlife-law concerns.Use lawful accepted substitutes and document sourcing.
JurisdictionPermitted substances, practitioner scope and labeling rules differ by country/state.A textbook listing is not authorization to possess or prescribe.
Supplier qualificationUse audited professional suppliers with authenticated inventory and recall procedures.Marketplace appearance or pinyin-only labels are inadequate.
Patient-facing labelInclude ingredients, processing, directions, warnings, lot and contact details.Patients need enough information for emergency identification.

Classic substitutions

Group / stepWhat to knowSafe decision
Xī Jiǎo 犀角Rhinoceros horn is illegal/obsolete.Shuǐ Niú Jiǎo (water-buffalo horn) is the standard lawful substitute where permitted, with formula adjustment.
Hǔ Gǔ 虎骨Tiger bone is prohibited wildlife material.Use strategy-based lawful substitutes; never seek “authentic” wildlife product.
Shè Xiāng 麝香Natural musk is wildlife-restricted.Use approved synthetic musk or an accepted formula alternative where lawful.
Guān Mù Tōng / Guǎng Fáng Jǐ / Qīng Mù XiāngAristolochia sources are unsafe.Do not make casual one-for-one substitutions; reformulate from the intended action using accepted species.

When to act immediately

EmergencyIf a product is mislabeled, contaminated or causes a serious reaction: stop use, preserve the package/batch, obtain medical care and report through the applicable regulator or poison-control system.

Educational use: this library does not replace medical evaluation, poison control, local regulation or pharmacist consultation.

Da Chai Hu Tang vs. Xiao Yao San

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueDa Chai Hu TangXiao Yao San
Shared clueBoth contain Chai Hu, address constrained Qi and may present with hypochondriac discomfort, irritability, nausea or digestive symptoms.
Fast distinctionShaoyang plus Yangming excess: alternating chills and fever with rib-side fullness, vomiting, constipation or focal abdominal pain.Liver-Qi constraint with deficiency: emotional or menstrual symptoms with fatigue, poor appetite and a wiry-weak pulse.

Comparative answer key

CompareDa Chai Hu TangXiao Yao San
Root patternExcess lodged in Shaoyang with interior Yangming accumulation.Liver-Qi constraint with Spleen-Qi and Blood deficiency.
Treatment directionHarmonize Shaoyang and drain interior excess.Course Liver Qi, strengthen Spleen and nourish Blood.
Decisive abdominal clueConstipation, focal fullness or pain and an excess presentation.Milder distention or discomfort with weak digestion and fatigue.
Key ingredientsChai Hu, Huang Qin, Ban Xia, Bai Shao, Zhi Shi and Da Huang.Chai Hu, Dang Gui, Bai Shao, Bai Zhu, Fu Ling and Zhi Gan Cao.
PulseWiry and forceful, often rapid.Wiry and weak.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersChai Hu does not make the formulas interchangeable. Da Chai Hu treats a forceful two-level excess and includes a downward-draining unit; Xiao Yao San moves constraint while rebuilding deficient systems.
Memory cueDa Chai Hu opens the pivot and the bowel; Xiao Yao moves while nourishing.
SafetyDa Chai Hu Tang has a purgative component and is not for self-treatment. Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting or suspected obstruction requires urgent evaluation.

Formula comparison

Bai Hu Tang vs. Zhu Ye Shi Gao Tang

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueBai Hu TangZhu Ye Shi Gao Tang
Shared clueBoth clear Qi-level Heat and generate fluids, and both use Shi Gao, Gan Cao and Jing Mi.
Fast distinctionBlazing excess Heat: the “four bigs”—high fever, great thirst, profuse sweating and a big forceful pulse.Residual Heat after febrile disease with injured Qi and fluids: lingering fever, thirst, fatigue and rebellious Stomach Qi.

Comparative answer key

CompareBai Hu TangZhu Ye Shi Gao Tang
Root patternStrong Yangming Qi-level excess Heat.Residual Heat with Qi and fluid deficiency after illness.
Therapeutic emphasisPowerfully clears Heat and generates fluids.Clears lingering Heat, augments Qi, nourishes fluids and harmonizes the Stomach.
Decisive clueAll four “bigs” are prominent.Weakness plus thirst, irritability, nausea or retching after the major Heat has declined.
PulseFlooding, forceful and rapid.Deficient and rapid.
Exam shortcutHeat is still full and blazing.Heat remains, but the patient is depleted.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersDo not choose only by the presence of fever and thirst. The patient’s strength distinguishes a full Heat pattern from residual Heat with deficiency.
Memory cueBai Hu fights the blaze; Zhu Ye Shi Gao clears the embers and rebuilds fluids.
SafetyPersistent high fever, confusion, inability to drink or signs of severe dehydration require urgent medical evaluation.
SourceSource bank: FWB 2.2 series, Comparison Formulas to Clear Heat; corroborated with Classic Formulas profiles and individual formula files.

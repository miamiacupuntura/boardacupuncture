Herb comparison

Rosae rugosae Flos (mei gui hua) vs. Rosae chinensis Flos (yue ji hua)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueMei Gui HuaYue Ji Hua
Shared cluesBoth flowers are sweet and warm, promote the movement of Qi and Blood, and treat irregular menstruation, premenstrual breast distention and abdominal pain from Qi and Blood stasis.
Fast distinctionPrimarily addresses Qi and harmonizes the Liver, Spleen and Stomach.Primarily addresses Blood, with stronger Blood-invigorating, swelling-reducing and toxicity-resolving actions.

Comparative answer key

CompareMei Gui Hua (Rosae rugosae Flos)Yue Ji Hua (Rosae chinensis Flos)
CategoryHerbs that Regulate the Qi.Herbs that Regulate and Invigorate the Blood.
Primary levelPrimarily addresses Qi.Primarily acts on Blood.
Channels in the sourceLiver and Spleen.Liver.
Primary emphasisRestores harmony among the Liver, Spleen and Stomach.More strongly invigorates Blood, reduces swelling and resolves toxicity.
Distinctive indicationsLiver Qi invading the Spleen and Stomach with chest oppression, belching, flank distention and poor appetite.Sores from Qi and Blood constraint transforming into Fire, and scrofula.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe flowers share gynecological indications, but the decisive clue is Qi regulation and digestive harmony for Mei Gui Hua versus stronger Blood movement and toxicity resolution for Yue Ji Hua.
Memory cueMei Gui Hua moves Qi; Yue Ji Hua moves Blood.
Source-data noteThe source's separate Yue Ji Hua flavor line is incomplete (“Sweet, Slightly.”), so this note does not infer the missing word.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 620. Source Exam Key CMID 9018, questions 6–11.

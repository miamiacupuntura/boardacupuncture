Herb comparison

Rehmanniae Radix preparata (shu di huang) vs. Asini Corii Colla (e jiao)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueShu Di HuangE Jiao
Shared cluesBoth enter Liver and Kidney, nourish Blood, enrich Kidney Yin and treat Blood deficiency and gynecological disorders. Both are greasy and may interfere with digestion.
Fast distinctionGenerates Essence and augments Marrow.Enriches Lung Yin, moistens Dryness and stops bleeding.

Comparative answer key

CompareShu Di HuangE Jiao
Shared categoryHerbs that Tonify the Blood.
Properties and channelsSlightly warm and sweet; Liver, Kidney and Heart.Neutral and sweet; Lung, Liver and Kidney.
Preparation clueThe source describes the root as steamed and sun-dried nine times before final drying.Prepared as gelatin from softened, cleaned and boiled donkey skin, then cooled and dried into solid pieces.
Primary emphasisTonifies Blood and Yin, generates Essence and augments Marrow.Tonifies Blood, enriches Lung-Kidney Yin and stops bleeding.
Distinctive indicationsFive-center Heat, steaming bones, night sweats, weak low back and legs, tinnitus and premature graying.Dry cough, post-febrile Yin deficiency and bleeding, especially from the Lung, stool or uterus.
Formula cluesSi Wu Tang, Liu Wei Di Huang Wan, You Gui Wan and Zuo Gui Yin.Bu Fei E Jiao Tang, E Jiao Ji Zi Huang Tang, Huang Lian E Jiao Tang, Jiao Ai Tang and Zhi Gan Cao Tang.
King-ingredient clueKing ingredient in many Rehmannia and Blood-tonifying formulas.King ingredient in E Jiao Ji Zi Huang Tang, Huang Lian E Jiao Tang and Bu Fei E Jiao Tang.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth are rich, greasy Blood and Yin tonics used in gynecology, so Essence-Marrow versus Lung-and-bleeding actions provide the clean distinction.
Common confusionTinnitus, gray hair, weak back or Essence deficiency favors Shu Di Huang; dry cough or active bleeding favors E Jiao.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 757. Source Exam Key CMID 8948, questions 1–7, and CMID 9070, questions 7–13.

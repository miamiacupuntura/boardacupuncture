Pathology or symptom comparison

Infantile Malnutrition Pattern Differentiation

Diagnostic orientation

Core ideaWeight loss or growth faltering in a child requires nutritional, gastrointestinal, endocrine, infectious and safeguarding assessment before pattern-based support.
Exam focusGrowth failure, abdominal distention and parasite-specific clues

Comparative differentiation

Pattern or presentationDecisive cluesRepresentative formula/herbStrategy
Spleen-Stomach deficiency with accumulationGradual weight loss, sallow complexion, thin hair, poor appetite, loose stool; later marked distention, emaciation, dry skin/hair and low feverZi Sheng Jian Pi Wan or Gan Ji SanHistorical strategy: support digestion and reduce accumulation.
Parasitic infestationMalnutrition signs plus overeating/pica, distended abdomen with visible veins, recurrent pain and tooth grindingFei Er WanHistorical strategy: reduce accumulation and expel worms after diagnosis.

Study rule

How to distinguishPoor intake/loose stool with progressive wasting suggests digestive deficiency; pica, recurrent pain and parasite exposure raise concern for infestation—but both need biomedical workup.
Memory cueFailure to grow = measure, test and feed safely before assigning a pattern.
SafetyWeight loss, edema, lethargy, dehydration, persistent diarrhea/vomiting, developmental regression or suspected neglect/food insecurity requires prompt pediatric assessment. Parasites require testing and standard treatment.

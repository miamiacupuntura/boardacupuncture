Herb comparison

Artemisiae annuae Herba (qing hao) vs. Bupleuri Radix (chai hu)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueQing HaoChai Hu
Shared cluesBoth enter the Liver and Gallbladder and treat malarial disorders with alternating chills and fever.
Decisive clueDeficiency Heat, Summerheat, injured Yin or bone steaming.Exterior Heat, Liver Qi constraint and raising clear Yang.

Comparative answer key

CompareQing Hao (Artemisiae annuae Herba)Chai Hu (Bupleuri Radix)
CategoryHerb that Clears Heat from Deficiency; the source also describes it as clearing Summerheat.Cool, Acrid Herb that Releases the Exterior.
Nature of actionLight, clearing and aromatically cooling.Raising and dispersing.
Heat patternSummerheat without injuring Yin; fever after Yin injury by warm or Summerheat pathogens; Yin-deficiency bone steaming or consumptive Heat.Alternating chills and fever with a Lesser Yang/exterior emphasis.
Distinctive actionResolves Summerheat while protecting Yin.Spreads Liver Qi constraint and raises clear Yang.
Yin clueSelected when clearing Heat without further injuring Yin is important.The source warns that overuse tends to injure Yin.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersAlternating chills and fever alone does not distinguish the herbs; the underlying Heat pattern and direction of action do.
Common confusionQing Hao clears deficiency/Summerheat and protects Yin; Chai Hu disperses and raises but may injure Yin when overused.
How it may appearBone steaming or Yin-injured Summerheat favors Qing Hao. Liver Qi constraint or raising clear Yang favors Chai Hu.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 220. Source Exam Keys CMID 8917 and CMID 9035, questions 1–2.

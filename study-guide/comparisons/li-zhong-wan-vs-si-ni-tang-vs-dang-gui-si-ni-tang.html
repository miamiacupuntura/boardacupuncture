Three warming strategies · FWB Warm the Interior

Li Zhong Wan vs. Si Ni Tang vs. Dang Gui Si Ni Tang

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueLi Zhong WanSi Ni TangDang Gui Si Ni Tang
Shared clueAll treat internal Cold, but they target different depths and mechanisms.
Fast distinctionMiddle-burner deficiency-Cold: abdominal pain, vomiting or diarrhea improved by warmth and pressure.Devastated Yang: severe systemic Cold, exhaustion, diarrhea and a minute pulse.Blood deficiency with channel Cold: chronically cold hands and feet with a fine, nearly imperceptible pulse.

Comparative answer key

CompareLi Zhong WanSi Ni TangDang Gui Si Ni Tang
Treatment levelSpleen-Stomach middle burner.Deep systemic Yang collapse.Channels and extremities with deficient Blood.
Primary actionWarm and tonify the middle burner.Restore Yang and rescue collapse.Warm channels, disperse Cold and nourish Blood.
Decisive clueDigestive Cold improves with warmth and pressure.Profound cold and weakness with faint pulse.Persistent cold extremities without systemic collapse.
Key ingredientsRen Shen, Bai Zhu, Gan Jiang and Zhi Gan Cao.Processed Fu Zi, Gan Jiang and Zhi Gan Cao.Dang Gui, Gui Zhi, Bai Shao, Xi Xin, Tong Cao and Da Zao.
Exam shortcutCold center.Failing fire.Cold channels with thin Blood.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it matters“Cold extremities” is not enough. Determine whether Cold begins in digestion, reflects life-threatening Yang failure or persists in the channels because Blood cannot nourish and warm them.
Memory cueLi Zhong warms the center; Si Ni rescues the fire; Dang Gui Si Ni warms the channels.
SafetySi Ni Tang requires professionally processed Fu Zi and qualified supervision. Xi Xin also requires verified species and professional dosing. Signs of circulatory collapse are an emergency.

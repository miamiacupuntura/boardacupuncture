Blood-Deficiency Wind vs. Liver-Yang-Rising Wind

Distinguish fine deficiency movement from stronger upward Yang with headache and hypertension.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueBlood-Deficiency Generating WindLiver Yang Rising Generating Wind
SharedBoth may cause tremor, dizziness, blurred vision, numbness and a wiry-thin pulse.
Fast distinctionFine chronic tremor with pallor, insomnia, poor memory and scanty menses; tongue pale and thin.More forceful upward signs: headache, tinnitus, facial twitch, hypertension and a red or uncoated tongue with Yin-deficiency signs.

Clinical table

CompareBlood-Deficiency Generating WindLiver Yang Rising Generating Wind
MovementFine, mild and rooted in failed nourishment.Stronger, rising and often accompanied by headache.
Formula associationE Jiao Ji Zi Huang Tang in the supplied table.Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin or Zhen Gan Xi Feng Tang by presentation.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersBoth are deficiency-rooted Wind; color and force separate Blood failure from rising Yang.
Memory cueBlood Wind flutters pale; Yang Wind pushes upward.
SafetySudden tremor, weakness, speech change, facial droop or severe headache is a neurologic emergency.

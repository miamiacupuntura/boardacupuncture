Herb comparison

Aurantii Fructus immaturus (zhi shi) vs. Aurantii Fructus (zhi ke / zhi qiao)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueZhi ShiZhi Ke / Zhi Qiao
Shared cluesBoth are acrid and bitter, enter the Spleen and Stomach, break up Qi stagnation, disperse clumps and Phlegm, and reduce focal distention.
Fast distinctionUnripe fruit; stronger and more forceful for obstruction, fullness and constipation.Almost-ripe fruit; milder and more appropriate for weaker patients, with a prominent prolapse clue.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe herbs come from the same fruit and share Qi-regulating actions. Strong obstruction or constipation points to Zhi Shi; weakness or a prolapse clue points to the milder Zhi Ke.
Memory cueZhi Shi is immature and intense; Zhi Ke is later and lighter.
Name cueZhi Ke is also called Zhi Qiao.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 515. Source Exam Key CMID 9122, questions 1–7.

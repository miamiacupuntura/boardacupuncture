Pericardium Qi Stagnation vs. Pericardium Blood Stasis

Use pain quality, color and emotional variability to distinguish Qi constraint from Blood stasis in the chest.

Fast differentiation clues

CluePericardium Qi StagnationPericardium Blood Stasis
SharedBoth may produce palpitations and chest discomfort associated with emotional constraint.
Fast distinctionDistending and variable: chest fullness that shifts with stress, frequent sighing and no strong purple signs.Fixed and stabbing: localized chest pain, purple lips/tongue, worse at night and a choppy pulse.

Clinical table

ComparePericardium Qi StagnationPericardium Blood Stasis
PainDistending, moving and emotion-sensitive.Fixed, sharp and persistent.
Tongue / pulseNormal or mildly dusky; wiry.Purple with spots; choppy, wiry or knotted.
Formula associationBan Xia Hou Po Tang when Qi-Phlegm constraint fits.Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang by presentation.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersQi stagnation changes and distends; Blood stasis fixes and stabs.
Memory cueQi moves the complaint; static Blood nails it down.
SafetyChest pain or palpitations require biomedical assessment; sudden, severe or exertional symptoms require emergency care.

Review prompts

  • Which pain changes with stress?
  • Which has purple signs?
  • What pulse points to stasis?

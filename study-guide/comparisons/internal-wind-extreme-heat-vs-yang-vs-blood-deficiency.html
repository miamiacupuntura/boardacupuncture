Internal Wind: Extreme Heat vs. Liver Yang vs. Blood Deficiency

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueWind from Extreme HeatWind from Liver Yang RisingWind from Blood Deficiency
Shared clueAll can produce tremor, spasm, dizziness or abnormal movement because the sinews lose stable nourishment and control.
Fast distinctionAcute emergency: very high fever, delirium, rigid neck and convulsions with a crimson tongue and rapid forceful pulse.Upward chronic excess-root/deficiency-root: severe dizziness, headache, tinnitus, irritability and coarse tremor with wiry pulse.Deficient nourishment: fine tremor, numbness, cramps, pale complexion/tongue and thin pulse.

Comparative answer key

CompareWind from Extreme HeatWind from Liver Yang RisingWind from Blood Deficiency
OnsetSudden during severe febrile disease.Usually chronic or episodic with Liver-Yang signs.Gradual with chronic Blood deficiency.
MovementConvulsion and marked rigidity.Coarser tremor, dizziness or potential sudden upward event.Fine tremor, twitching and cramps.
Tongue / pulseCrimson; rapid-forceful.Red edges; wiry, often rapid.Pale; thin/choppy.
Formula examplesLing Jiao Gou Teng Tang historically; endangered animal ingredients require substitution.Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin.Si Wu Tang with Wind-extinguishing support by presentation.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersMovement alone is nonspecific. Fever severity, upward Yang signs or pale deficiency signs identify the source of Wind.
Memory cueHeat convulses; Yang rises; Blood-starved sinews flutter.
SafetyAny first seizure, seizure lasting 5 minutes, sudden weakness/speech change, severe new headache, high fever with stiff neck or altered consciousness is an emergency.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

  • Which Wind has high fever?
  • Which has headache and tinnitus?
  • Which produces fine tremor with pallor?

Herb comparison

Paeoniae Radix alba (bai shao) vs. Angelicae sinensis Radix (dan gui)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueBai ShaoDang Gui
Shared cluesBoth belong to Herbs that Tonify the Blood, nourish Blood, alleviate pain and are commonly combined in Blood patterns and gynecological formulas.
Core contrastCool, sour and restraining: preserves Yin, softens the Liver, controls sweating and relieves deficiency-type cramping.Warm, acrid and moving: tonifies and circulates Blood, treats stasis, regulates menstruation and lubricates the intestines.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth herbs can appear in Blood deficiency, menstrual and pain questions. Their temperatures and movement distinguish them: Bai Shao is cool and restraining; Dang Gui is warm and circulating.
Common confusionIrregular menstruation, anemia and abdominal pain overlap. Sweating, Yin preservation and cramping favor Bai Shao; constipation, Blood stasis, amenorrhea and warm-moving action favor Dang Gui.
How it may appearA question may contrast inability versus ability to lubricate the intestines, cool-sour versus warm-acrid properties, or Liver-softening cramp relief versus Blood-moving menstrual and stasis treatment.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 751.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

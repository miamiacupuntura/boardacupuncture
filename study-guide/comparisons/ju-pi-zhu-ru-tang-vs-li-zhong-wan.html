Ju Pi Zhu Ru Tang vs. Li Zhong Wan

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueJu Pi Zhu Ru TangLi Zhong Wan
Shared clueBoth support deficient middle-burner Qi and redirect rebellious Stomach Qi in nausea or vomiting.
Fast distinctionStomach Qi deficiency with residual Heat: hiccup, retching, thirst and a tender red tongue.Spleen-Stomach deficiency-Cold: chronic vomiting/diarrhea, abdominal pain, no thirst and a pale tongue.

Comparative answer key

CompareJu Pi Zhu Ru TangLi Zhong Wan
Thermal patternDeficiency with residual Heat.Deficiency with internal Cold.
Primary actionClears Heat and descends rebellious Qi while augmenting Stomach Qi.Warms and tonifies the middle burner.
Decisive cluePersistent hiccup or retching with Heat signs.Cold abdominal pain and loose stools improved by warmth.
Exam shortcutWeak, hot, rebellious Stomach.Weak, cold middle burner.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth may vomit from deficiency, so temperature is decisive: Ju Pi Zhu Ru clears residual Heat; Li Zhong restores deficient warmth.
Memory cueZhu Ru cools the hiccup; Li Zhong warms the center.
SafetyPersistent vomiting or hiccup, inability to keep fluids down, blood, severe pain or neurologic symptoms requires medical evaluation.
SourceSource bank: FWB 9.1.2, Comparison Formulas to Regulate the Qi; corroborated with individual formula profiles.

Herb comparison

Artemisiae annuae Herba (qing hao) vs. Artemisiae scopariae Herba (yin chen)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueQing HaoYin Chen
Shared cluesBoth are aromatic, resolve Damp-Heat and may be combined for Damp-Heat jaundice, Damp-Warm disorders and Summerheat-Warmth.
Fast distinctionEnters Liver and Gallbladder; favors steaming bones, Summerheat-Warmth and malarial disorders.Enters Spleen and Stomach; drains Dampness and is specific for jaundice.

Comparative answer key

CompareQing HaoYin Chen
Botanical nameArtemisiae annuae Herba.Artemisiae scopariae Herba.
CategoryHerbs that Clear Heat from Deficiency; also clears Summerheat.Herbs that Drain Dampness.
Channel or organ focusLiver and Gallbladder.Spleen and Stomach.
Primary emphasisClears Deficiency Heat, vents Summerheat-Warmth and treats malarial disorders.Drains Dampness and treats jaundice specifically.
Decisive indicationSteaming bones from consumption or malarial-type alternating fever.Jaundice from Damp-Heat.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth address aromatic Damp-Heat patterns, but Qing Hao emphasizes Heat in the Liver-Gallbladder and Summerheat or malarial patterns, while Yin Chen emphasizes Spleen-Stomach Dampness and jaundice.
Memory cueQing Hao clears Heat and malaria; Yin Chen drains Dampness and jaundice.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), pp. 311–312. Source Exam Keys CMID 9035, questions 4–5 and 10–11, and CMID 9112, questions 1–4.

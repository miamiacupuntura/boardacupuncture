Lung Qi Deficiency vs. Wind-Cold Invading the Lung

Separate a chronic weak Lung baseline from an acute Exterior invasion.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueLung Qi DeficiencyWind-Cold Invasion
SharedBoth can cause cough, shortness of breath, aversion to wind and susceptibility to colds.
Fast distinctionChronic deficiency: weak voice/cough, spontaneous sweating, fatigue and recurrent infections; pale tongue and weak pulse.Acute invasion: sudden chills, body aches, nasal discharge and a floating-tight pulse.

Clinical table

CompareLung Qi DeficiencyWind-Cold Invasion
Time courseChronic or recurrent baseline.Acute onset after exposure.
Formula associationBu Fei Tang or Yu Ping Feng San by root pattern.Ma Huang Tang when an appropriate excess presentation is present.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersHistory and pulse depth distinguish weak constitution from a new pathogen; both may coexist.
Memory cueDeficiency is the weak door; Wind-Cold is the visitor.
SafetyHigh-risk patients, persistent fever, chest pain, breathing difficulty or low oxygen need medical care.

Review prompts

  • Which is acute?
  • Which sweats spontaneously?
  • Can deficiency predispose to invasion?

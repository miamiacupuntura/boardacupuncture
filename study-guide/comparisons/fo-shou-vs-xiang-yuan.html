Herb comparison

Fo Shou vs. Xiang Yuan

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueFo ShouXiang Yuan
Shared clueBoth are warm, acrid and bitter citrus herbs that regulate Qi and enter the Liver, Spleen and Lung channels.
Fast distinctionSpecifically breaks up Qi stagnation.Specifically breaks up Phlegm and pathogenic water.

Comparative answer key

CompareFo ShouXiang Yuan
Botanical nameCitri sarcodactylis Fructus.Citri Fructus.
CategoryHerbs that Regulate the Qi.Herbs that Regulate the Qi.
Properties and channelsWarm, acrid and bitter; Lung, Liver, Stomach and Spleen.Warm, acrid, slightly bitter and sour; Liver, Spleen and Lung.
Primary emphasisStrongly moves Qi, but has relatively weak pain-relieving and Phlegm-transforming effects.Breaks up Phlegm and pathogenic water; relatively mild and often combined with other herbs.
Clinical clueDysenteric disorder with tenesmus, especially using an aged product; not used for the early stage of externally contracted cough.Cough due to clogged Qi, preferably using an aged product; the source also associates it with “waist and leg Qi.”
Preparation clueThe aged product is preferred for dysenteric tenesmus.Removing the pith and kernels reduces the excessive sour constraint described in the source.
PrecautionsUse cautiously without Qi stagnation or with Yin deficiency and Heat; contraindicated with Excess Fire symptoms.Use cautiously during pregnancy, with Yin deficiency or with Blood Dryness.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe herbs share temperature, flavor, category and three channels; the decisive split is Qi stagnation versus Phlegm and pathogenic water.
Memory cueFo Shou frees Qi; Xiang Yuan clears Phlegm and water.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 524. Source Exam Key CMID 8953, questions 1–6.

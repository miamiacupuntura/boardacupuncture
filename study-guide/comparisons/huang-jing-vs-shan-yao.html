Herb comparison

Huang Jing vs. Shan Yao

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueHuang JingShan Yao
Shared clueBoth are sweet and neutral, tonify Qi, nourish Yin, enter the Spleen, Lung and Kidney, and may be used as foods.
Fast distinctionMoist, gradual and stronger at nourishing Yin and supplementing Essence.Astringent and better at restraining loss through diarrhea, vaginal discharge, spermatorrhea or frequent urination.
Exam patternDryness, chronic debility and recovery.Deficiency with leakage or failure to contain.

Comparative answer key

CompareHuang JingShan Yao
Medicinal and common namePolygonati Rhizoma; King Solomon's seal root.Dioscoreae Rhizoma; Chinese yam.
Shared categoryHerbs that Tonify the Qi.
Shared properties and channelsSweet and neutral; Spleen, Lung and Kidney.
Shared actionsTonify Qi, nourish Yin and address Spleen, Lung and Kidney deficiency.
Primary emphasisMore moistening and stronger at nourishing Yin; supplements Kidney Essence.Tonifies Spleen and Lung while consolidating Kidney Essence through its astringent action.
Distinctive indicationsFatigue or chronic debility, wasting-thirst, dry stool, dry cough and chronic illness.Chronic diarrhea, vaginal discharge, spermatorrhea, frequent urination and other deficiency-related leakage.
Food-use clueModerate and slow; the source describes it as suitable for gradual support and as a food.A food-grade Qi tonic used in soups and gruels; the source especially associates it with weak digestion or malnutrition in children.
Combination or preparation clueIts emphasis follows its partners: Qi tonics, Yin tonics, middle-Jiao tonics, Lung tonics or Kidney tonics.Dry-fried preparation emphasizes Spleen tonification; raw preparation emphasizes Yin nourishment.
Dose stated in source6–18 g.9–30 g; the source lists up to 250 g for wasting-thirst.
Source cautionsSpleen deficiency with Dampness or poor digestion.Excess patterns, Qi stagnation, Damp accumulation or Food Stagnation.
Formula cluesXuan Yu Tong Jing Tang.Liu Wei Di Huang Wan, Ba Wei Di Huang Wan, You Gui Wan, Qi Ju Di Huang Wan, Zuo Gui Wan, Zuo Gui Yin, Shen Ling Bai Zhu San, Wan Dai Tang, Yi Huang Tang and Tu Si Zi Wan.
Scientific names in sourcePolygonatum kingianum, Polygonatum sibiricum or Polygonatum cyrtonema.Dioscorea opposita.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe two herbs share category, flavor, temperature, channels and core tonifying actions. Moistening and gradual recovery clues favor Huang Jing; astringency and leakage clues favor Shan Yao.
Memory cueHuang Jing moistens and builds; Shan Yao tonifies and holds.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 738. Source Exam Key CMID 8979, questions 1–10.

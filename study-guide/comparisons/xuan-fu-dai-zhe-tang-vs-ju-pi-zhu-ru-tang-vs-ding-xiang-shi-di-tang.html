Three Stomach-Qi rebellion patterns · FWB Regulate Qi

Xuan Fu Dai Zhe Tang vs. Ju Pi Zhu Ru Tang vs. Ding Xiang Shi Di Tang

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueXuan Fu Dai Zhe TangJu Pi Zhu Ru TangDing Xiang Shi Di Tang
Shared clueAll descend rebellious Stomach Qi in hiccup, belching, nausea or vomiting with an underlying deficiency.
Fast distinctionStomach deficiency with Phlegm-Dampness: persistent belching, epigastric fullness and a greasy coat.Stomach deficiency with residual Heat: loud hiccup or belching, thirst and a red tongue.Stomach deficiency-Cold: quiet weak hiccup, cold limbs, no thirst and a pale tongue.

Comparative answer key

CompareXuan Fu Dai Zhe TangJu Pi Zhu Ru TangDing Xiang Shi Di Tang
ComplicationPhlegm-Dampness.Residual Heat.Empty Cold.
Treatment directionTransform Phlegm, augment Qi and strongly descend rebellion.Clear Heat, augment Stomach Qi and stop hiccup.Warm Stomach, augment Qi and stop hiccup.
Key ingredientsXuan Fu Hua, Dai Zhe Shi, Ban Xia, Sheng Jiang and Ren Shen.Ju Pi, Zhu Ru, Ren Shen, Sheng Jiang and Da Zao.Ding Xiang, Shi Di, Ren Shen and Sheng Jiang.
Decisive clueBelching with fullness and Phlegm.Hiccup worsened by warmth; red tongue, weak-rapid pulse.Hiccup improved by warmth; pale tongue, deep-slow pulse.
Exam shortcutHeavy descent for Phlegm rebellion.Cool the hot hiccup.Warm the cold hiccup.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe symptom “hiccup” is not diagnostic by itself. Identify whether Phlegm, residual Heat or Empty Cold prevents Stomach Qi from descending.
Memory cueXuan Fu–Dai Zhe weighs Phlegm down; Zhu Ru cools; Ding Xiang warms.
SafetyPersistent hiccup or vomiting, inability to keep fluids down, blood, severe pain, neurologic symptoms or weight loss requires medical evaluation.

Liver Qi Stagnation vs. Rebellious Liver Qi

Separate Qi that is stuck from Qi that has acquired an abnormal upward or invading direction.

Fast differentiation clues

ClueLiver Qi StagnationRebellious Liver Qi
SharedBoth feature emotional triggers, rib-side or epigastric distention, irritability, sighing and a wiry pulse.
Fast distinctionQi is stuck: pressure, distention and variable pain dominate.Qi moves the wrong way: hiccup, belching, nausea, vomiting or churning accompany the distention.

Clinical table

CompareLiver Qi StagnationRebellious Liver Qi
DirectionFree coursing is blocked.Constrained Qi invades Stomach/Spleen or rises upward.
Decisive clueDistention without strong counterflow.Counterflow symptoms—belching, hiccup, nausea or vomiting.
Formula associationChai Hu Shu Gan San or Xiao Yao San.Si Ni San or a harmonizing/downbearing strategy according to the invaded organ.

Exam and safety

Why it mattersRebellious Qi includes stagnation plus a wrong direction; the counterflow symptom identifies it.
Memory cueStagnant Qi stops; rebellious Qi goes the wrong way.
SafetyPersistent vomiting, blood, severe pain, dehydration or weight loss needs prompt evaluation.

Review prompts

  • Which has hiccup and belching?
  • Can rebellious Qi exist without stagnation?
  • What identifies the invaded organ?

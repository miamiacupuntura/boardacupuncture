Herb comparison

Magnoliae Flos (xin yi hua) vs. Magnoliae officinalis Cortex (hou po)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueXin Yi HuaHou Po
Shared propertiesBoth are warm, acrid Magnolia medicinals that enter the Lung and Stomach.
Fast distinctionDried flower bud; releases Wind-Cold and opens the nasal passages.Bark; dries Dampness, moves Qi, relieves fullness and transforms Phlegm.

Comparative answer key

CompareXin Yi HuaHou Po
Medicinal name and partMagnoliae Flos; dried flower bud.Magnoliae officinalis Cortex; bark.
CategoryWarm, Acrid Herbs that Release the Exterior.Aromatic Herbs that Transform Dampness.
Nature and tasteWarm and acrid.Warm, bitter, acrid and aromatic.
ChannelsLung and Stomach.Lung, Large Intestine, Spleen and Stomach.
Primary focusWind-Cold nasal obstruction, sinus congestion, profuse discharge and headache; combinations extend its use to other nasal-obstruction patterns.Dampness with stagnant Qi or Food causing chest or abdominal fullness, nausea or diarrhea.
Lung clueOpens the nasal orifice.Directs Qi downward and transforms Phlegm-Dampness for cough or wheezing.
Formula cluesThe source emphasizes nasal-opening combinations.Ping Wei San, Ban Xia Hou Po Tang and Su Zi Jiang Qi Tang.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe Magnolia name is not enough: flower-bud and nasal clues indicate Xin Yi Hua; bark, Dampness, fullness and descending Lung Qi indicate Hou Po.
Memory cueXin Yi Hua opens the nose; Hou Po dries Dampness and relieves fullness.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 40. Source Exam Key CMID 9101, questions 2–5.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

  • Which medicinal is the flower bud and which is the bark?
  • Which herb opens the nasal passages?
  • Which herb dries Dampness and moves Qi to relieve fullness?
  • Which herb transforms Phlegm-Dampness and directs Lung Qi downward?

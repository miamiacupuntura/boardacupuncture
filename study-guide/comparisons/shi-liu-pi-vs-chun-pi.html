Herb comparison

Granati Pericarpium (shi liu pi) vs. Ailanthi Cortex (chun pi)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueShi Liu PiChun Pi
Shared cluesBoth bind the Intestines for diarrhea/dysentery, stop bleeding, kill parasites, and belong to Herbs that Stabilize and Bind.
Fast distinctionWarm, sour and more strongly binding for deficiency Cold.Cold, bitter and less strongly binding for Heat or Damp-Heat.

Comparative answer key

CompareShi Liu Pi (Granati Pericarpium)Chun Pi (Ailanthi Cortex)
Shared categoryHerbs that Stabilize and Bind.
PropertiesSour, astringent and warm.Bitter, astringent and cold.
Binding strengthStrongly restraining, inhibiting and binding.Weaker binding/stabilizing action, with added Heat-clearing and Dampness-drying.
PatternDiarrhea, dysentery, persistent uterine bleeding or vaginal discharge from deficiency Cold.Dysentery, vaginal discharge or persistent uterine bleeding from Heat or Damp-Heat.
Shared extensionsStop bleeding and kill parasites.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersThe indication list overlaps closely; temperature, flavor, strength and Cold-versus-Heat pattern determine the answer.
Common confusionDeficiency Cold plus strong binding favors Shi Liu Pi. Heat/Damp-Heat plus cooling and drying favors Chun Pi.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 872. Source Exam Key CMID 8922.

Possible exam questions · Study prompts

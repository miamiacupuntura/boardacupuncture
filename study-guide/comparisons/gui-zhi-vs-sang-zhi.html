Herb comparison

Cinnamomi Ramulus (gui zhi) vs. Mori Ramulus (sang zhi)

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueGui ZhiSang Zhi
Shared clueBoth unblock the collaterals and work well together for upper-limb Wind-Damp painful obstruction.
Fast distinctionWarms Yang throughout the body; favors Cold painful obstruction.Expels Wind-Dampness; favors accompanying Heat or prominent itching.

Comparative answer key

CompareGui ZhiSang Zhi
Botanical nameCinnamomi Ramulus.Mori Ramulus.
CategoryWarm, Acrid Herbs that Release the Exterior.Herbs that Expel Wind-Dampness.
Collateral-unblocking mechanismWarms Yang and unblocks its flow throughout the body, giving it a wider scope.Expels Wind and eliminates Dampness; does not unblock Yang throughout the body.
Temperature clueAcrid and warm; more appropriate for Cold painful obstruction.Relatively cooling; superior when Wind-Dampness is accompanied by Heat or itching.
PropertiesWarm, acrid and sweet.Bitter and neutral.
ChannelsLung, Urinary Bladder and Heart.Liver.
Distinctive indicationsWind-Cold Exterior Deficiency; limb or shoulder Bi pain; chest pain from Heart-Yang deficiency; abdominal pain or masses from Cold and Blood stasis.Wind-Damp Bi syndrome, especially in the upper extremities.
CautionsContraindicated in warm febrile disease and Yin deficiency with Heat; use caution during pregnancy.Use caution in Yin deficiency.
Source dose3–15 g.10–30 g.
Formula cluesMa Huang Tang; Gui Zhi Tang; Xiao Qing Long Tang; Ge Gen Tang; Wu Ling San; Dang Gui Si Ni Tang.Juan Bi Tang.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersUpper-limb Bi pain can call for either herb, but a Cold-Yang pattern points to Gui Zhi, while Wind-Dampness with Heat or itching points to Sang Zhi.
Memory cueGui Zhi warms Yang; Sang Zhi sweeps Wind-Damp.
SourceChinese Herbal Medicine: Materia Medica, Third Edition, Dan Bensky, Steven Clavey, Erich Stöger and Andrew Gamble (2004), p. 356. Source Exam Keys CMID 8963 and CMID 9049, questions 1–6.

Stomach Fire vs. Stomach Yin Deficiency

Study tip · Clues to help answer

ClueStomach FireStomach Yin Deficiency
Shared clueBoth may cause thirst, dry mouth, constipation, a red tongue and epigastric discomfort.
Fast distinctionExcess Fire: burning pain, voracious hunger, foul breath, swollen bleeding gums, strong thirst and a forceful rapid pulse.Deficient Yin: dull or empty discomfort, hunger without desire to eat, dry mouth with small sips, dry retching and a peeled central tongue.

Comparative answer key

CompareStomach FireStomach Yin Deficiency
AppetiteExcessive hunger and rapid return of appetite.Poor appetite or hunger without desire to eat.
Mouth / gumsFoul breath, mouth ulcers, swollen or bleeding gums.Dry mouth/throat without strong foul Heat signs.
Tongue / pulseRed with thick/dry yellow coat; slippery-forceful-rapid.Red, dry, peeled in center; thin-rapid.
Formula examplesQing Wei San; Yu Nu Jian when Kidney Yin deficiency coexists.Yi Wei Tang or Sha Shen Mai Dong Tang by context.

Study guide · Exam focus

Why it mattersBoth are dry and red. Appetite intensity, gum Heat, tongue coating and pulse strength separate excess Fire from depleted Yin.
Memory cueStomach Fire devours; Stomach Yin wants but cannot receive.
SafetyVomiting blood, black stool, severe persistent pain, progressive swallowing difficulty, dehydration or unexplained weight loss needs prompt medical evaluation.

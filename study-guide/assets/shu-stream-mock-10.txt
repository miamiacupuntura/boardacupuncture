8. Which statement correctly describes Shu-Stream and Yuan-Source classifications?
A) They coincide on all twelve channels.
B) They coincide on Yin channels; Yang channels have separate Yuan-Source points. +++++
C) They coincide only on Yang channels.
D) No Shu-Stream point is a Yuan-Source point.
Feedback: The six Yin Shu-Stream points are also Yuan-Source; Yang channels have separate Yuan-Source points. Why are the other options incorrect? They coincide on all twelve channels. — Yang Shu-Stream and Yuan-Source are separate, so coincidence does not apply to all twelve. They coincide only on Yang channels. — This reverses the Yin and Yang relationship. No Shu-Stream point is a Yuan-Source point. — LU9, SP3, HT7, KID3, PC7 and LIV3 carry both classifications.

9. Which Shu-Stream point is located between the medial malleolus and Achilles tendon rather than in the hand or forefoot region?
A) SP3 Taibai
B) LIV3 Taichong
C) KID3 Taixi +++++
D) ST43 Xiangu
Feedback: KID3 Taixi is Kidney Shu-Stream and Yuan-Source, located posterior to the medial malleolus and anterior to the Achilles tendon. Why are the other options incorrect? SP3 Taibai — SP3 is on the medial foot near the first metatarsophalangeal region. LIV3 Taichong — LIV3 is on the dorsum of the foot between the first and second metatarsals. ST43 Xiangu — ST43 is on the dorsum between the second and third metatarsals.

10. Which description matches the classical transport metaphor for Shu-Stream?
A) The point at which Qi rises
B) The point at which Qi glides
C) The point at which Qi enters inward
D) The point at which Qi pours through +++++
Feedback: Shu-Stream is the point at which Qi pours through; the transport metaphor describes a category rather than a literal anatomical fluid stream. Why are the other options incorrect? The point at which Qi rises — Qi rises or emerges refers to Jing-Well. The point at which Qi glides — Qi glides refers to Ying-Spring. The point at which Qi enters inward — Qi enters inward refers to He-Sea.

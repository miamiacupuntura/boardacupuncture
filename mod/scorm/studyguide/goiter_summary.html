01 • GOITER — Biomedicine

GOITER — Biomedicine

Thyroid Anatomy

The thyroid gland is located in the anterior neck adjacent to the trachea. It consists primarily of the right lobe, left lobe, and isthmus.

Normal Thyroid Physiology

Hypothalamus → TRH → Pituitary → TSH → Thyroid → T4 / T3

Thyroid hormones participate in regulation of metabolic rate, heat production, energy utilization, cardiovascular activity, and neurologic function. The system operates through negative feedback.

What Is a Goiter?

Goiter = Enlargement of the Thyroid Gland

A goiter does not by itself determine thyroid function.

Euthyroid
Normal thyroid function
Hyperthyroid
Excess thyroid hormone effect
Hypothyroid
Insufficient thyroid hormone effect

Hyperthyroidism

Heat intolerance • Excessive sweating • Tremor • Irritability • Increased awareness of heartbeat • Weight loss

Palpitations vs Tachycardia

Palpitation: subjective awareness of the heartbeat.

Tachycardia: objectively rapid heart rate. In an adult at rest, tachycardia is generally defined as a heart rate above 100 beats/minute.

High-Yield: Palpitation does NOT automatically mean tachycardia.

Graves Disease

Diffuse Goiter + Hyperthyroidism + Exophthalmos → Graves Disease

Hypothyroidism

Possible clues include fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, dry skin, constipation, and slowed activity.

Laboratory Reasoning

TSH: pituitary hormone that stimulates thyroid hormone production.

Free T4: circulating unbound thyroxine available to tissues.

T3: metabolically active thyroid hormone; produced directly and through peripheral conversion from T4.

Primary Hyperthyroidism
Typically: TSH ↓ + Free T4 ↑
Primary Hypothyroidism
Typically: TSH ↑ + Free T4 ↓

BIO Exam Reasoning

Anatomy → Physiology → Pathology → Signs/Symptoms → Laboratory Findings → Clinical Condition

High-Yield:
Goiter tells you the thyroid is enlarged. It does not, by itself, tell you whether thyroid function is high, low, or normal.

03 • GOITER — Foundations of Oriental Medicine

GOITER — Foundations of Oriental Medicine

Core Concept

Goiter → Differentiation → Manifestation → Treatment Strategy

The source presents Goiter through differentiation and specific clinical manifestations that guide the selection of supplementary points.

Differentiation

Qi Goiter
Diffusive, soft swelling of the neck, gradually increasing in size, with unclear margins and normal color. The size may change with emotions.
Flesh Goiter
Movable, smooth, painless lumps below the Adam’s apple, with manifestations including exophthalmos, hot temper, irritability, tremor, sweating, chest stuffiness and palpitation.
Liver-Qi Stagnation
Supplementary treatment with CV17 + LV3.
Source relationship: both points are used to regulate Liver Qi.

Pathophysiology Concepts Tested

Qi Stagnation + Phlegm Accumulation
SJ13 is specifically identified as a point used to remove obstruction from the meridians and relieve Qi stagnation and Phlegm accumulation.
Blood Stasis / Agglomeration
LI17 + SI17 + CV22 are described as regulating local Qi and Blood circulation to remove Blood Stasis and disperse agglomeration.
Yangming Qi Circulation
LI4 + ST36 are described as promoting Qi circulation in the Yangming Meridians and eliminating stagnation of Qi and Blood.

Manifestation-Based Differentiation

Palpitation → PC6 + HT7
Exophthalmos → SJ23 + BL2 + BL1 + GB20
Hot temper / anxiety / excessive perspiration → SP6 + KD7
